There is a calendar coincidence worth noting: Amata entered early access on 20 August 2024, and it reaches version 1.0 on 20 August 2026. Two years to the day.

For a game carried by one person, holding that course without vanishing is already information.

![Amata, 2D exploration and the worlds of the Memetic Expanse](/images/amata/screenshot-1.webp)

## What it is

A 2D side-scrolling action RPG platformer, described by its author as mostly linear, which is an honest clarification: this is not an open-map metroidvania.

You play Charlet, who must escape the Memetic Expanse, the place where Amata was formed, found and ended. The vocabulary is deliberately obscure and the game does not try to explain everything.

The structure follows the genre's classic progression, carried by four pillars the author frames as precepts.

## The systems

**Movement.** This is the main argument, and the author's phrasing is clear: an agent should have grace. The game bets on smooth, fluid and dynamic movement across varied worlds, and that is where the feel in hand is decided.

**Combat.** You cut down enemies with the **memetic blade**, unbinding them from their experience. That released experience becomes the progression resource: you then apply it to a suite of stats. The link between killing and growing is direct and thematised, rather than an abstract counter.

**Defence.** Blocking and parrying enemy attacks, with the precision that implies. It is what stops the game reducing to charging forward.

**Equipment.** You wear, wield and consume magical artifacts, items and charms, which opens character building beyond raw stats.

There is also a comfort worth flagging: **instant teleportation between all known realms and checkpoints**. For a linear game, that is a generous choice avoiding punishing backtracking, and the author explicitly frames it as freedom offered to the player.

![Amata, combat and the memetic blade](/images/amata/screenshot-2.webp)

## What to know

The game sells for around ten euros, which places it in a category where a lot is forgiven.

Two reservations.

**The review sample is tiny.** The game has fewer than ten Steam ratings after two years of early access, which says a great deal about its visibility. That is not a quality judgement, it is a distribution fact: this game has not found its audience, and 1.0 is its last card.

**The writing is hermetic.** The invented vocabulary, Amata, Memetic Expanse, memetic blade, agents, is laid down without introduction. Some will read elegance, others a barrier. The game does not hold the player's hand, narratively included.

## What sets the score

It rewards long-haul work by one person over two years, a clear intention around movement and fluidity, and a thematised progression loop where experience released from enemies literally becomes your power. Free teleportation is a well-considered comfort.

It accounts for an owned linearity that limits exploration, a deliberately opaque world that does not court you, and visibility so low the game remains a curiosity rather than an obvious pick.

At that price, for anyone after a snappy and slightly strange 2D action RPG, it is worth a look.
