No Body No Problem!'s best idea arrived on launch day: the carpet. You lay the body on it, roll it up, and carry it in the open. Except that someone walking out of an apartment with a rolled carpet on their shoulder, just as a cop arrives, does not exactly look innocent. The developers say it themselves: a missing body raises suspicion, but a strangely placed carpet is not innocent-looking either.

![A body lying on a carpet in a living room, with the police arrival countdown](/images/nobodynoproblem/screenshot-7.webp)

The premise, detailed in [our preview](/articles/no-body-no-problem-preview), is simple. Four players: three suspects who must hide the body and wipe the evidence, and a cop who arrives after the report and searches everything. The police can knock at any moment, and an on-screen counter reminds you how much time is left.

It all rests on physics, which is what makes it funny. The body gets stuck in doorframes, furniture falls over, a bin that is too small leaves two legs sticking out. The cop is not just looking for the corpse: an open door, a broken object, a floor that is too clean, a player running for no reason. The best matches play out in the lies, when one suspect starts blaming the others.

![A body in a bin, legs sticking out](/images/nobodynoproblem/screenshot-6.webp)

Maps are varied (apartment, shop, car park, suburb, Wild West town, an island with jet skis), and the latest update added vehicles of all kinds and five mini-games to unwind between crimes: basketball, volleyball, foosball, pool and fishing.

The downside is well known: without three friends, the game loses most of its appeal, and the balance between cop and suspects still needs work. The studio itself says it noted, during the playtest, when physics chaos stops being funny and becomes frustrating. You feel it sometimes, when a body stubbornly refuses to fit through a door.

![A helicopter cockpit above an island](/images/nobodynoproblem/screenshot-5.webp)

But with four people who enjoy lying, it is an excellent surprise.
