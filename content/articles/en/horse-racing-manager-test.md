One Horse Racing Manager player sums up the game in their Steam review better than any pitch: "Horses, gambling, and economics." Another explains the developers seem to have made it specifically for them, a horse fanatic who works in accounting. That is exactly it: a racing management sim that owns being a spreadsheet, and a spreadsheet you sink hundreds of hours into.

![A flat race in the snow, in pixel art, by a lake](/images/horseracingmanager/screenshot-2.webp)

## Start from nothing, or not

The first decision is the most interesting. You can start as an owner on day one. Or start at the very bottom, as an unemployed manager or jockey: get hired by an existing stable, ride other people's horses, follow other people's training plans, collect your pay, and slowly build up money, reputation and contacts before opening your own stable. Some players found a more cynical shortcut: marry someone and take over their farm. The game allows it.

Then come auctions (public and private, against rival stables), and above all breeding. The Pseudo-DNA system gives every horse a genetic profile (speed, stamina, acceleration, temperament, durability and dozens of other traits) passed on by both parents through dominant and recessive genes. Studying pedigrees to find the rare pairing quickly becomes an obsession.

![A horse's breeding sheet, with portrait and stats](/images/horseracingmanager/screenshot-1.webp)

## And now, jumps

The game has been in Early Access since October 2025, and its update log is impressive: several patches a week. The latest big addition, this week, brings steeplechase: gallop races over fences, playable on their own or alongside flat and harness racing. Courses depend on each country's jump tradition (England, Ireland, Scotland, Wales and France lead the way), with their own distance bands and several fence types. Horses now have a Jump value next to Trot and Gallop. A voiced awards gala has also been added.

During races, you can watch the simulation or switch to Jockey Mode and ride yourself. The pixel art races, with scenery that changes with the seasons, have real charm.

![A race start on a dirt track, by an autumn lake](/images/horseracingmanager/screenshot-4.webp)

The learning curve is steep: many players talk about information overload in the first hours, although the explanations that pop up in each new tab help a lot. And the studio states on Steam that some inbox content was generated then edited, adding that it has hired writers to rewrite it.

At 24.50 euros, with 89% positive out of more than 260 reviews and a studio this responsive, it is the benchmark of the genre.
