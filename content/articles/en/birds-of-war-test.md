Some concepts you can't take seriously, and that's precisely why you want to try them. Birds of War is one: an aerial PvP game where nine birds steal each other's eggs beak-first in a chaos of feathers. We snickered launching it, spent a good half hour barreling into nests while screaming, then asked the real question: is there a game behind the joke, or just a good afternoon idea?

![Birds of War, the 3v3v3 aerial combat where you steal eggs beak-first](/images/birdsofwar/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Birds of War is a team-based aerial PvP game developed by HexNest Games, available free-to-play since 21 July 2026 on PC. The format is as clear as it is zany: three teams of three birds clash in 3v3v3, with one goal, bring back as many eggs as possible to your nest before the match ends. You raid enemy nests, you fight beak to beak in fast, chaotic matches, and proximity chat lets you hear the enemy before you see them. Free, snappy and unpretentious, it's the kind of small multiplayer game made for laughs with friends.

## The gameplay: feathered chaos

Birds of War's strength is its immediacy. You understand everything in ten seconds, you dive into the melee, and the mix of free flight and brawling produces delightful situations where you swoop down to snatch an egg out from under a rival's beak before bolting. The three-team format adds a clever layer of tension: no one is really an ally, everyone watches everyone, and a team in the lead becomes the target of the other two. The proximity chat is a find that multiplies the fun, turning every ambush into a little sonic theater where you hear an opponent snicker before they charge you.

The roster bets on role variety, if not numbers. The game launches with four birds of marked styles: Crow the stealth assassin, Hummingbird the rocket, Shoebill the damage-soaking tank, and Pigeon the support who marks enemies recon-drone style. That small cast covers the classic archetypes of the team game, and each has enough personality for you to find your main. It's well observed, legible, and perfectly suited to a game that wants to be accessible in a single match. You feel a real understanding of what makes a team PvP immediately enjoyable.

![Birds of War, the bird classes with distinct roles, from assassin to tank](/images/birdsofwar/screenshot-2.webp)

## What sticks: a still-empty nest

Let's be critical, because the pleasure of the first matches shouldn't mask the thinness of the content. Four birds at launch is few, even with the promise of more, and you quickly do the rounds of the possible combinations. A competitive multiplayer game lives on its depth and its renewal, and Birds of War starts with a roster and a number of modes that betray its small-production status. The concept is excellent, but the arsenal that must sustain it over time isn't there yet, and repetition looms faster than you'd want.

The other unknown, and it's decisive, is the community. A free 3v3v3 game is only worth the number of players available to fill its matches, and history is cruel to small multiplayer games that struggle to retain their audience past the initial curiosity. Birds of War will have to prove it can build loyalty, add content at a good pace and keep matchmaking alive, on pain of becoming a deserted nest. The free-to-play model helps attract, but you'll have to watch how the game plans to fund itself without falling into the usual traps.

![Birds of War, raiding enemy nests at the heart of fast, chaotic matches](/images/birdsofwar/screenshot-3.webp)

## What we take away

Birds of War is exactly what it promises to be: a small free game, funny and immediate, perfect for a few matches of feathered chaos with friends. Its 3v3v3 egg-stealing concept works, its bird roles are well thought out, and its proximity chat adds a welcome dose of hilarity. For zero euros, it's a good surprise to try without hesitation, the kind of game you launch for a laugh one evening and that fulfills its contract.

But you mustn't ask it for more than it can give today. With only four birds and still-skeletal content, its ability to retain players beyond the discovery remains to be proven, and its future will depend entirely on the vitality of its community and the care given to its updates. It's a good idea in search of the depth that would make it last. One to watch, to try, but not yet to crown.

## Verdict

A free, chaotic aerial PvP where you steal eggs beak-first: genuinely fun in the moment, but with content too thin to promise it will last.

**Strengths:**

- An immediate, hilarious concept, 3v3v3 egg-stealing
- Varied, legible bird roles, from assassin to support
- A proximity chat that multiplies the fun of ambushes
- Free, so worth trying with no risk at all

**Weaknesses:**

- A thin starting roster, only four birds
- Skeletal content where repetition looms fast
- A future hanging on the vitality of its community
