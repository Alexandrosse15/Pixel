Two numbers tell Wizdom Academy's story better than any summary. Over its whole life on Steam, the game sits at 71% positive out of 318 reviews. Over the last thirty days, 93% out of 78.

The first number is the April 2025 Early Access, a difficult start. The second is the game it has become. Kipwak Studio and Lake On Fire spent eighteen months correcting course, and the 1.0 released today is the result of that catch-up.

![The academy from above, its blue roofs and autumn forest](/images/wizdomacademy/screenshot-5.webp)

You are the Headmaster of a wizarding school, and you build it floor by floor, room by room. It is a management game in the vein of Two Point Campus, but with real verticality: you stack levels, draw corridors, and end up with a castle full of towers and secret passages. Students return year after year and accumulate traits (more than a hundred) that decide their Destiny (more than fifty). The best graduates stay on as teachers.

That continuity is what hooked me. You are not managing an anonymous crowd, you are following class years. Seeing a first-year come back as a teacher, carrying the traits you let them pick up, is deeply satisfying.

![A student's profile and skill tree](/images/wizdomacademy/screenshot-2.webp)

## The double pressure

The game rests on a balance: students want to enjoy school life (and play pranks), while the Directorium wants results and sends an envoy to collect taxes. The 1.0 strengthened the latter: Directorium Trust weighs more, and unpaid teachers or incidents now reduce it. The game is tenser than before, and better for it.

The rest of 1.0 is mostly finishing work: the Headmaster's Tower to raise and enchant, a fully redesigned interface, over 200 new sounds, a narrated intro and a proper ending. Performance in large academies was improved, which was a recurring complaint.

![The learning tree, and the Headmaster's Tower](/images/wizdomacademy/screenshot-4.webp)

It is not perfect. There are still plenty of menus, and fine-tuning schedules takes a fair amount of back and forth. The studio also states on Steam that it uses generative tools in its creative process, specifying that no art in the game is entirely generated.

But at 19.79 euros, if you enjoyed Spellcaster University or Two Point Campus, it is one of the most generous management games around.
