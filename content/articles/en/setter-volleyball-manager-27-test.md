Football has Football Manager, hockey has Franchise Hockey Manager, cycling has Pro Cycling Manager. Volleyball had nothing. Setter: Volleyball Manager 27 fills that gap, and that alone makes it worth a look.

![A live match, the score displayed and players at the net](/images/settervm27/screenshot-2.webp)

The principle is the genre's. You take charge of a men's volleyball club. You recruit, negotiate transfers and contracts, and balance talent and experience within your budget and wage bill. You plan training, develop young players, replace key players when they leave. And you chase domestic titles and European cups.

Everything is decided during matches, followed point by point. You read the momentum, choose where to apply pressure, how much risk to take, when to change approach. That is where Setter finds its personality: volleyball is a sport of sequences and rotations, and the game translates that well into tactical decisions. Those who want to move faster can switch to instant results.

![The team lineup on court, with each player's role](/images/settervm27/screenshot-1.webp)

The included world is fictional, but you can create your own setting or import your own competition data, opening the door to realistic community-made databases. The studio states that portraits of fictional and youth players were created with AI image tools during development.

The limits are those of a first instalment. The interface is austere, very spreadsheet-like, and match presentation stays basic. Only men's volleyball is available for now. And without real licences, the content requires imagination or a trip through community data.

For a volleyball fan who likes management games, it is the only game of its kind, and a serious one. Everyone else may want to wait until it fills out.
