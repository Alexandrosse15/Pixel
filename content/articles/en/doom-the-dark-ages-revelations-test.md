Let us be honest about the mindset we launched this expansion with. After an excellent but divisive base game, after months of reading that The Dark Ages was the weak link in a near-perfect modern trilogy, we expected the expansion too far, the one that recycles, that stretches, that confirms a beloved franchise is quietly starting to spin its wheels. We waited for that misstep for twelve hours. It never came. Revelations is not a last hurrah, it is a demonstration.

![DOOM The Dark Ages Revelations, the Slayer thrust into a merciless purgatory](/images/doomtdarevelations/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

DOOM: The Dark Ages - Revelations is the campaign expansion developed by id Software and published by Bethesda, released on 7 July 2026 on PC, PS5 and Xbox Series. This is not a cosmetic DLC or a handful of arenas: it is a genuine ten-to-twelve-hour solo campaign, on its own larger than DOOM Eternal's two expansions combined. The announced split, roughly sixty percent main campaign and forty percent endgame content, says the ambition plainly. And the critical reception followed, with a 92 on OpenCritic, a "Mighty" status and one hundred percent of critics recommending it. Suffice to say we were swimming against the current with our reservations.

## The gameplay

The big new addition, the one that redefines the rhythm, is the Chain Spear. This chained spear acts as a grappling hook: it launches you toward an enemy, pulls a target to you, allows sudden repositioning in the middle of chaos. On paper, a simple mobility gadget. Controller in hand, it is the base game's missing link. Where The Dark Ages embraced a bulldozer Slayer, heavy and frontal, the Chain Spear reinjects the verticality and dodge speed that those coming from Eternal missed. You dash toward danger again, you chain targets on the fly, and the infamous critique of the too-plodding rhythm evaporates at once. It is no accident the press speaks of an expansion that "recaptures the speed and challenge of Eternal".

The real feat is that the Chain Spear does not replace The Dark Ages' identity, it completes it. The shield-parry-melee triptych that made the game singular stays intact, but you add a dimension of offensive movement that turns fights into far more fluid ballets. Deflecting a projectile with the shield, launching yourself with the Chain Spear toward the shooter, finishing them in melee, all in one continuous motion: that is exactly the movement creativity we were asking for, and it gives the fights a depth the base game only reached in flashes.

![DOOM The Dark Ages Revelations, the Chain Spear launching the Slayer into the heart of combat](/images/doomtdarevelations/screenshot-2.webp)

The endgame content is not cynical filler, and this is where we definitively laid down our arms. A large hub area riddled with secrets rewards exploration, revisited levels offer new puzzles and combat challenges, an Uber Boss awaits the boldest, and Master Arenas are built for high-level play. This is not padding: it is a real playground for anyone wanting to push the system to its limits well after the credits. For the diehards, Revelations offers dozens of hours of additional mastery, and every challenge feels designed to showcase the richness of the moveset.

One snag remains, a single one, but a real one: the Chain Spear pushes controllers and keyboards to their limits. Finding a mapping that makes its use fully natural takes time and trial, and some players will curse before taming the beast. It is the price of an ambitious mechanic grafted onto a game not originally built for it, and it can create some friction in the first fights. Once the movement is internalised, the pleasure takes over, but the running-in period exists and it would be dishonest to gloss over it.

## The story

Revelations does not just stack arenas, it tells something, and rather well. Wounded and betrayed, the Slayer is thrust into a merciless purgatory escapable only by confronting painful truths and forging new strength, aided by a mysterious ally. The narrative stays in service of the gameplay, as it should in a DOOM, but it gives a welcome frame and tension to this descent, and the purgatory setting gives id Software the chance to deploy some of the most striking environments in all of The Dark Ages. You push on to kill, yes, but also to understand, and that balance is better held here than in the original campaign.

![DOOM The Dark Ages Revelations, the purgatory environments among the most striking in the saga](/images/doomtdarevelations/screenshot-3.webp)

## The tech

Technically, Revelations is pure id Software, which is to say a showcase. The engine holds a hellish onslaught without flinching, the purgatory environments have a visual richness that lifts the expansion above the base game artistically, and the readability of the chaos, crucial when an arena fills with demons and projectiles to parry, stays impeccable. Performance is solid, and the whole exudes the technical mastery that has been the studio's signature since 2016.

The only technical reservations join the question of the Chain Spear and its integration with peripherals, rather than a lack of polish. The rest is exemplary in its cleanliness, without the hiccups sometimes forgiven of hastily shipped expansions. You sense a studio that did not treat Revelations as a spin-off product, but as a chance to prove something. And the bet pays off.

## The verdict

We came looking for the flaw, the proof that the magic was fading, the opportunistic expansion that heralds the decline of a beloved franchise. We leave having changed our minds. Revelations fixes the rare complaints made about the base game by reinjecting speed and verticality through the Chain Spear, offers a long, dense, well-told campaign, and crowns it all with endgame content that genuinely respects demanding players. This is not padding, it is an achievement.

The only caveat, taming the Chain Spear on our peripherals, is the kind of friction you forget once the mechanic is mastered, and it weighs nothing against what the expansion achieves. DOOM's modern trilogy is not running out of steam: it has just proved, with what is one of 2026's best campaigns, that it still has plenty to say. We waited for the misstep too far. We got a lesson in humility.

**The heralded misstep that never comes: Revelations is dazzling proof that a franchise can age without repeating itself.**

Score: 9/10
