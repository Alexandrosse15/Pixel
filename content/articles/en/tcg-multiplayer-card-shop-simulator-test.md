When I reviewed [TCG Card Shop Simulator](/articles/tcg-card-shop-simulator-test) in September, there was only one serious reservation in a review that scored it 9: no multiplayer, and no plan to add it. TCG Multiplayer Card Shop Simulator, from Alkame Games, is a different game, from a different studio, that rushed into that gap. Its name is its pitch.

And at first, I could not quite see what it would change. A shop simulator is a game of routine: you fill shelves, you ring up sales, you open boosters on the sly. A solitary activity by nature, almost meditative. Why invite three people in?

![The shop, its booster shelves and its game-room armchairs](/images/tcgmultiplayer/screenshot-2.webp)

## What multiplayer changes, once the shop is open

The answer comes quickly, and it fits in three words: division of labour. In OPNeon's game, the limit is you. You cannot restock the back aisle while a queue builds at the till. With two or three people, the shop becomes a small team: one on the register, one restocking, one handling online shop orders and refilling the vending machines placed around town. The pace is nothing like solo.

The second gain is the cards themselves. They go into your Steam inventory, which means you can trade duplicates with friends, and opening a booster becomes a shared moment. The studio publishes its drop rates, which is honest: 0.9% legendary per slot, 0.1% ultra rare. Roughly one ultra rare every 125 boosters. When it drops, you want someone next to you to scream with.

The Steam Market is open, but do not dream: common cards sell there for 3 cents. The few listings at tens of dollars are asking prices, for a single copy, not sales.

![A booster vending machine placed on the street](/images/tcgmultiplayer/screenshot-4.webp)

So yes, once you play, the point of multiplayer is obvious. It turns a patience game into a game with friends, and that is exactly what the big brother does not offer.

## The problem is everything else

The thing is, the game has to hold up. And it does not hold up well. Stutters even on a recent rig, lag between input and movement, a loading screen to open a booster (in a game where that is the main action), friends floating above the floor when they join, quests losing their progress. Recent reviews all tell the same story, and 53% positive out of 206 is a game that genuinely divides.

Multiplayer with strangers barely exists: 26 people were online at the time of writing. Come with friends or do not come.

![The shop front, seen from the street](/images/tcgmultiplayer/screenshot-3.webp)

What worries me most is the Steam page. The studio's last four announcements promote its other games (a figure-sorting game, a manga library game, a taskbar pet), not an update to this one, still in Early Access since June 2025. The page also discloses generated images for the capsule art and some of the in-game artwork.

At 5.37 euros, with three motivated friends and a tolerance for bugs, the idea is still good enough for an evening. But you constantly feel the game it could have been, and you end up relaunching OPNeon's game solo.
