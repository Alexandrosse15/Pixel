ShredDead's description starts like a thousand other zombie games, then it swerves, and that is the moment I decided to write about it.

The world is over, the zombie apocalypse has arrived, you are ready to make your last stand and go down in a blaze of glory.

**The problem is, you are too good at this asymmetric warfare thing, and this last stand is taking forever.**

At this point, writes the studio, this is not about survival. This is about fun.

![ShredDead, the defence](/images/shreddead/screenshot-1.webp)

## Why that joke is good design

Because it solves the genre's credibility problem.

Every horde survival game has the same contradiction: it tells you about the end of the world, and two hours later you are a god erasing three hundred corpses a minute. The store page's tone no longer matches what is on screen.

ShredDead takes the problem from the other end: it admits in its opening line that you are going to become ridiculously strong, and turns that into the subject.

That is writing, and it costs nothing. A lot of studios should think about it.

## What you do

You shoot them, you burn them, you blow them up. And when all else fails, you shred the dead, with your very own heavy-duty industrial shredder.

The studio calls its loop the **zombie blender**: you get into a flow state methodically decimating the unending deluge, or you have your grunts do it for you, or both.

There is a skill tree to explore to optimise the killing, and gear to kit your grunts out with, which the zombies are nice enough to deliver right to your doorstep.

![ShredDead, the shredder](/images/shreddead/screenshot-2.webp)

## The reception, and it is more mixed

**105 reviews, 77 per cent positive.** Mostly positive, but with a quarter of players not following along.

That is a notable gap compared with the day's other releases, and it deserves stating honestly. A 4.79 euro game whose main argument is humour has a narrow margin: when the joke runs out, only the loop remains, and the loop is everybody's.

What divides people in this genre is usually one of three things: readability when the screen fills up, a progression curve that flattens, or enemy variety. It will take more distance to know which.

## What it is worth

Under five euros, the question is not whether this is the year's best game.

It is whether you will want to start a run on an evening with nothing planned, to watch a mass of corpses disappear into a machine while listening to a podcast.

For that, the format is right, the tone is right, and the pitch is honest: nobody is selling you a life-changing experience.

![ShredDead, the skill tree](/images/shreddead/screenshot-3.webp)

One last word on the art direction, because it is part of the joke. The deliberately coarse pixel art, the blood spraying in big squares, the top-down view: all of it tells the player not to take things seriously, and it is consistent with a game that opens by explaining your last stand is dragging on.
