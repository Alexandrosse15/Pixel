Roadhouse Simulator hands you a rundown bar and a long list of profitable bad ideas. The problem is that bad ideas get expensive when taken in the wrong order.

Here is the order I would recommend. Our [full review is here](/en/articles/roadhouse-simulator-test).

![Roadhouse Simulator, the room mid-service](/images/roadhouse/screenshot-3.webp)

## Understanding Hype

It is the game's engine and the source of most disasters.

The higher your Hype climbs, the faster you progress. But more people means more money **and more chaos**.

The practical consequence is simple and counter-intuitive: **do not raise Hype faster than your ability to hold the room**.

A packed night with one bouncer and nobody cleaning does not earn more than a half-full, well-run night. It earns less, because a brawl costs furniture, customers and time.

My rule: before every new attraction, ask who will handle the extra customers it brings. If the answer is you, it is too early.

## The beers, which are not interchangeable

This is the detail the game explains badly and which causes the most accidents.

Different brews **affect customers differently**. Serving the wrong person at the wrong moment can make a quiet night go bad.

Two habits.

**Watch before you pour.** An already agitated customer and a strong drink is a scheduled brawl. A calm customer deserves whatever is most profitable.

**And keep a balanced range in stock.** A cellar with only one category forces you to serve anything to anybody.

## The brawl, and how to handle it

It will happen. Rival gang members and drunks turn a quiet night into a brawl in seconds.

Three principles.

**Step in early.** An altercation between two people can be settled, a mass brawl can only be endured.

**Keep spare furniture.** The studio advises it itself, and it is not a joke: replacing a broken table mid-evening beats serving in a field of rubble.

**And hire a bouncer before you need one.** See below.

![Roadhouse Simulator, a brawl](/images/roadhouse/screenshot-2.webp)

## When to hire, and whom

You cannot do everything forever. The question is the order.

**The bouncer first.** It is the post that protects every other one. Without him, a single brawl wipes out an evening's takings.

**The cleaner next.** Cleanliness is not decorative in this game, see the following section.

**The bartender and server after.** They raise your throughput, and therefore your revenue, but they are useless if the room is unmanageable.

And do not forget staff cost money. An overstaffed bar on a quiet day loses money as surely as a bar in a brawl.

## The questionable half, and its accounting

Distilling, slot machines, pig races, Fight Club: the game offers you varied and disreputable ways to make money.

The system to understand fits in one line from the studio: **crime leaves evidence, and every mess becomes your problem**.

So cleaning is not drudgery, it is cover. Three tips.

**Clean after each operation, not at the end of the week.** Evidence accumulates, and a visit triggers at the wrong moment by definition.

**Do not open two risky activities at once.** One source of evidence can be handled, two accumulate.

**And keep the place presentable permanently.** When the police arrive, the game asks you to act natural. That is far easier when there is genuinely nothing.

## The progression order I would recommend

**Phase one: hold the room.** Beer, cleaning, door. No side activities. Objective, one evening without incident.

**Phase two: bouncer and cleaner.** You are buying stability, not revenue. That is normal.

**Phase three: one attraction.** One. Slots or pig races, and you watch what it does to Hype and to disorder.

**Phase four: distilling.** You produce what you were buying, so you gain on margin. That is where the business takes off.

**Phase five: the rest.** And only then, the thing we do not talk about.

## The recurring mistakes

**Raising Hype too fast.** Mistake number one.

**Serving without looking at whom.** Number two.

**Postponing the cleaning.** Number three, and the only one visible from outside.

**And doing everything yourself.** You are not the bottleneck, you are the owner.
