A survivors-like has come out every week for four years. Most copy Vampire Survivors with the same damage circles and the same screen that ends as particle soup, and we forget them in an evening.

This one spent a year in early access and comes out with **895 positive reviews out of 979**. Nine in ten. That is the kind of figure that deserves a look at why.

![Funguys Swarm, a mushroom facing the flaming minions](/images/funguysswarm/screenshot-1.webp)

## What it is

You are a mushroom. One of the forest weirdos, to be precise, and you fight the Fire King's flaming minions.

The loop is the genre's: you survive waves, you grab upgrades mid-run, you stack powers until the screen becomes unreadable. Giant bosses turn up regularly, and each arena ends on something bigger still.

Where the game separates itself is in what you do **between** runs. You rescue creatures who join your **Forest Sanctuary**, which turns into a tree of permanent upgrades. You unlock heroes, pets, skills, and even mechas.

That is what separates a good survivors-like from a mediocre one: the long loop. Dying has to pay something, otherwise you do not press restart.

## What players say

I read the feedback, and its consistency is interesting.

The positives talk about execution. A player who already owns a stack of the genre ranks it among the good ones and notes it runs well on Steam Deck. Another, tersely: perfectly executed, high quality game. A third files it among the best in its category.

The criticisms are precise and worth knowing before buying.

**Crashes come up.** A player at twenty-seven hours recommends waiting for that reason, while finding the game solid otherwise. That is exactly the sort of complaint a 1.0 is meant to erase.

**Base weapon balance is criticised.** Several players find the starting weapons make a poor impression until upgraded, and that some power-ups are visibly better than others.

**And the difficulty has a spike.** The infamous yellow paprika recurs in the feedback as the moment you realise you have not upgraded enough.

The studio has plainly listened. A February update targeted exactly that, reducing difficulty spikes and grind by unlocking weapons, characters and skills far earlier to allow experimentation. Another in March went after CPU optimisation. Hotfixes addressed frame drops on AMD cards.

That is what a well-run early access looks like: player complaints reappear in the patch notes.

![Funguys Swarm, upgrades stacked mid-run](/images/funguysswarm/screenshot-2.webp)

## What changes today

Version 1.0 launches today, and the game arrives **on consoles for the first time**, alongside Steam and the Epic Games Store.

This is a genre that marries well with a controller and short sessions, so the console arrival makes sense. And it is a game under ten euros, which counts: the entry ticket costs what a sandwich costs.

The tone is the other argument. It is silly and owns it, with mutated vegetables, things that probably should not be on fire, and humour that never takes itself seriously. In a genre where everyone goes gothic and skeletal, a colourful forest is a relief.

![Funguys Swarm, a giant boss at the end of an arena](/images/funguysswarm/screenshot-3.webp)

## The score

Seven and a half.

It rewards a well-designed long loop, with a sanctuary that gives every defeat meaning, endearing art direction that does not look like its competitors, a year of exemplary follow-up, and a price that requires no thought.

It accounts for an imperfect stability record and early-weapon balance that players still flag.

It also accounts for the genre being what it is: brilliant across three evenings, then a matter of personal taste by the hundredth run.

For under ten euros, with nine positive reviews in ten and a studio that answers its players, there are not many reasons to hold back.
