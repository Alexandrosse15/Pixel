Some games take you by the hand and refuse to let go, those cozy games that turn a quick session into a whole evening without you noticing the clock. Suncraft belongs to that dangerous and adorable family. With its pixels that evoke old Game Boy Pokémon and its village gameplay inherited from Stardew Valley, it weaves a retro, warm cocoon where you patiently rebuild a village and relight a great lighthouse. The formula isn't new, but it's executed with a charm and gentleness that make it simply irresistible.

![Suncraft, a pixel-art cozy game with an old Pokémon vibe](/images/suncraft/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Suncraft is a pixel-art cozy adventure centered on growth and restoration, developed by Spicy Tuna Games, available on PC since 28 July 2026. You gather resources, craft items and upgrade your tools to expand your territory, with the goal of rebuilding the grand Lighthouse and restoring the village to its former glory. The game has been described as a blend between the old-school visuals of Pokémon and the village-oriented gameplay of Stardew Valley, a dual lineage that immediately speaks to any genre fan. The demo was a hit, with 97% positive reviews, and we were keen to check whether the full version kept its promises.

## The gameplay: the gentleness of rebuilding

The heart of Suncraft is that soothing loop of gathering, crafting and upgrading that made Stardew Valley a success. You glean resources, craft items, upgrade your tools, and gradually expand your territory in a logic of gentle, rewarding progression. The unifying goal, rebuilding the grand Lighthouse and restoring the village's splendor, gives meaning and a throughline to that patient accumulation: every stone laid, every tool upgraded brings you closer to the goal, and you get caught up in watching your little world reborn under your efforts. It's the timeless pleasure of the building and restoration game, executed with real warmth.

The dressing does a lot for the charm. The pixel-art aesthetic, evoking old handheld Pokémon, bathes the game in a gentle, immediately welcoming nostalgia, and its warm village atmosphere makes it an ideal refuge to decompress. Notably, the developer clearly learned the genre's lessons: to avoid the repetition and chore that lurk over so many cozy games, they planned to add new helpers and machines that reduce manual labor, a care for comfort that shows real attention to the player's enjoyment. The 97% demo doesn't lie: Suncraft found that rare alchemy that simply makes you feel good in it.

![Suncraft, gathering, crafting and rebuilding the grand Lighthouse](/images/suncraft/screenshot-2.webp)

## What sticks

You have to stay honest about what this formula implies. Suncraft doesn't reinvent the farm-and-village cozy game: it fully owns its dual lineage with Pokémon and Stardew Valley, and whoever seeks a radically new proposition in the genre may find the whole familiar, even deja-vu. It's a careful execution of a proven recipe rather than a revolution, and you have to love this type of experience, this loop of peaceful gathering and building, to dive in fully. Its strength is in the charm and gentleness, not in the originality, and you have to approach it with the right expectations.

The other reservation is lifespan and the risk of repetition, that fate that lurks over so many cozy games. The developer did take care to add helpers and machines to lighten the manual chore, a real plus, but everything will depend on the richness of the content and the generosity of the progression to last over time. The pleasure of rebuilding the Lighthouse is real, but a game of this type must renew its activities enough to keep the loop from running empty once the village is well along. It's a proposition to savor at your own pace, whose value will depend on its ability to sustain the wonder beyond the first hours. A lovely cocoon, provided it stays stocked.

## What we take away

Suncraft is an irresistible cozy game that executes a proven recipe with tons of charm. Its soothing loop of gathering, crafting and rebuilding, carried by the unifying goal of relighting the grand Lighthouse, delivers that timeless pleasure of watching a little world reborn under your efforts, and its old-Pokémon pixel-art aesthetic bathes the whole in a gentle, welcoming nostalgia. The care for comfort, with its helpers that lighten the chore, shows an attentive developer, and the 97% demo confirms the alchemy works. For whoever seeks a warm refuge to decompress, it's a gem.

You just have to approach it with the right expectations. Suncraft doesn't reinvent the village cozy game, it owns its dual lineage with Pokémon and Stardew and bets on charm over originality, which will make it familiar to the genre fan. And its lifespan, like that of all games in the genre, will depend on the richness of its content to avoid repetition, despite the developer's good comfort reflexes. But on the essential, the gentleness, the charm and that rare feeling of well-being it delivers, Suncraft keeps exactly its promise. A nostalgic, warm cocoon, the kind you launch for a quick session and drop hours later, a smile on your lips.

## Verdict

A pixel-art cozy game that marries Pokémon's retro aesthetic with Stardew Valley's village gameplay: a nostalgic, warm cocoon with irresistible charm, on a familiar but careful recipe.

**Strengths:**

- A nostalgic pixel-art aesthetic with an old-Pokémon vibe
- A soothing loop of gathering and rebuilding inherited from Stardew
- The unifying goal of relighting the grand Lighthouse, giving meaning
- A care for comfort, with helpers against the chore

**Weaknesses:**

- A familiar recipe that doesn't reinvent the village cozy game
- Charm and gentleness that prevail over originality
- A lifespan and risk of repetition to confirm over time
