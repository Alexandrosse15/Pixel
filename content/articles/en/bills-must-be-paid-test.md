Few games sum up modern economic dread as well as this one: you are buried in bills, and your only solution is to smash piggy banks until your hand gives out. Bills Must Be Paid has the merit of a satirical idea that lands at first glance. Still, the joke, tasty as it is, runs out faster than your supply of piggy banks.

![Bills Must Be Paid, the piggy bank smashing loop](/images/billsmustbepaid/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Bills Must Be Paid is developed by Rike Games and available on PC via Steam, with a free demo and a browser version to get a feel before buying. It is an active incremental game whose premise is as absurd as it is telling: paying your bills by smashing piggy banks. Each hit costs stamina, and when your hand is too tired, the run ends. The bills pile up, and ignoring them leads straight to bankruptcy. Behind the clicker mechanic hides a little satire of precarity that gives the game its flavour.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is the tension between smashing and holding on. You smash piggy banks to collect money, but each hit eats into a stamina gauge, and when the hand is spent, the run ends. This simple but clever constraint prevents mindless mashing and imposes a rhythm: you manage your fatigue like a resource, pushing to the edge of breaking point to maximise the take. Paying your bills unlocks perks that help you pay back faster, creating the self-improvement loop typical of the genre, and the "run, bankruptcy, restart stronger" structure gives the skeleton of an addictive progression.

The meta-progression system is where the game plays its cards. Paying your bills earns legacy points, which you spend on rings and bracelets to upgrade your hand after declaring bankruptcy, a narratively funny way to start over more powerful. The skill tree is not short on humour: grip strength, caffeine addiction, gym sessions, wrist warm-ups, luck increases, and wilder abilities like rock rain or electrified hammers. Different hammers offer trade-offs, one with higher crit chance and a reduced radius, another ultra-fast with a big radius but less damage. There is a genuine little optimisation layer to explore.

![Bills Must Be Paid, the skill tree and the hand upgrades](/images/billsmustbepaid/screenshot-2.webp)

Where it falls short is on real depth and longevity. Beneath its clever satirical dressing, Bills Must Be Paid remains a clicker with a classic structure, and once the concept is grasped and the first meta-progression tiers crossed, repetition sets in fast. The humour of the skill tree carries for a while, but it does not indefinitely compensate for a loop that, mechanically, goes in circles. The game genuinely amuses over its first sessions, then reveals it does not have many cards in reserve to renew interest over the long term.

It is the paradox of concept-driven incrementals: the idea appeals immediately, but the execution then has to sustain hours of play, and that is where Bills Must Be Paid shows its limits. The satire works, the stamina mechanic is astute, but the whole lacks that little extra of systems or surprises that would have turned a good joke into a genuinely gripping game over time.

## The tech

Technically, Bills Must Be Paid owns a simple, functional presentation, coherent with its status as a small concept game. The act of smashing piggy banks is satisfying, with the visual and audio feedback needed to make the destruction enjoyable, and the skill tree interface stays legible despite the whimsy of its labels. The existence of a browser version underlines the light, accessible nature of the project.

The technical reservations are minor and on the scale of the game. A bit more visual variety in the targets and a more marked staging of the progression tiers would have reinforced the sense of evolution. But the whole runs without a hitch and serves its loop correctly, never betraying its modest ambition through an annoying technical flaw.

![Bills Must Be Paid, bankruptcy and restarting with new bonuses](/images/billsmustbepaid/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Bills Must Be Paid starts from an excellent satirical idea and translates it into a clever clicker loop, carried by an astute stamina mechanic and a skill tree full of humour. Over its first sessions, the game amuses and hooks, and its concept of economic survival by hammer blows hits the mark. For the price of a small concept game, there is real pleasure to draw from it.

But we have to be honest about its limits: beneath the satire, the structure remains that of a classic clicker, and repetition sets in once the novelty fades, for lack of systems deep enough to renew interest. It is a good satirical stress-reliever to savour in small doses, not an incremental that will hold you for dozens of hours. A successful joke that deserved a bit more substance behind the punchline.

**A satire of debt that hits hard at first, but whose loop runs out of steam before the last bill.**

Score: 6/10
