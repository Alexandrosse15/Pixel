Your fleet is hours away from the enemy's homeworld. And somebody else's is hours from yours.

If that sentence raised an old anxiety, you played OGame. And you know exactly what VoidCraft is trying to resurrect.

![VoidCraft, the shared star map](/images/voidcraft/screenshot-1.webp)

The game has no firm date yet and it will be free. No score today.

## What it puts back on the table

The studio states its thesis plainly: bring back what the modern mobile 4X wave threw away, namely real-time fleet travel, genuine consequences, and a galaxy you can actually win.

**Real-time travel.** You dispatch fleets across a shared star map and watch the warp trail draw towards its target, with a live ETA. When a hostile fleet is inbound, the countdown is real: you can rally your defences, call reinforcements, or ask your alliance for help before it lands.

That is the genre's founding mechanic, and it produces a tension nothing has ever replaced. A game where an attack takes six hours to arrive is a game where you check your phone during dinner.

**The replayable battle.** Combat resolves into a deterministic replay you can watch second by second and share, with a full breakdown of losses and haul. Good idea: it turns a result into a story, and stories are what keep these communities alive.

**The rest of the vocabulary.** Mining, building, research across your worlds. A tech tree running from Energy to Lasers to Plasma. Tiered espionage. And relics scattered across the galaxy, whose discovery is meant to explain why everything resets.

## The reset, and why it is the game's best idea

Every six months the galaxy goes through what the studio calls a Big Bang: empires are archived into an era hall of fame, and a fresh shard spins up.

The studio justifies it exactly right: no entrenched whales, no dead servers, and a real shot for everyone.

That is the answer to the genre's fatal flaw. In a real-time fleet game, whoever has six months of head start is untouchable, and the newcomer is a resource deposit for the veterans. Resetting the counters twice a year turns a structural problem into a season calendar.

On monetisation, the studio is equally direct: free, never pay-to-win, cosmetics only, no gacha, no vaults, no buying your way past anyone.

Those are promises, not verified facts, and that will need checking at launch. But at least they are written down in black and white.

## The AI disclosure, and it deserves to be read in full

The developer filled in the box Steam provides, and filled it in thoroughly. Here is what it says.

During development, generative AI tools were used to produce content that ships with the game, all reviewed and edited by the developer before inclusion:

- **Text**: lore, item and research descriptions, mission templates, NPC flavour text.
- **3D models**: the buildings and ships were generated with text-to-3D tools from the studio's own prompts, then cleaned up and optimised.
- **Images**: concept art for ships and buildings, the kind shown in the fleet roster and in dossiers.
- **Voice and sound**: the narration of in-game lore entries is AI text-to-speech, and the battle sound effects and ambience came from an AI tool.
- **Music**: the soundtrack is generated.
- Marketing visuals were also explored with these tools.

And during gameplay, two features generate text live using large language models: an optional Advisor that analyses your empire and offers recommendations, and contract missions.

I am reporting that as it stands, with no moral commentary, for three reasons.

First, **it is factual information published by the studio itself**, not an accusation. Second, the disclosure is precise and exhaustive, which is rare and honest: plenty settle for a vague line. Third, it covers roughly the entire visible and audible production chain, so it is information a buyer needs in order to decide.

Your call. We review games, not methods, but we do not hide what is written on the store page.

## What will need checking

**Population and its distribution.** A real-time fleet game without neighbours is a game of timers. The galaxy has to be populated enough for your system to have rivals in range.

**Whether the cosmetics-only promise holds.** Easy to write before launch, harder to hold after six months of operation.

**And the rhythm.** Saying the universe keeps running when you log off is accurate and it is the salt of the genre, but it is also what wakes people at three in the morning. The game will need genuine automated defences, otherwise only those willing to live on galactic time will remain.

![VoidCraft, the tech tree](/images/voidcraft/screenshot-4.webp)

I have affection for this genre. It produced friendships, alliances, betrayals and sleepless nights, and it has all but vanished, replaced by building games where you wait out a timer you can shorten by paying.

Seeing somebody pick it back up, with six-monthly resets and a written cosmetics-only commitment, is good news. We will judge the evidence.
