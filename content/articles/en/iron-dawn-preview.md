Iron Dawn rests on a single question, asked every mission: do I keep going?

It is an isometric shooter where you pilot a mercenary mech across fully destructible battlefields. Each contract has objectives. Completing them earns the full payout. But you can extract at any time to lock in what you have already earned, and everything you destroy pays out, even if you fail. It is extraction-game logic applied to a short loop of 5 to 15 minute missions.

![An orange explosion in an isometric city, among destroyed buildings](/images/irondawn/screenshot-1.webp)

The money buys better mechs and better weapons. You choose your chassis (bipedal, quadrupedal or tracked), your turret and your weapons, and every component changes how the machine moves, fights and survives. You also have to balance armour, mobility and firepower against deployment cost: heading out in the big machine is expensive, and losing it is even more so.

![A mech in the corridors of an indoor base](/images/irondawn/screenshot-3.webp)

Polylab Studio keeps a low profile, and the Steam page does not say much more. But the pitch is clear, and the demo lets you try it before release on 26 November. The low-poly look, with buildings collapsing into cubes, has plenty of charm.

Are you the type to extract early, or to risk it all for the payout? The debate is open on the [InsertCoins Discord](https://discord.gg/473FE3dWvw).
