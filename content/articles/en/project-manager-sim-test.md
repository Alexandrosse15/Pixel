There is something deeply absurd about coming home from work, turning on your PC and launching a game that puts you right back into the shoes of the person you just left at the office. Project Manager SIM owns that paradox with disconcerting nerve: it is a game where you manage a team, difficult clients and a moody hierarchy, and it does so with a realism that occasionally triggers genuine work flashbacks. Strangely, it works.

![Project Manager SIM, the view of the office and the team to manage](/images/projectmanagersim/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Project Manager SIM is developed by a solo developer and launches in Early Access on PC via Steam. Inspired by the real struggles of the corporate world, it puts you in the project manager's chair, tasked with balancing your team's burnout, chaotic client contracts and management's shifting moods. You walk the office floor, talk to your staff, and activate your management skills from your own desk. That a single developer built a company simulation this detailed already commands respect.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is human management, and that is where it shines. Every employee has a personality, traits, relationships, moods and an efficiency level, which turns your team into a genuine little social ecosystem rather than a column of stats. Two colleagues who do not get along drag down their productivity, a talent on the edge of exhaustion threatens to crack, a difficult character demands tailored handling. Navigating this micro-society, arbitrating between everyone's wellbeing and the deadlines falling due, is exactly the tension of the real job, rendered with a truth that surprises.

Being able to physically walk the office and talk to your team from your character adds a welcome layer of embodiment. You do not pilot spreadsheets from afar, you go see people, take the pulse, intervene. The game offers two project types, traditional and support, each with its own mechanics, which keeps management from boiling down to a single recipe. And to perfect the sometimes cruel realism, it folds in the realities of the IT sector: burnout, vacations, days off, sick leaves, and even competitors headhunting your best people. Nothing is sugar-coated.

![Project Manager SIM, an employee card with their traits and mood](/images/projectmanagersim/screenshot-2.webp)

That is also where the game's paradoxical limit lies. This realism, its greatest strength, is sometimes its ludic weakness. By reproducing office management so well, with its frustrations, surprises and mental load, the game can become stressful rather than entertaining, and deliver exactly that "going back to work" feeling that will win over some as much as it puts off others. It is an owned and intelligent design choice, but one that mechanically narrows its audience to those who find pleasure in simulating management, not escaping it.

The other reservations are those of a solo-made Early Access. Some loops need deepening to hold up over the very long term, the balance between random events and player control deserves tuning, and a few systems would gain in clarity. Nothing surprising at this stage, and the room for growth is obvious, but the current state remains a promising foundation more than a finished game.

## The tech

Technically, Project Manager SIM owns a sober, functional presentation, coherent with its subject. The office-floor view is legible, the employee cards with their traits and moods are clear, and the whole favours information over flash, which is the right reflex for a management game. For a solo production, the density of systems shown without major confusion is a real achievement.

The technical limits are those of the format. The interface, dense by nature, takes time to adjust to and would benefit from better prioritising its most critical information. A few finishing touches are missing, clearer feedback on the impact of certain decisions and better staging of standout events. But the whole runs correctly and never betrays its ambition through a lack of stability.

![Project Manager SIM, managing projects and deadlines](/images/projectmanagersim/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Project Manager SIM is a niche success, carried by an idea as cheeky as it is effective: simulating office management with a realism that does not cheat. Human management, with its living employees, its tensions and its fragile balance between wellbeing and productivity, is fascinating for anyone who loves this register. That a solo developer reached this level of detail is remarkable, and the foundation is solid.

Its strength is also its limit: by reproducing work so well, it sometimes imports its stress, and its pleasure clearly addresses fans of demanding simulation rather than a mainstream audience seeking relaxation. Add the inevitable rough edges of a solo Early Access, and you get a highly recommendable game in its category, provided you know what you came for. If the idea of managing an office after your office day amuses you, go for it. Otherwise, it will remind you why you closed your laptop.

**A management simulation so realistic it becomes a specialist's pleasure, for better and sometimes for the stress.**

Score: 7/10
