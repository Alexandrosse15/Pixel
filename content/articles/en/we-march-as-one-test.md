You do not place your troops one by one. You paint them.

In We March as One, you pick up a brush and draw your army straight onto the battlefield, like colouring in an area. A line of infantry here, a block of shooters behind, a wing on the flank. Then the battle starts, and hundreds of little blue geometric shapes march towards hundreds of red ones.

![Two massive armies of blue and red blocks about to collide](/images/wemarchasone/screenshot-2.webp)

Every soldier and every projectile is simulated individually, and that is what makes the spectacle so satisfying. Lines bend, formations give way in places, a breach opens on a flank. You can follow the action in isometric view, top-down, or in a free fly-through.

The loop is an incremental game's. Win or lose, defeated enemies earn gold. You recruit more troops, unlock new unit types in a skill tree, and come back with a stronger army. Fallen soldiers return for the next battle: you keep the army you built.

![The skill tree and its unlockable units](/images/wemarchasone/screenshot-4.webp)

The full game adds what demo players asked for: hold and march orders before battle, formation tools to copy, paste and move units, and a full level editor with terrain painting.

It is a minimalist game, and it does not try to be anything else. Anyone after Total War-level tactical depth will find only a sketch here. Anyone who likes watching a simple strategy unfold en masse will spend some good hours with it, for 9.99 dollars, less with the launch discount.
