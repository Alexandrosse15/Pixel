Your first reflex, seeing the image from a distance, was to assume this must be a German game. That is not prejudice, it is experience: management simulators are a genre historically dominated by German studios, to the point that the functional, slightly austere look of these games has become a signature recognisable at a glance.

This one comes from a small studio called IORDER, but your instinct was right about what it offers: it is exactly the genre brief, no more, no less.

![Airport Manager Simulator 2026, managing an international airport](/images/airportmanager2026/screenshot-1.webp)

## The Die Hard 2 question

It deserves asking seriously, because it sits at the heart of the genre's appeal. Do we really want to shoulder responsibility for an airport and the disasters that implies?

For anyone who has seen Die Hard 2, the answer is obviously yes. That film did for the airport what maritime disasters did for the ocean liner: it showed its systemic fragility. Aircraft holding with no fuel left, a neutralised control tower, a blizzard, and that terrifying idea that an airport is nothing but a chain of dependencies where one broken link brings down everything else.

That is precisely what this kind of game promises, and the store page puts it well: every system is connected, and your decisions directly affect overall efficiency. The pleasure of airport management comes from there, from the cascade, not from the building.

## What you do

The programme is classic and complete. You manage passenger flow, baggage handling, aircraft servicing and preparation, and flight scheduling. You constantly arbitrate between operational efficiency, safety standards and customer satisfaction.

The point that sets the game slightly apart is that you do not only supervise from a dashboard. You take direct control of ground equipment: boarding bridges, baggage conveyors, fuel trucks. That mix of overview and hands-on operation is what works best in recent simulators, because it avoids the sense of only playing with menus.

## The reservations, and they are real

Some tempering is needed, because the game's presentation stays very generic. The store page promises detailed environments, stunning visuals and authentic sound design, which is exactly the vocabulary used by every simulator in this category, including the least polished. Nothing there distinguishes an ambitious production from a quick product.

Two signals call for caution. The game launches in Early Access, with the incomplete content that implies. And it is offered in English only, which for a management game full of terminology is not trivial and says a great deal about the resources behind it.

No user reviews exist yet, the game launching today, hence the absence of a score.

The real question will be the depth of the simulation. An airport is a fascinating system to run if the dependencies are genuinely modelled and a mistake propagates. If incidents amount to a stream of alerts to click, boredom arrives fast. We would love to find the tension of a stormy night at Dulles in here. We are waiting to see.
