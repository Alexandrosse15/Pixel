The auto-battler is a genre where you prepare then watch, where everything plays out before the first blow lands. Order Automatica pushes that logic to its most radical distillation: a ritual grid of three by three, nine slots, and nothing more. You might think it a limitation, it's actually a statement of intent. By stripping the space to the bone, the game turns every placement into a crucial decision and describes itself as chess with demons. Dark, tense, clever: here's a little roguelite proving you can do a lot with very little.

![Order Automatica, the dark roguelite auto-battler on its 3x3 ritual grid](/images/orderautomatica/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Order Automatica is a dark tactical roguelite developed by New Beings, available on PC since 27 July 2026, with console versions including the Switch also planned. The principle: build synergies and position your units on a small three-by-three ritual grid, then watch the battles unfold automatically. But behind the automation, success depends entirely on careful planning, creating powerful synergies and staying one step ahead of the opponent. You arrange your units, harness forbidden relics, and must complete the ritual before your last candle burns out. The game runs perfectly on Steam Deck and offers full controller support.

## The gameplay: nine slots, a thousand decisions

All of Order Automatica's genius lies in its constraint. The three-by-three grid offers only nine slots, and that tightness is precisely what gives the game its depth. Every placed unit counts, every adjacency creates or breaks a synergy, and there's nowhere to hide and no slot to waste. Where other auto-battlers drown the player under dozens of tiles and units, Order Automatica concentrates the thinking on a tiny space where the slightest positioning decision has immediate repercussions. That's what justifies the chess comparison: few pieces, a restricted board, but a tactical depth that explodes the moment you start anticipating.

The roguelite layer enriches that base. Between fights, you collect forbidden relics that transform the run's rules and open new synergies, in that logic of constant improvisation specific to the genre. The burning-candle mechanic adds welcome tension: you're not playing against a mere opponent, you're also racing against time, against the extinction of your own light. That pressure gives rhythm and stakes to fights that, elsewhere, can turn into passive contemplation. Automated battles, yes, but nervous, decisive preparation: that's where the pleasure nestles, in that moment when you arrange your ritual betting on the synergies that will tip the match.

![Order Automatica, forbidden relics and the burning candle add tension and synergies](/images/orderautomatica/screenshot-2.webp)

## What sticks

You have to stay lucid about the genre's limits. The auto-battler stays a particular pleasure, that of preparation and optimization rather than direct control: you arrange, then delegate the combat to the machine. Whoever doesn't take to this principle, to this form of armchair strategy where you watch your choices play out without intervening, won't change their mind here. Order Automatica fully owns that identity, and its radical minimalism even pushes it to the extreme. It's a game of thought and anticipation, not action, and you should know that before diving in.

The other reservation is balance and variety, always a delicate ground for a roguelite. With such a restricted space, everything rests on the richness of the units, relics and synergies to keep runs from ending up resembling each other. The risk, with a nine-slot grid, is seeing dominant combinations emerge that flatten diversity, or conversely going in circles once the best synergies are identified. Longevity will therefore depend on the generosity of the content and the studio's support. But on principle, the constraint is a strength, and it's that distillation that makes the title's originality and charm.

## What we take away

Order Automatica is a fine demonstration that a strong constraint can birth a deep game. By reducing the auto-battler to a nine-slot ritual grid, it turns every placement into a crucial decision and distills a tactical tension worthy of chess, thickened by its roguelite layer of forbidden relics and its burning-candle mechanic. Dark, stripped-down and nervous in its preparation, it's a little roguelite with a real identity that proves, once again, that well-thought-out minimalism beats confused abundance. For the genre fan, it's a clever gem.

You just have to love the auto-battler and accept its codes. That pleasure of delegated preparation, where you watch your choices play out without intervening, won't speak to everyone, and the balance and variety of such a tight roguelite will need confirming over time. But for whoever seeks a dark, demanding game of thought, fitting in the palm of your hand and perfect on Steam Deck, Order Automatica offers exactly what it promises: chess with demons, tense to the last candle. An owned constraint, turned into an asset.

## Verdict

A dark, minimalist roguelite auto-battler on a 3x3 ritual grid, where every placement is a crucial decision: tense, clever and original, to reserve for genre fans.

**Strengths:**

- The 3x3 grid that turns the constraint into tactical depth
- A tension worthy of chess, with decisive synergies
- The forbidden relics and burning candle, tension and replayability
- A format perfect on Steam Deck, full controller support

**Weaknesses:**

- The auto-battler principle, which won't speak to all
- A balance and variety to confirm over time
- A risk of dominant synergies on such a restricted space
