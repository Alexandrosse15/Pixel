9 Kings was originally supposed to have 81 cards. Nine kings, nine cards each, a tidy number. Version 1.0 has more than 130.

Some Early Access periods are about finishing a game. This one was about growing it far beyond what was planned, and that is the best compliment you can pay a studio.

![A kingdom under construction on its grid, in the middle of the forest](/images/9kings/screenshot-1.webp)

## What it is, for those who missed it

A fast, brutal kingdom-building roguelike. You are a king, with a starting deck of buildings, troops and spells. You place them on a small grid, and placement matters: buildings boost their neighbours, combos stack. Every year a rival king attacks, and the battle plays out automatically. Win, and you can grab their cards. Lose, and your kingdom pays the price.

The game's motto is breaking the game, and it means it. Absurd combos are the goal, not a bug. Knights, occultists, imps, walking mushrooms, thousands of mercenaries bought with gold, the dead raised again. Reaching the end of a run with an army that no longer makes any sense is the main pleasure.

![Choosing a king at the start of a run, presented as playing cards](/images/9kings/screenshot-3.webp)

## What 1.0 adds

Four game modes: classic single-player, with dozens of difficulty levels; asynchronous multiplayer, where you take on other players' kingdoms to climb the ladder; a Quest mode that travels across the nine lands with puzzle-like challenges; and the much-requested Joker mode, to customise your deck and the rules.

More than 140 decrees, with each king now getting nine of its own, and more than 110 perks. Enough to find hundreds of new combinations for anyone who thought they had seen it all.

![A desert kingdom, with an army lined up below the citadel](/images/9kings/screenshot-4.webp)

The flaw is the same as in Early Access: runs share the same rhythm, and once you work out which kings and decrees dominate, the curve of discovery slows. But 9 Kings has always been a short-session game, and it still is.

More than 2,600 players were online on launch day, and the half-price launch discount puts it at 9.99 euros. It is the best strategy roguelike of the week, and one of the best of the year.
