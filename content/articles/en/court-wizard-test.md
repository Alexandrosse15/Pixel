There is a very particular power fantasy in playing the court wizard, the one who, alone against the hordes, transforms the battlefield through the sheer force of their spells. Court Wizard cultivates that fantasy with a clever idea: each wizard archetype does not just change your stats, it radically changes how you play. The result is a replayable and demanding wave defense that compensates for a modest presentation with real strategic depth.

![Court Wizard, the court wizard casting spells against waves of attackers](/images/courtwizard/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Court Wizard is developed by Blackhearth Games and available on PC via Steam. It is a real-time strategy game where you cast spells, brew potions and command defenders to survive endless waves of attackers. You choose from radically different wizard archetypes, master your spells via talent trees, and the goal is to hold against the assault while seeking to defeat all the bosses. Tagged casual, indie, RPG and strategy, the game owns a genre blend around a loop of survival and progression.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is wave defense nourished by real tactical richness. You do not just spam spells: you have to manage your positioning, your defenders, your potions and the timing of your abilities to hold against ever-denser assaults. That combination of real time and resource management gives the fights a satisfying strategic tension, where every decision counts and panic is paid for dearly. Progression via the talent trees lets you specialise your wizard over the runs, adding a layer of customisation that feeds experimentation.

What sets Court Wizard apart is its master idea: each wizard archetype radically transforms how you play. We are not talking about simple stat variations, but profoundly different play styles that force you to rethink your entire strategy. That diversity is the real engine of replayability: switching from one archetype to another means relearning how to play, discovering new synergies and new approaches against the same waves. Combined with the goal of defeating all the bosses, it gives a solid reason to restart the game again and again to master each path.

![Court Wizard, the wizard archetypes that radically change the play style](/images/courtwizard/screenshot-2.webp)

The game's limits lie in its scale and its presentation, on the scale of a modest independent production. The game favours the depth of its system over the dressing, which is coherent but makes for a visually sober whole, far from the glitz of big productions. The wave-defense loop, gripping, can also reveal a certain repetitiveness over the long haul, once you have gone through the archetypes and strategies. That is the genre's lot, and the renewal rests largely on the diversity of wizards rather than an abundance of content.

The other reservation concerns the balance, often delicate in a game where each archetype offers a radically different experience. Some paths prove more effective than others, and the demand of surviving the waves can create abrupt difficulty spikes that will destabilise players who came for a relaxed experience. Court Wizard is a game you earn and that rewards strategic investment, rather than an accessible stress-reliever, and you have to know what you came for.

## The tech

Technically, Court Wizard owns a functional presentation in service of its mechanic. The legibility of the action, crucial in a wave defense where you must constantly watch the attackers, your spells and your defenders, is correct once the game is tamed. The interface gives the information needed for decision-making, an essential condition for a strategy game resting on management and anticipation.

The technical reservations are on the scale of the project, that is, modest but real. A bit more visual variety and a slightly more careful dressing would have been welcome to reinforce immersion in this wizard fantasy. But the whole runs cleanly and effectively serves its strategic depth, without betraying its system through annoying technical flaws. It is a game that bets everything on its replayability and its archetype diversity, and for this type of title, that is the right trade-off.

![Court Wizard, surviving the waves and the goal of defeating all the bosses](/images/courtwizard/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Court Wizard is a clever, replayable wave defense, carried by a strong master idea: wizard archetypes that radically transform how you play. Its tactical richness, its progression via talent trees and its diversity of styles make it a gripping strategy game that rewards experimentation and gives a real reason to restart to master each path. For fans of strategic wave defense, there is a fine depth to explore here.

Its limits lie in its modest presentation, a repetitiveness that shows over the long haul and an imperfect balance between archetypes. But in its register, that of the replayable, demanding wave defense, Court Wizard keeps its promises and stands out through its intelligence. To reserve for strategists who like every game to be a new way to play, and who find their pleasure in mastery rather than immediate relaxation.

**A clever wave defense where every wizard reinvents the game, deep and replayable despite a sober dressing.**

Score: 7/10
