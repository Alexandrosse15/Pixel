The opening minutes recall something specific, and it is not a recent game: that way of dropping you into a hostile place with no explanation, a torch and a vague mission belongs to the early Resident Evil school. You move forward because you must, not because a marker says so.

What follows, however, belongs to this game alone.

![SILENT BREATH, the night forest](/images/silentbreath/screenshot-1.webp)

## The idea, and it is excellent

You are in the middle of a mysterious forest and you have to find people who got lost there before you.

The central rule fits in one word: **stay silent**. At night, the forest listens to your voice **through your microphone**. And it spends its time trying to make you scream, using scares of every kind.

Every scream brings you one step closer to losing.

That is a mechanic doing exactly what good horror should: it makes you responsible for your own fear. In most games in the genre, flinching has no consequence. Here your physical reaction is the failure. You find yourself holding back, breathing through your nose, gritting your teeth, and that bodily tension is worth every script ever written.

The game adds a second layer so that caution alone is not enough: there are also **real threats** hunting you, so you have to watch your back constantly. You lose by screaming, or by being caught.

Finally, on each failure the game restarts but **the locations of the missing people you already found have changed**. Memorisation does not save you, which keeps the tension alive from attempt to attempt.

![SILENT BREATH, the dynamic scares](/images/silentbreath/screenshot-2.webp)

## A note on the calendar

A word to clear up an easy confusion: **this game is not a newcomer**. It launched on 5 April 2024 in early access, and it is still there.

That explains why you find articles and videos about it going back more than two years. This is not a release, it is a long-running project from a solo developer, EXODIAC STUDIOS, with regular updates.

The most important arrived in early 2026: the free addition of a **multiplayer mode**, with an open mic option letting you hear your friends scream in real time. On a game built around voice detection, turning the constraint into a shared spectacle is the best possible idea, and it is probably what gave it a second life.

## What to know

The game runs on Unreal Engine 5 and bets on photorealistic rendering and fully dynamic scares, which prevents learning positions by heart.

Three reservations.

**The microphone is essential to the experience.** Without it, what remains is a competent but unremarkable horror game. This is a purchase conditioned on your hardware and your environment: playing at three in the morning with adjoining neighbours poses a problem the game will not solve.

**It is a long-running early access.** More than two years, versioning hovering around 0.99, and an update rhythm dictated by one person's availability.

**The format is short.** This is a scare experience, not an adventure. It is best taken in sessions, ideally with company.

![SILENT BREATH, searching for the missing](/images/silentbreath/screenshot-3.webp)

## What sets the score

It rewards a central mechanic that barely exists anywhere else and that physically engages the player, a structure that shuffles locations on every failure to prevent memorisation, and a multiplayer mode that multiplies the value of the original idea.

It accounts for total dependence on hardware and playing context, an early access dragging on, and content that remains a short experience rather than a complete game.

For an evening with friends and open mics, there is not much better.
