Duo Quest's villain is called the Demon King and his evil invention is called the **Bondbreaker**.

On paper that is a gag. In practice it is the clearest possible statement of intent: in this game, your weapon is your relationship.

![Duo Quest, the two adventurers](/images/duoquest/screenshot-1.webp)

## How it works

You are two adventurers, the kingdom's last hope, wielding **Duo Energy**, a weapon whose strength is tied to the bond between its users.

In practice it is a co-op deckbuilder with a mechanic on top: **over a thousand questions** are put to you, and you and your partner have to **match your answers** to defeat enemies.

The studio advertises over twenty question themes, and every choice shapes your strategy and unlocks combo cards.

So this is not a quiz sitting beside a card game. Guessing what the other person will answer is the combat mechanic.

## Why it is clever

Because it solves a problem nobody solves: **getting somebody who does not play games to play**.

The studio says it openly, the game is designed so players can drag their non-gaming friends and partners in front of a screen. And the mechanic suits that perfectly, since the skill required is not dexterity but knowledge of the other person.

Your partner who has never held a controller starts with a considerable advantage over you: they know what you would answer.

That is also why the game is **two-player only**, in split screen, with no solo mode. The constraint is owned and it makes sense.

![Duo Quest, the questions and cards](/images/duoquest/screenshot-3.webp)

## What surrounds it

The rest is an honest roguelite: procedurally generated levels with randomised routes, bosses, secrets to find by replaying levels, and cards to unlock.

The presentation is a deliberately offbeat 8-bit fantasy that does the job without taking itself seriously.

## What to know

The game launches today, developed by 1+1 Studios and co-published by Rising Tide and Electronic Sheep Games. No price displayed as I write, no reviews.

Two sincere warnings.

**There is no solo mode.** If you have nobody to hand, this game does not exist for you. It is stated plainly and it is not an omission, it is the project.

**And the questions can sting.** A game that tests your relationship with provocative questions will raise subjects you had not planned to open on a Tuesday evening. That is the salt of the concept, and it is also the risk.

That said, in a year saturated with co-op games that settle for putting two people in the same room, this one understood that a two-player game ought to be about what happens between the two.
