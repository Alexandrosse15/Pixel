There is a line in Deadly Days: Roadtrip's description that half the industry should copy.

You decide when to extract, **alone or in co-op with a friend who doesn't even need to own the game.**

A publisher giving away the second licence, in 2026, in a 9.74 euro game. Worth noting, because it has become rare enough to be a selling point.

![Deadly Days: Roadtrip, the bus](/images/deadlydaysroadtrip/screenshot-1.webp)

## The backpack is the game

This is the central mechanic and it is well found.

Space is tight on a road trip. Every weapon and item takes up room in your tiny backpack, and you have to **arrange them just right so their adjacency effects stack** and power up your weapons. You can also merge them into stronger versions, or craft brand-new legendaries.

In other words, your power does not come from what you found, it comes from how you packed it.

Players spontaneously compare it to Resident Evil's inventory, and the comparison is fair: the spatial constraint turns loot into a puzzle rather than a list.

The studio is upfront about the destination: keep stacking and at some point the numbers stop making sense. Congratulations, you broke the game. There is even an Endless Mode where your build keeps scaling until it, or your PC, finally gives up.

## The setting

A 2.5D pixel art bullet heaven in a zombie apocalypse, where you drive a bus collecting fuel, with a final boss at the end of the road.

It is effective, it gives direction to what would otherwise be a run of arenas, and the bus elegantly solves the hub problem: your base travels with you.

![Deadly Days: Roadtrip, the backpack](/images/deadlydaysroadtrip/screenshot-2.webp)

## What players say

**694 reviews, 87 per cent positive.** And the reports are detailed, which helps.

One player with thirty hours of beta behind him writes that it is his game of the year, honestly noting it may not beat 2026's competition, and that the only other title in the genre that comes close for him is Brotato.

Another makes a list that sums the content up well: good progression system, fun Resident Evil style inventory, missions that are not too hard with reasonable boss fights, crafting, weapons to unlock, map design variety across a run, classes and upgrades, endless mode.

## The two complaints, and they answer each other

**A lack of build variety.** One player writes it plainly: collecting items and puzzling out the backpack is fun, and the game is forgiving enough that you can get away with wackier builds. But there is not much variety, and the combinations rarely change how you play.

**Weapon balance and slow meta-progression.** Another player declines to recommend the game for that precise reason: most weapons are simply unusable because they perform badly, and progression between runs is so slow he finds it dispiriting.

Those two complaints say the same thing from two angles: the system is excellent, the content filling it is less so. That is exactly what an update can fix, and exactly what separates a good game from a very good one.

![Deadly Days: Roadtrip, a horde](/images/deadlydaysroadtrip/screenshot-3.webp)

At ten euros, with a friend playing free, and an inventory mechanic that genuinely demands thought, the contract is amply fulfilled.

Whether Pixelsplit packs its own bag as well as its players do remains to be seen.
