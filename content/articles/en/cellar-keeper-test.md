I like wine. Not as a connoisseur who swirls the glass and talks about minerality, but as someone who opens a good bottle now and then and finds the evening improves for it. So when a game about a wine cellar came past, I had that small favourable reflex we all have toward anything touching a pleasure we know.

Then I read what you do in it. You tidy.

You do not taste, you do not make wine, you do not trade, you do not build a list. You put 2,448 bottles back where they belong. You identify their variety, you carry them to the right rack, and you restore order in a cellar that has fallen into chaos. The number is the one the studio EgoTrampoline advertises, and it should be taken seriously: it is the content of the game.

![Cellar Keeper, the wine cellar organizing simulator](/images/cellarkeeper/screenshot-1.webp)

And here I have to be honest about my own case. I already do not tidy my Warhammer miniatures. They are there, in a heap, half painted, in a state any hobbyist will recognise without needing a photo. The idea of spending my evenings virtually sorting booze I cannot even drink has something faintly cruel about it. It is the chore without the reward.

Which does not make the game bad, and this is where I have to be fair. Virtual tidying is a genre that exists, that works for a lot of people, and whose principle is well understood: watching disorder disappear provides genuine satisfaction, and the game builds everything on that, rack after rack, until the room becomes beautiful again. There is even a small story as a pretext: the cellar belonged to an eccentric collector who, attempting a spell to preserve his collection forever, turned himself into a chicken and ended up trapped in a cage. You tidy, you break the curse, you give him back the daylight. It is absurd and rather endearing.

The content is structured with a little more care than expected. You unlock skills that improve carrying, searching and movement, which gives you progression and a reason to optimise your routes. Above all there are two modes: a cozy mode with no time limit, and a speedrun mode with no skill upgrades, purely about efficiency, with an online leaderboard. That second mode is the best idea here, because it turns the chore into a timed discipline, and that is probably where the game will find its real audience.

![Cellar Keeper, the racks to restore and the bottles to classify](/images/cellarkeeper/screenshot-2.webp)

What remains is the fundamental limit: the game holds on a single note. Once you have understood the identify, carry, classify loop, there is no second system to renew your interest. No tasting, no management, no trade, nothing that extends the cellar fantasy beyond the inventory. Under five euros the deal is honest and nobody is crying robbery, but you should know you are buying an activity, not a game of systems.

So here is my verdict, and it is partial: if tidying relaxes you, go ahead, it is well made for what it is. If you came for the wine, you will stay thirsty, in the most literal sense. As for me, I am going back to not painting my Space Marines, while opening a bottle. A real one.
