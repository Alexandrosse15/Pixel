There are two schools of thought in Meccha Chameleon. Those who, like a hapless knight staring at a map of the realm, fix a blank wall hoping some miracle will turn them invisible. And those who win. The difference isn't luck, it's map knowledge. Flattening dumbly against a smooth wall is a foolproof plan right up until a seeker finds you and looks at you with the weary eyes of a disappointed king. This guide reviews the six official maps and their best hiding spots, so you stop being the prey and become the scenery. For everything else, painting, poses and tactics, head to our [complete Meccha Chameleon guide](/guides/meccha-chameleon-guide).

## The universal principle: imitate what the map already expects

Before detailing each map, a principle that holds everywhere, and that you should repeat like a prayer before every round. A good hiding spot isn't about finding the darkest corner, it's about making your body look like something the map already contains: a cushion, a shelf object, a hay shape, a sign detail, a wall patch, a themed prop, a roof shape, a pole, a ceiling object. The seeker's brain isn't hunting for a strange color, it's hunting for a suspicious shape among shapes that all seem to belong. Your job is to be a shape with a good reason to exist here.

A good hiding spot therefore combines four parameters: it matches your silhouette, it fits the local colors, it uses the surrounding prop density, and it denies the seeker obvious angles. Neglect a single one and you're spotted, with the dignity of a knight falling off his horse in front of the whole court. Keep these four criteria in mind, they'll come back on each of the maps below.

## Where to hide on the Mansion map (Hide-and-Seek Mansion)?

The Mansion is the beginner's map, and for once that's not an insult: with roughly eleven known hiding spots, it's the most generous in the game. Its classic layout of rooms, hallways, bathrooms and framed walls brims with props to imitate, making it the ideal learning ground. This is where you understand the game without ending the round in ten seconds, which, let's admit, does the ego good.

Your best options: the furniture clusters, where your silhouette drowns in a tangle of shapes, and the frames and wall details, which you can imitate by sticking to the wall like one more painting. The bathrooms and their objects also offer good camouflage for whoever can copy the right shade. The Mansion's trap is precisely its richness: you think you're safe everywhere, so you let your guard down. Stay disciplined, imitate a precise object rather than a vague corner, and this map will forgive your early days.

## Where to hide on Indoor Country?

Indoor Country is the second most spot-rich map, with roughly six confirmed spots, and the other good ground for beginners. It mixes outdoor-style geometry, hay bales, standees, crates, with indoor ceiling space, opening two distinct game plans. You can play low, among the rustic props, or look up and exploit the ceiling, where few seekers think to look on their first pass.

The hay bales are your friends: their irregular shape and busy color naturally break your silhouette, provided you tune your settings to match the material. The crate clusters work on the same principle, layering shapes that mask your outline. And if you feel adventurous, the ceiling space rewards boldness, because a seeker sweeping a room at floor level doesn't always think to raise their head. Fair enough, isn't it.

## Where to hide in the Sewer?

The Sewer bumps up the difficulty a notch. This dark industrial environment, made of pipes, barrels and graffiti, offers fewer obvious spots and a peculiar lighting that doesn't forgive mistakes. It's the map that rewards bold color-blocking and clean, edge-complete paint: here, a badly finished outline stands out like a jester in the middle of a battle.

Bet on the barrels and pipes, whose cylindrical shapes and groupings break the reading of your silhouette. The busy graffiti zones are precious, because their patterns scramble the seeker's eye and make your outline hard to isolate. The key on this map is precision: the sewer's harsh light betrays the slightest mis-tuned reflection, so mind your metallic and roughness sliders, and check your disguise from every angle before the hunt starts.

## Where to hide in the Backrooms?

The Backrooms is the map that wants you dead. Recognized as the hardest for new hiders, this monotonous yellow aesthetic offers few props and a visual uniformity that leaves few places to melt your silhouette into. You arrive full of hope, as before a simple quest, and you leave certain that nothing is ever simple.

Your rare allies: the chair piles, the vending rows and the bikes, those prop clusters that are about the only places where your body can dissolve into a group of shapes. Stick to these groupings and imitate their average tone rather than an isolated object. Elsewhere, in the bare yellow hallways, the mission is a calling: better a perfect pose imitating a wall-mounted element than a position in the open, where you'll shine like armor in the sun. On this map, humility and patience save more rounds than boldness.

## Where to hide in the Penguin Hotel and Sugarland?

The Penguin Hotel is a map made for flashes of disguise genius. Its ice-hotel bedrooms, party balloons and penguin-themed props form a busy, colorful setting, perfect for whoever loves imitating a precise themed object. The balloons and decorative elements are your best targets: a well-painted, well-posed silhouette blends into them with disconcerting ease. It's the map where you can truly shine, provided you match the cold hue of the icy atmosphere.

Sugarland, the sixth official map added in the 1.4.0 update, rounds out the picture with its sweet, gaudy universe. The principle there stays rigorously the same as everywhere else: spot the colorful props, imitate their average tone, adopt a pose that turns your body into scenery, and stop moving. The visual richness of these two maps plays in your favor, because the busier the environment, the better your camouflage's chances of going unnoticed. Where the Backrooms leave you bare, Penguin Hotel and Sugarland give you plenty to vanish into with flair.

## Which map should you pick for your level?

To sum up bluntly. If you're a beginner, play the Mansion or Indoor Country: they're the richest maps in hiding spots and visual complexity, the ones that let you learn without humiliating you every round. You'll build the reflex of imitating objects rather than walls, and that's the game's most important lesson. Penguin Hotel and Sugarland are a notch above but stay welcoming thanks to their busy, colorful sets.

If you're after a challenge, the Sewer and the Backrooms are for you. Their limited number of spots and their peculiar lighting demand real strategy, flawless paint and millimeter-precise poses. These are the maps that separate the Sunday hiders from the true chameleons. Whatever you do, remember the principle running through this whole guide: don't try to disappear, try to look like something that belongs. The rest, like any grail, must be earned.

## Meccha Chameleon hiding spots FAQ

**How many maps does Meccha Chameleon have?**

Six official maps: Hide-and-Seek Mansion, Indoor Country, Sewer, Backrooms, Penguin Hotel and Sugarland, the last one added in the 1.4.0 update.

**Which is the best map for beginners?**

The Mansion (Hide-and-Seek Mansion) and Indoor Country, which offer the most hiding spots and the greatest visual richness to learn without getting spotted in ten seconds.

**Which is the hardest map?**

The Backrooms, whose uniform yellow aesthetic and lack of props leave very few places to melt your silhouette into. The Sewer is a notch below in difficulty.

**Where do you hide when there are almost no props?**

Stick to the rare groupings (chairs, vending machines), imitate their average tone, or adopt a perfect pose imitating a wall-mounted element rather than exposing yourself in the open.

**Should you hide in dark corners?**

No, that's a classic mistake. A good hiding spot imitates an object the map already contains, not a mere dark corner that seekers check first.
