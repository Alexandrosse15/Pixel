A murder has been committed. The body is right there, on the floor, and the police are on their way. You have a few minutes.

No Body No Problem! is an asymmetric game for four players. Three play the suspects, who must hide the body, get rid of the evidence and leave without raising suspicion. The fourth is the cop, sent over after the report, who searches every corner and watches every suspicious move.

![A body in the back of a stagecoach, in a Wild West town](/images/nobodynoproblem/screenshot-2.webp)

The game's engine is physics. Almost every object obeys it, and carrying a body down a narrow hallway quickly turns into a disaster: knocked-over furniture, stuck corners, noise that draws attention. The cop is not just looking for the corpse either: a door left open, a broken object, a floor that is too clean, a player walking funny, anything can put them on the trail.

It is a game of bad faith, really, in the line of games where the real danger is your friends. The Steam page says it plainly: under pressure, one of them may rat you out to save their own skin.

![A suspect on a jet ski, beneath a rock arch](/images/nobodynoproblem/screenshot-3.webp)

Maps change things every match: a small apartment, a crowded shop, a multi-storey car park, a suburban neighbourhood playable by day or night (where you also have to check the backyards, behind the grill and under the pink flamingo), and even a Wild West town. An open playtest just ended, and the studio says it noted feedback on the balance between cop and suspects, and on when physics chaos stops being funny and becomes frustrating. That is exactly the question the game will have to answer.

Out on 26 November.
