There is a whole genre of YouTube videos: maps of Europe or the world whose borders shift year after year, front lines advancing, empires rising and falling in fast forward. Their creators often make them by hand, frame by frame, in drawing software. Mapping Creator wants to make their lives easier.

![A map of interwar Europe, countries coloured and named](/images/mappingcreator/screenshot-2.webp)

It is not a grand strategy game, and it says so itself: none of the genre's complex mechanics hold you back. You colour the map however you like, redraw borders with a brush down to the pixel, change names, colours and flags. Then you place units, rotate and drag them to form a front line, and you decide where each battle happens, how big it is and how it ends.

The best tool is the export. The game automatically records your session and turns it into a timelapse video, no extra software required. For someone who wants to tell an alternate history or reconstruct a real war in a few minutes of video, it is a considerable time saver.

![The Iberian peninsula split in two by a front line](/images/mappingcreator/screenshot-1.webp)

The flip side is obvious: without rules, opponents or goals, there is no game in the usual sense. If you do not already have a video project or a story in mind, you will close it after twenty minutes. It is creative software sold on Steam, and that is how it should be judged.

In that role, it does the job but stays fairly basic: units are moved one by one, the interface is spartan, and the base maps still lack detail in some regions. The developer states that generative tools helped with the code, some assets and the logo.

![Yuan dynasty China and Goryeo Korea on the map](/images/mappingcreator/screenshot-3.webp)

At 6.08 euros, it is a reasonable purchase for history video makers and alternate history fans. For everyone else, there is not much to do.
