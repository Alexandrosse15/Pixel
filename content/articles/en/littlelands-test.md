There is a fish-shaped hat in Littlelands. The Steam page mentions it proudly: "Yes, even a fish hat." That sums up the tone rather well: an adventure that never takes itself seriously, but is clearly made with great seriousness.

Littlelands glues together two genres that rarely meet this openly. On one side, an old-school top-down action adventure, with caves, dungeons and simple combat. On the other, country life: fishing, bug catching, a vegetable patch, a farm to restore. You switch between them whenever you like, and the game never pushes you one way.

![The little hero on a stone path, between flowers and fences](/images/littlelands/screenshot-1.webp)

The world is handcrafted, diorama-style, with big-headed characters and sets full of small details. It has the charm of a scale model you want to lean in and inspect. Every neighbour has some small problem, and helping out often leads to an unexpected quest.

![A stone ruin overgrown with plants, and its chests](/images/littlelands/screenshot-3.webp)

Combat stays light. It is designed to be approachable, and anyone hoping for the tension of a real Zelda dungeon will find it a bit soft. The Goblinettes, who stir up trouble, are more annoying than dangerous. That is deliberate, but the dungeons could use more bite.

This is Early Access, made by two developers, Rafael Martín and Kyle Creamer, and published by Apogee. The demo was already heavily reworked based on feedback (keyboard controls moved closer to WASD, auto camera rotation and an object highlight added), which suggests the rest will be closely followed.

![A villager's shop and its items for sale](/images/littlelands/screenshot-4.webp)

At 17.54 euros, the foundation is solid and very pleasant. What it lacks is a bit of challenge and density, and that can come.
