Let us be clear straight away: this game does not launch today, it launches on **22 October**. So this is not a review, it is a reconnaissance.

And there is one idea worth pencilling the date in for.

![THE DEAD WE KNEW, the forest and the shelter](/images/deadweknew/screenshot-1.webp)

## Fog as a mechanic

The zombie survival genre is probably the most saturated in existence, and most candidates settle for stacking the same systems.

This one proposes something finer: **weather drives the threat**.

The wind changes direction, rain falls without warning, and dense fog can completely destroy your visibility. But fog is not only a natural phenomenon, it is also a warning sign: when visibility drops, **zombies become more aggressive** and attack from within the darkness.

That is a simple, well-found idea. It turns weather into an alert system, and therefore into strategic information. Watching fog roll in stops being pretty and becomes a decision: go home, or finish what you started.

## The rest of the programme

The rest is the classic formula, honestly announced.

**A shelter to grow**, from a simple cabin to a larger, more liveable base. You organise, you craft, you make somewhere to rest.

**A twenty-four-hour day-night cycle**, which paces gathering, reinforcing and preparing.

**Food to produce**, and this is the best-detailed part: hunting animals such as deer, processing the meat, cooking it over a fire. And when ready-to-eat food runs short, working the soil, planting tomatoes, growing your crops.

**And co-op for up to four players**, to explore, defend and build together.

![THE DEAD WE KNEW, a night in the fog](/images/deadweknew/screenshot-3.webp)

## What we will be watching on 22 October

Three things, and the first matters most.

**Does the weather keep its promise?** An alert system only has value if it genuinely changes player behaviour. If fog merely darkens the screen without changing how you play, the game becomes one more survival among many.

**Is hunger a problem or a gauge?** The studio details a complete food chain, from hunting to farming. That is promising, provided the chain is a constraint rather than a chore.

**And does co-op bring anything beyond extra hands?** With four players, a survival game has to offer a division of roles, or it merely divides the work by four.

The game arrives in early access, developed and published by MAY19 Studio, with no price displayed as I write.

See you on 22 October.
