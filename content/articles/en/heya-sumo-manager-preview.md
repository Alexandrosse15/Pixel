Sumo has a fiendishly complex ranking system: six divisions, a banzuke recalculated after every tournament, fifteen-bout basho at the top and seven-bout ones below, promotions and demotions that hinge on the exact win-loss record. It is, in short, a management game waiting to be adapted.

Heya! Sumo Manager, from Houseman Games, comes out on 23 November and takes all of it seriously. You run a heya, a stable, with a handful of rikishi with everything to prove. You train them one by one, upgrade the gym, kitchen and bedrooms, hire coaches, scouts and cooks, and keep the koenkai happy, the supporters' club that funds the stable.

![Two wrestlers facing off on the dohyo, in front of a crowd](/images/heyasumomanager/screenshot-1.webp)

The simulated world is vast: more than 650 wrestlers across more than 40 stables, and an endless simulation that follows real tournament rules. More than 150 events and event chains spice up careers: supporter demands, rivalries, scandals.

What sold me is the bouts. The game has a real 3D match engine with live commentary, and all 82 kimarite (the official winning techniques) are animated. Throws, push-outs, even the slightly cheeky sidestep. Before each bout, you study the opponent and pick tactics to exploit their weaknesses.

![The stable roster, wrestler by wrestler](/images/heyasumomanager/screenshot-3.webp)

And for those who know nothing about sumo, every term is explained on hover. It is a detail, but it is the difference between a closed niche game and a gateway into a fascinating sport.

The art style is simple, almost naive, and it remains to be seen whether the management depth holds up over time. But the intent is right.
