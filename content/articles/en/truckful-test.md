Pocketpair is the Palworld studio. The phenomenon, the controversy, millions of copies in days.

Their publishing arm is today putting out a game whose central challenge is transporting a grandmother's vase without breaking it.

I love this swerve.

![Truckful, the country road](/images/truckful/screenshot-1.webp)

## The premise

You drive a pickup through countryside made of winding roads, dusty lowlands and small towns. Villagers hand you errands and quests. And around all of it sits an old wood, and an ancient mystery surrounding it.

It is a single-player game, and it owns that. No bolted-on co-op, no server to populate.

## The mechanic, and it is in the truck bed

Here is what separates Truckful from the mass of delivery games: **the cargo is fragile and it moves.**

The studio gives two examples that say everything. Ice-cold fish that has to arrive fast, so a race against melting. And a classic grandma's vase, which will put your driving skills to the true test.

Read the opposition properly. The fish demands speed. The vase demands care. And the road is the same road.

That is elegant design: instead of a speedometer and a timer, you have cargo with an opinion about your driving. Every corner becomes a negotiation between what you are carrying and what is expected of you.

You then upgrade the truck, its weight and its load capacity, to take on more demanding routes.

![Truckful, a fragile load](/images/truckful/screenshot-2.webp)

## And the countryside is hiding things

The store page insists: this seemingly welcoming countryside has its secrets. Hidden paths, misty wetlands, unforgiving marshes, dusty quicksands, telling stories of the past.

That is the difference between a delivery game and an exploration game disguised as one. The first gives you point A and point B. The second makes you want to take the wrong road to see where it goes.

The landmarks are announced as varied, with secrets behind the branches. The old forest's mystery runs as the through line.

## The honest caveat

I need to be straight: **the game came out a few hours ago and has seven reviews.**

Seven. I am not going to build you a reception analysis on seven people, that would be dishonest, and it is exactly what hurried outlets rightly get criticised for.

What I can tell you is that the pitch is coherent, that the contradictory-cargo idea is good, and that the game promises nothing it cannot deliver: no infinite open world, no multiplayer, no three-year roadmap. A pickup, a region, people who need things.

The two unknowns I am keeping in mind.

**Errand variety.** A game built on cargo fragility has to keep inventing constraints. If after six hours everything comes down to driving slowly, the mechanic runs dry.

**And the density of the mystery.** Announcing an ancient riddle in a forest is promising a resolution. Cosy games that miss that promise leave an odd aftertaste.

![Truckful, the old forest](/images/truckful/screenshot-3.webp)

What appeals to me, and this may be personal, is the format. A quiet driving game with a clear loop, a region to get to know and people to help, plays in forty-minute sessions and asks nothing of you beyond being there.

There have been a lot of games this year demanding commitment. This one just asks you to drive carefully with a vase.

![Truckful, the upgraded pickup](/images/truckful/screenshot-4.webp)

At 17.54 euros, for a finished single-player game with no live service behind it, that is an honest contract. I will revisit it if the community spots something seven people have not seen yet.
