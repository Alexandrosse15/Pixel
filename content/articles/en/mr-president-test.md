It had been a while since a game put us in the Oval Office chair for real, with the weight of decisions that make the world tremble. Mr. President fills that gap with rare ambition: simulating the American presidency in all its complexity, from major legislative reforms to international crises that hit you without warning. It is dense, demanding, and fiendishly gripping for anyone who loves political management games.

![Mr. President, the Oval Office and managing the American presidency](/images/mrpresident/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Mr. President is a sandbox simulation of the United States presidency, leaving Early Access for a full version on PC via Steam on 31 July 2026. The premise is clear and clever: you have already won the election, now it is up to you to govern. You must pursue your legislative agenda, compete with Russia and China, and face crises inspired by real events like 9/11 or the pandemic, all while directing the US military against terror groups and rogue states. It is a political management game in the grand tradition, a genre we had rather lost sight of.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is crisis management, and it offers more than one hundred and fifty, both historical and counterfactual. It is that event richness that gives Mr. President all its flavour: every game becomes a succession of dilemmas where you arbitrate between public opinion, international power balances, budget constraints and your own convictions. Managing a major crisis means weighing the pros and cons of decisions with multiple consequences, and the game excels at making you feel the weight of the office, that thrilling and anxious impression of holding the country's fate in your hands.

The long-term management layer gives the whole depth. Pursuing your legislative agenda, dealing with a Congress you have to convince, managing the geopolitical rivalry with the two great rival powers, all of it makes up a rich simulation where every lever matters. The addition of midterm elections and reelection, which let you extend your presidency to eight years if you convince the voters, intelligently extends the experience and adds a welcome electoral stake to pure management. You get caught up in building a legacy over two terms, juggling crises and ambitions.

![Mr. President, over 150 historical and counterfactual crises to manage](/images/mrpresident/screenshot-2.webp)

The game's limits lie in its demand and its deliberately narrow address. Mr. President is a niche game for fans of political simulation, and its density of systems demands real investment before you grasp all its subtleties. Players looking for an accessible, immediate experience risk feeling overwhelmed by the complexity and seriousness of the subject. It is an owned stance, coherent with the game's ambition, but one that narrows its audience to those genuinely passionate about political management mechanics.

The other reservation concerns presentation and accessibility, on the scale of an independent production. The game favours the depth of its simulation over dressing and interface comfort, which is coherent but can put off. The Mostly Positive rating of 78 percent reflects that balance well: a rich, ambitious game, praised by genre fans, but whose austerity and complexity will not win everyone over beyond its target audience.

## The tech

Technically, Mr. President owns a functional presentation in service of its simulation. The interface, dense by nature, shows the many pieces of information needed for decision-making, which is essential in a game where you constantly juggle opinion, budget, diplomacy and legislation. The legibility of the stakes is correct once the game is tamed, an indispensable condition for a management title of this scope.

The technical reservations join the gameplay ones. The interface takes time to adjust to and would benefit from better guiding the player in discovering its complex mechanics, and the presentation stays sober, far from the glitz of big productions. But the whole runs correctly and effectively serves its simulation ambition, without betraying its depth through major technical flaws. The game bets everything on its system, and for this type of title, that is the right order of priorities.

![Mr. President, geopolitical rivalry and long-term management over two terms](/images/mrpresident/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Mr. President fills a real gap: that of an ambitious, serious political management game that simulates the American presidency in all its complexity. Its event richness with more than one hundred and fifty crises, its geopolitical management and its depth over two terms make it a dense, gripping simulation that conveys the thrilling weight of the office. For genre fans, it is a success that comes at just the right time.

Its limits are those of its ambition: a demand and an austerity that will push away players seeking immediate accessibility, and a modest presentation proper to an independent production. But in its register, that of demanding political simulation, Mr. President keeps its promises and revives a genre we thought dormant. If running the United States from crisis to crisis makes you tick, settle into the chair, it is made for you.

**A dense, demanding presidency sim that revives the political management game, for genre enthusiasts above all.**

Score: 7/10
