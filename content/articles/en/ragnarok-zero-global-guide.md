There are two ways to start Ragnarok. The first involves reading a guide, allocating your points with method and reaching level 50 in three days. The second involves making an INT Swordsman because the name sounded good, dying a hundred times in Payon, and remembering it twenty years later.

The second produces better stories. This guide offers you the first.

![Ragnarok Zero Global, a forest farming map](/images/ragnarokzero/screenshot-2.webp)

## The rule that governs everything else

Before classes, before maps, one thing needs understanding because it changes how you play entirely.

**What slows you down is not the map. It is dying.**

In Zero, dying costs experience, costs the walk back, and costs the re-buff time. A player farming a map at their own level without ever going down progresses faster than a player hitting monsters five levels above and returning to town every ten minutes.

Put differently: the optimal map is not the one giving the most XP per monster, it is the one where you survive without thinking about it. Everything else in this guide follows from that.

Second principle, just as important: **follow the main quests at least to level 20**. They give more experience than the equivalent grind, they equip you for free, and they walk you across the world map. Wild grinding from level 1 is a nostalgic veteran's mistake.

## The six jobs, no diplomacy

Six starting classes at launch: Swordsman, Mage, Merchant, Acolyte, Thief, Archer.

The second jobs, thirteen in total, open on 3 September 2026. What you pick now decides where you land then, so it is worth looking further than the end of the XP bar.

### Mage: the fastest to level

If your goal is to reach the cap as soon as possible, this is it. Bolts do decent damage very early, they hit at range, and a target that dies before reaching you deals no damage at all.

It is also the best candidate for auto-hunting while you do something else, particularly on Flora maps.

The downside, and it is real: you are made of paper. A Mage surrounded by three Orcs does not negotiate, it dies. And the SP bar empties faster than you would think, which means trips back to town or a consumables budget.

Where it leads: **Wizard**, the game's area-damage class, the one that clears a whole screen with Meteor Storm and becomes indispensable in endgame parties.

### Merchant: the best overall pick, and it is counterintuitive

Nobody dreams of playing a merchant at fourteen. Everybody should.

It carries more than anyone, meaning fewer trips to empty the bag. It has a cart, so mobile storage. It has very little downtime. And it buys cheaper and sells dearer, which matters enormously in the first weeks when nobody has money.

Above all, it is the best job for auto-hunting, because auto-hunting's main constraint is carry weight.

Where it leads: **Blacksmith** or **Alchemist**. The Blacksmith is the server's golden anvil, the one who crafts and vacuums up everything on the floor with Greed, a zeny machine doubled with a high-burst melee fighter. The Alchemist commands a homunculus and brews potions, so it is self-sufficient and sells what everyone needs.

Put another way: the Merchant is boring at level 10 and king at level 70.

### Thief: the cheapest to run

That is the argument people always underrate. The Thief needs almost nothing. No arrows to rebuy, no gems, no SP to manage, easy gear to find. You start it and it runs.

It dodges instead of absorbing, which, once AGI is up, makes it surprisingly solid against normal monsters.

Where it leads: **Assassin** or **Rogue**. The Assassin is raw burst, Sonic Blow then Soul Breaker, the class that deletes a target before it finishes its animation. The Rogue masters stealing and skill copying, and it is one of the cleanest long-term farming paths in the game.

### Archer: range without the extreme fragility

Ranged physical damage, a comfortable levelling curve, and good versatility. It hits hard, it hits far, and it does not melt like a Mage.

Its constraint is silly but permanent: arrows. They weigh, they cost, you have to think about them. An Archer running dry mid-dungeon discovers exactly how weakly it punches in melee.

Where it leads: **Hunter**, with traps and the falcon, or the **Bard and Dancer** duo, whose songs give attack speed and SP recovery to the whole party. The duo is not built to play alone, but it transforms a group.

### Swordsman: the one that survives your mistakes

The most forgiving. Plenty of health, defence, and a margin for error that leaves time to understand the game. If you have never played Ragnarok, or you are coming back after fifteen years, this is a reasonable pick.

It levels slower than a Mage, it kills one monster at a time, but it does not go back to town every ten minutes.

Where it leads: **Knight** or **Crusader**. The Knight is the famous perpetual machine, endlessly chained Pierce that makes it one of the best zeny engines in the game. The Crusader reflects damage and protects its party, so it becomes precious in a group.

### Acolyte: the worst solo, the most wanted in a party

Let us be honest: levelling an Acolyte alone is a chore. Your damage is anecdotal for a long time and you spend your time healing yourself to compensate.

But an Acolyte finds a party in thirty seconds, at any hour, on any map. And a party levels far faster than a solo.

Where it leads: **Priest** or **Monk**. The Priest is the party's engine, keeping everyone's SP up and making compositions possible that would not exist without it. The Monk goes the opposite way and lands the single hardest hit in the game with Asura.

The Monk, it must be said, is also one of the most satisfying classes here. Charging for several seconds to erase a target in one blow is a pleasure that does not wear out.

## The XP route, tier by tier

Here is the itinerary. There is nothing exotic about it, which is exactly why it works.

**Levels 1 to 20: quests, full stop.** Do not grind. Chain the main quest and the mission board. You will gain faster and you will finish equipped. A progression wall waits for many players around 20, and it is cleared by returning to quests rather than hunting for a better map.

**Levels 20 to 30: Prontera Culvert, second floor.** Thief Bugs, Female Thief Bugs, Tarous and Spores, high density, monsters that do not hurt, easy access. This is the map that trained entire generations and it has not aged. Mages take Fire Bolt to level 10 there in the same run.

As an outdoor alternative, the Sograt Desert with Mukas does the job perfectly.

**Levels 30 to 40: Labyrinth Forest** for Hornets and Floras, or Poison Spores for anyone preferring a quieter map. Flora maps are the safest auto-hunt ground for a Mage up to level 40, because they do not kill it while you are away.

Second quest wall around level 30. Same remedy as the first.

For anyone who prefers grinding by hand, Parasite packs offer better density than the maps above, provided you can handle a monster group arriving all at once.

**Levels 40 to 50: Orc Dungeon.** This is the right place and there is not much debate. The spawn rate is high, monsters fall quickly in groups, and the XP follows. If you play a fragile class, take a party or stay near the entrance.

Outdoors, Geffen Field 10 with Orc Warriors, Orc Ladies and Orc Babies makes an excellent middle ground. Sograt 17 with Hodes, Sandmen and Frilldoras suits ranged classes.

**One remark that changes the maths**: experience in Zero is not scaled to level the way Renewal does it. Continuing to hit low-level monsters in quantity stays profitable, which is not true on modern servers. If a map suits you, there is no urgency to change.

## Auto-hunting, and how not to let it ruin the game for you

The game includes automated hunting. You configure it, it runs, it farms.

It is useful and I am not going to pretend otherwise. Three uses are legitimate:

Farming boring materials you need, like consumables and components. Running a secondary character while you play your main. And smoothing out downtime when you want zeny without giving an evening to it.

But two warnings, and I would rather be clear.

**Auto-hunting crushes the market.** It dumps enormous quantities of materials, so the price of anything farmable mechanically collapses. On this server, wealth does not come from how much you pick up, it comes from what people do not think to pick up. Look at crafting consumables, at stems for potions, at alcohol for acid bottles. That is what will sell when the endgame parties come looking for resources.

**And auto-hunting eats the game.** Ragnarok was never a good combat game. It was always a good people game. Letting a script farm in your place removes the only part that made the boredom bearable, namely the conversation in the corner of the screen while you hit the same Flora for the four hundredth time.

My advice, for what it is worth: keep auto-hunting for what bores you, and do by hand what brought you back.

## Refining, without getting fleeced

This is where the server goes for the wallet, so it is worth knowing first.

The shop sells **Enriched Oridecon and Elunium**, which raise the success rate, and **HD Oridecon and HD Elunium**, which prevent the item being destroyed above +7 by making it simply lose a level instead.

What to know before reaching for the card: **Blacksmith Blessing is found in the game**. It drops from MVP raid quests, it can be bought on the market with zeny, and it guarantees your item will not be destroyed. Do your daily instances, sell what drops, buy your blessings with the proceeds.

Practical translation: **paid refining is not a necessity, it is a shortcut**. On the Asian Zero servers everyone walks around in full +9 gear without the shop being involved.

And a reminder that holds for your entire career: never refine an item whose loss would make you quit. Refining is gambling, and it is played with what you can afford to lose.

## Three mistakes that cost a week

**Picking your class for level 1 instead of level 70.** The Merchant is limp at the start and dominant at the finish. The Acolyte is painful solo and king in a party. Look at where you want to end up.

**Refusing parties.** This game is calibrated for the collective. An Acolyte following you doubles your levelling speed, and party experience sharing remains the most effective accelerator in the game, ahead of any shop item.

**Sprinting for the cap.** The job level cap is 50 and it is reached in a few days. What will occupy you afterwards is not your level, it is your gear, your cards and your zeny. Farm monster cards while you level rather than after: they are what will decide what your character is worth in September.

## And if you still do not know

Make a Merchant. Seriously.

It is not the glamorous answer, but it is the one that leaves every door open, funds your other characters, handles auto-hunting better than the rest, and leads to two equally excellent second jobs.

You will want a Mage or an Assassin afterwards. You will be glad you had the Merchant first.
