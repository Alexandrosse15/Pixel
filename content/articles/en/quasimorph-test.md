Some games take you by the hand. Quasimorph takes you by the collar, throws you into a space corridor infested with horrors, and coldly watches your mercenary get torn apart before shrugging: print another one. This cyberpunk tactical roguelike is of rare brutality and demand, and that is precisely what makes it fascinating for those willing to pay the price.

![Quasimorph, cosmic horror and turn-based tactical combat](/images/quasimorph/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Quasimorph is developed by Magnum Scriptum and published by HypeTrain Digital, available on PC via Steam and verified playable on Steam Deck. It is a turn-based tactical roguelike that blends cyberpunk, cosmic body horror and private military companies trying to profit from the Apocalypse. You play the leader of a group of mercenaries, sending your people to complete corporate contracts in the depths of space. The reception is Very Positive, with 85 percent favourable reviews, a deserved recognition for a title as harsh as it is ambitious.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is a merciless extraction loop grafted onto a turn-based tactical combat of remarkable depth. You accept a contract, infiltrate a mission, loot everything you can, and must get out alive with your haul. The rule is cruel but clear: everything you extract, you keep to strengthen your military company; but if you die, you lose everything you brought. The only, chilling consolation is that you can always print a new mercenary and retry the contract. That disposable-clone mechanic gives the game its identity and its permanent tension.

What sets Quasimorph apart is the richness of its combat system and the unique atmosphere it deploys. Every turn-based clash is a tense tactical problem where positioning, resource management and weapon choice decide life or death. The cyberpunk layer of cosmic horror, with its transforming bodies and its owned graphic violence, sets an oppressive and fascinating mood, worlds away from tamer roguelikes. You sense a game that is not afraid to disturb, that pushes its subject and its cruelty far, and that generously rewards those who accept its logic.

![Quasimorph, the extraction loop and managing your military company](/images/quasimorph/screenshot-2.webp)

But you have to be clear-eyed about what the game demands. Quasimorph is brutally difficult and punishing, and its learning curve is steep. Losing your hard-earned gear is part of the deal, and that cruelty, which is the zest for some, will discourage others. The game makes no effort to spare you, and its interface as well as its systems demand real investment before revealing their depth. It is not a game for everyone, and it does not try to be.

It is also a title that owns its status as a demanding niche. Its grimy cyberpunk art direction and its graphic violence will not please everyone, and its methodical pace contrasts with the sharp action many roguelikes have accustomed us to. But for the fan of turn-based tactics looking for an uncompromising challenge and a singular atmosphere, Quasimorph offers a depth and identity cruelly missing from many smoother rivals.

## The tech

Technically, Quasimorph bets on a grimy, oppressive art direction perfectly in tune with its cyberpunk horror world. The tactical legibility, crucial in a turn-based game where every decision counts, is there, and the visual atmosphere powerfully contributes to immersion in this desperate future. The Steam Deck verification guarantees good accessibility for playing on the go, a real asset for a game you like to restart in sessions.

The technical reservations join the gameplay ones: the interface and the game's many systems demand patience and a considerable learning time, and the game does little to smooth that abrupt entry. But once the hurdle is cleared, the depth rewards the effort, and the whole runs cleanly in service of its ambition. Quasimorph clearly prioritised systemic richness and atmosphere over accessibility, and it is a choice coherent with its identity.

![Quasimorph, the grimy cyberpunk atmosphere and its owned violence](/images/quasimorph/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Quasimorph is a tactical roguelike of remarkable richness and personality, carried by a merciless extraction loop, a deep turn-based combat and a cyberpunk cosmic-horror atmosphere unique in its genre. Its disposable-clone mechanic, its owned cruelty and its systemic depth make it a fascinating and addictive experience for those willing to pay the price. The 85 percent positive reviews rightly salute a title that fears nothing.

Its limits are inseparable from its identity: a brutal difficulty, a steep learning curve and a graphic violence that narrow its audience to fans of uncompromising challenges. It is not a game for everyone, but in its register, that of the demanding, singular tactical roguelike, Quasimorph is a success worth the detour. To reserve for those who love it when a game forgives them nothing and rewards their tenacity.

**A cyberpunk tactical roguelike of fascinating cruelty, reserved for those who love dying a lot to win big.**

Score: 8/10
