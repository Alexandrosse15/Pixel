We've managed a trading card shop, a kebab place, a fast food, a corner store, a wargame shop. By now we know the ritual by heart: restock the shelves, ring up sales, set prices, expand. So when Mall Together shows up promising to hand us not a stall but a whole shopping mall, we want to believe. Finally thinking big. The problem is that once the scale effect fades, you find the same mechanic as in ten other games, and its graphics, though far from ugly, are starting to seriously smell of deja-vu.

![Mall Together, first-person management of an entire shopping mall](/images/malltogether/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Mall Together is a first-person simulation game developed and published by Business Tycoon, available on PC since 24 July 2026. You take the role of a shopping mall owner, alone or with friends, tasked with turning a modest arcade into a huge complex. In practice, you open new stores, sign contracts with big brands, hire teammates and meet customers' needs. Co-op mode welcomes up to four players, who can split the duties, or you manage everything yourself solo. The demo convinced with 96% positive reviews, proof of genuine know-how in an otherwise saturated genre.

## The gameplay: the same loop, at a bigger scale

What sets Mall Together apart from the horde of shop sims is its change of scale. You no longer manage a single business, but a whole set: several stores to open, brands to negotiate, an arcade to bring to life and grow. That dimension adds an extra layer of management, that of overall layout, complementary stores and upscaling the mall, where classic sims stay confined to a counter. You go from local shopkeeper to developer, and that shift offers a wider horizon, a builder's satisfaction the more modest iterations of the genre lacked.

The other asset is cooperation. Splitting a whole mall four ways, one at the registers, another on logistics, a third on layout, gives the concept a real social flavor solo never reaches. That's where Mall Together best justifies its existence: in the slightly silly, joyful pleasure of running a shared machine, shouting instructions and watching the complex grow under combined effort. The core loop stays the one you know, restock, sell, expand, but scale and multiplayer give it new breath, provided you have friends on hand to fully enjoy it.

![Mall Together, opening stores and signing contracts with brands](/images/malltogether/screenshot-2.webp)

## What sticks

Let's be honest: under its change of scale, Mall Together doesn't invent much. The fundamental mechanic stays the thousand-times-seen one of the first-person shop sim, with its cycle of restocking, ringing up and expanding. Managing several businesses rather than one adds surface, not necessarily depth, and you can quickly feel like you're repeating the same gestures in greater number. Whoever has already worn out evenings on the countless variations of the genre risks going in circles once the mall's novelty fades, for lack of truly new mechanics to renew the interest.

The other reservation is the genre's aesthetic wear. Mall Together's graphics aren't bad, far from it, but they reuse that smooth, functional art direction found in absolutely every shop sim around, to the point they start blending into one another. You feel you've already walked these aisles, passed these generic customers, clicked these same interfaces. It's not a flaw specific to the game, it's the fatigue of an overexploited formula, and Mall Together, despite its qualities, doesn't have the arguments to truly break out of it. It does well what the others do, without doing it differently.

![Mall Together, co-op mode up to four players to run the complex](/images/malltogether/screenshot-3.webp)

## What we take away

Mall Together is a competent, likeable management sim that has the merit of thinking bigger than its cousins. By handing us a whole mall rather than a single store, it adds a layer of overall layout and upscaling that enriches the classic loop, and its four-player co-op gives it a real social flavor, that of the joyful pleasure of running a shared machine. As long as you love the genre and have friends on hand, there's plenty here for good times, carried by know-how the 96% demo had already hinted at.

You just have to accept it doesn't bring much fundamentally new. Under its change of scale, you find the thousand-times-seen mechanic of the shop sim, and its clean but generic graphics betray the fatigue of an overexploited formula. It's a good student of a genre starting to go in circles, pleasant but skippable for whoever has already put in the hours. To reserve for co-op management fans who want to think big, rather than those hoping for a reinvention. The mall is wider, but the stroll has a taste of already-done.

## Verdict

A mall-management sim that thinks big and shines in co-op, but recycles the thousand-times-seen shop-sim formula without real novelty: competent and likeable, but skippable.

**Strengths:**

- The change of scale, from single shop to whole mall
- A four-player co-op mode full of social flavor
- A layer of overall management that enriches the classic loop
- Genuine know-how, hailed since the demo

**Weaknesses:**

- A core mechanic identical to countless shop sims
- Clean but generic graphics, marked by the genre's fatigue
- Repetition lurking once the scale effect fades
