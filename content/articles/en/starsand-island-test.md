*Correction, 18 August 2026. This article stated the game was available in French. That is wrong, and a reader pointed it out: Starsand Island offers only English, Japanese and Chinese. The information has been corrected.*

There is a category of game you cannot fault for being what it is. Starsand Island is cute, simple, easy and presents no particular difficulty. That is not a flaw: it is the advertised product, and you would have to argue in bad faith to hold it against a game for keeping its promise.

It moves to version 1.0 today, 18 August 2026, after six months in early access. So this is the ideal moment to say what it brings against a particularly crowded field, and what it does not.

![Starsand Island, the farm and the island seen from above](/images/starsandisland/screenshot-1.webp)

## What it does better than its rivals

Three things, and they are real.

**Customisation.** This is its strongest argument, and the people who have poured hundreds of hours in always come back to it. Build mode lets you construct your house from scratch, Sims-style, with a volume of furniture and clothing options few games in the genre offer. For anyone who likes decorating, it is a generous playground.

**Density of objectives.** The game never leaves you without a list. Daily quests, a bulletin board, an isladopedia to fill, plots to buy, fish and bugs to collect. If your pleasure is ticking boxes, the loop is efficient and it catches.

**The art direction.** It is frankly a success, and it is what sells the game. The island is pretty, colourful and welcoming.

Add two plus points its rivals do not always have: an official **modkit** released in June, which hands longevity to the community, and a simultaneous release on Nintendo Switch 2.

## What it does less well, and it is structural

Here we need precision, because this is where the comparison with Stardew Valley, Coral Island or the My Time series is decided.

**There is no story, and the studio owns it.** This is not missing content to be added later, it is a choice. The premise fits in a sentence: you have had enough of city life, you return to the island of your childhood and inherit a run-down shack. Nobody needs saving, there is no grand narrative and no antagonist.

For part of the audience, that is perfect. For the other part it is a deal-breaker, and better known before paying thirty-nine euros.

**The content runs dry fast.** This is the most documented complaint from long-term players, and it is specific: five professions to raise through five levels, each level opening new materials, crops, animals and crafts. The problem is that all of it can be finished in two in-game months. The comparison doing the rounds is cruel and apt: imagine reaching the end of Stardew Valley during summer of year one.

There is still plenty left to do afterwards, fishing, catching bugs, filling the isladopedia, saving for new plots. But you move from a game that progresses to a game that occupies.

**Relationships are flat.** Characters stop talking to you once the introductory quests are done, and you cannot get to know them any better. The update devoted to romance was judged lazy by part of the community, closer to a one-minute chat with a friend than an actual courtship, and the absence of voice acting sharpens that sense of emptiness.

That is precisely the reverse of what Stardew Valley and My Time at Sandrock get right, where the residents are the reason to come back.

## The review affair, which you should know about

The game shows a Very Positive reception, with 4,691 positive reviews out of 5,648, around 83 percent. That figure comes with a warning, and it is not rumour: the story was covered by Kotaku, PC Gamer, GamesRadar and several others.

In spring, a flood of extremely positive reviews appeared on the game's page. The characteristics were troubling: accounts posting after less than an hour of play, a writing style strongly resembling automatically generated text, a mass simultaneous arrival, and some accounts requesting refunds right after posting.

Studio **Seed Sparkle Lab** responded publicly on its Discord asking whoever was behind the messages to stop, explaining it had no concrete evidence but felt someone was doing it deliberately.

Let us be scrupulous here: nothing establishes that the studio is behind it, and it says the opposite. But the practical result for you is the same: **this game's Steam score cannot be read like any other title's**, and you are better served by long reviews from hundred-hour players than by the overall percentage.

Two further complaints circulated and are verifiable. The first concerns paid add-ons during early access: the store lists several, including one devoted to European furniture priced at 19.50 euros, half the base game's price, which angered part of the crowdfunding backers. The second concerns the studio presenting itself as a small independent team when documentation links it to the Seasun group, a Kingsoft subsidiary.

![Starsand Island, island life and its residents](/images/starsandisland/screenshot-3.webp)

## What sets the score

It rewards a game that keeps its promise exactly: a pleasant island, a farming, crafting and collecting loop that works, generous customisation, and an art direction that makes you want to move in. Anyone after something comfortable and frictionless will get their money's worth, and a hundred hours is possible for people who like ticking lists.

It marks down a world and characters without depth, progression that reaches its end far too early for the genre, and a thirty-nine euro price hard to defend against more complete and cheaper rivals.

It also accounts for a commercial context inviting caution: a cosmetic add-on at half the price of the game, and a Steam score over which a documented doubt hangs.

One thing to know before buying: **there is no French translation**. The game offers English, Japanese and Chinese, simplified and traditional, and nothing else.

Practical advice: if your library already holds Stardew Valley, Coral Island and My Time at Sandrock, this one will not add much. If what you mainly want is to decorate a house on a pretty island without being asked to make an effort, it does the job.
