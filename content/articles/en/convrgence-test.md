CONVRGENCE's best argument fits in one line of its store page: **no floating windows, no laser pointers, all interfaces are diegetic**.

For anybody who has spent time in virtual reality, that sentence is worth more than a trailer. It means the game has you handling objects rather than menus.

![CONVRGENCE, the anomaly zone](/images/convrgence/screenshot-1.webp)

## The setting

At the turn of the 21st century, an explosion rocks a clandestine laboratory in Chernokamensk. A massive energy release turns the city into an exclusion zone, periodically lit by an eerie glow. The government walls the region off.

Documents leak, in which one word recurs: convergence. Nobody knows what the zone really hides. But rumours of treasure and artefacts draw people from across the country.

If that premise reminds you of something, it should: the game comes from the creator of Paradox of Hope, and it openly cultivates that heritage.

## The loop

This is a **single-player extraction shooter**, and it follows the rules.

You prepare your raid: choose and maintain your firearms, stock up on ammunition and supplies. The game warns bluntly that a sloppy plan is paid for with a foolish death.

You scavenge, you survive, you exfiltrate. And the penalty is clear: **on death, all uninsured equipment is lost**.

Then you sell what you found, artefacts included, and reinvest in better equipment, a more interesting gun or a bigger backpack.

Locations are procedurally generated, so every expedition has its own geography.

## The dog

This is the game's find, and it is better than it sounds.

You have a **four-legged companion** that helps not only in battle but also in scavenging for supplies.

In virtual reality that counts for more than in a conventional game. An animal companion you see at hand height, that you can follow with your eyes, with whom you share a physical space, creates a bond no interface replaces. And in a genre you play alone, raid after raid, having somebody beside you changes the mood of an expedition.

![CONVRGENCE, equipment and weapons](/images/convrgence/screenshot-3.webp)

## What 1.0 brings

The game has been in early access since April 2024, and shows **1,377 positive reviews out of 1,557**, roughly 88 per cent.

Three practical facts to know before launching the update.

**There will be no general wipe.** Your gear, weapons, hideout and vehicle upgrades, and all accumulated money, are kept.

**But your mission progress resets.** The main quests have been significantly reworked, so mission progress and unlocked reputations are reset. That is the trade-off, and it is clearly announced.

**And 1.0's major addition is a full story**, tying together the game's events and finally letting you reach the heart of the Chernokamensk anomaly zone.

The studio has also prepared exclusive rewards for early access players, including a knife skin reserved for veterans.

## What to know before buying

The decisive point: **this is a VR-only game**. No flat-screen mode, no compromise. If you do not own a headset, this game does not exist for you.

At 24.99 euros, from a studio called Monkey-With-a-Bomb that sustained two and a half years of support, it is an honest proposition in a sector where plenty of VR projects are abandoned along the way.

Accessibility options are plentiful, with seated mode, left-handed mode and multiple movement and turning styles, which is essential in a genre where motion sickness eliminates half the candidates.
