Phantom Blade Zero's most useful review runs to eleven words.

**"Guys, the trailer was real. It's not fake mobile game ads."**

Five hundred and seventeen people found it helpful. And it perfectly captures the climate of suspicion this game arrived in.

![Phantom Blade Zero, a fight](/images/phantombladezero/screenshot-1.webp)

## Where the suspicion came from

Phantom Blade Zero was first shown during a PlayStation presentation, and it provoked exactly the reaction you would expect: it is too good-looking, it will never run like that, and nobody has heard of this studio.

S-GAME is Chinese, it had never shipped anything on this scale, and the industry has grown used to technical showcases that do not survive into the finished product.

One player puts it bluntly in his review: **another Chinese studio came out of nowhere and made a banger game.**

## The story

Framed for murdering his master, Soul suffers a devastating wound to the heart, leaving him just **sixty-six days to live**.

Alone and hunted, he roams the Wulin, determined to unravel a conspiracy that has ensnared the martial world. Through rain and blood, kindness and vengeance, he embarks on a journey where love and hatred intertwine in search of a heart.

The Wulin, for anyone unfamiliar with the term, is the martial arts world of Chinese literature: a parallel universe with its own schools, codes of honour and rivalries, coexisting with ordinary society without really touching it.

Choosing that setting over the genre's usual gothic kingdom already sets it apart from eighty per cent of the field.

And the sixty-six day countdown is an excellent narrative idea: it gives urgency to a genre that normally lets you die and restart indefinitely without the story caring.

## The combat, and this is the argument

This is what everybody talks about, and the reviews converge with rare unanimity.

A player with twenty hours writes that he does not know where to begin: the combat is top tier, the fluidity, the animations, the impact, the variety, and the sheer satisfaction of pulling off a perfect sequence.

Another notes a detail that says a lot about the care taken: **there are unique animations when you parry certain boss attacks**. That is not a mechanic, that is animator work almost nobody will consciously notice and everybody will feel.

The game is described as an action-adventure crossed with hack and slash rather than a pure soulslike, and that distinction matters. One player explains at length that he never really took to soulslikes and their five repeated moves, and that this one speaks to him precisely because it offers a wider combat vocabulary.

![Phantom Blade Zero, the Phantom World](/images/phantombladezero/screenshot-2.webp)

## The Phantom World

Exploration is not a corridor between bosses.

You travel the mysterious Phantom World, where shifting light and shadow conceal mysteries at every turn. The studio lists them: Memory Flowers, the Wulin and the Discordance, Transcension, the Lady of the Veil, Inanis' Dreamscape.

With hidden passages and intricate mechanisms for those who dare to explore.

That is a promise to verify over time, but the announced structure is that of the good action games: a world that rewards curiosity rather than a sequence of rooms.

## The technical side, and the pleasant surprise

This is the point I was waiting to catch the game on, and it does better than expected.

One player writes that **the game runs way better than he expected**. Another lands a jab I find excellent: the lion does not concern himself with the twenty-five minute shader compilation that infects every Unreal Engine 5 game on the market.

Translation: yes, there is a long shader compilation on first launch, as there is everywhere right now. No, that is not what will spoil your experience.

![Phantom Blade Zero, exploration](/images/phantombladezero/screenshot-3.webp)

## What the numbers say

**2,514 reviews, 87 per cent positive**, rated very positive. At 59.99 euros.

For a first game of this scale, from a studio nobody was expecting, that is a clear success. And the shape of the reception is healthy: not launch enthusiasm that collapses, but detailed reviews from people with twenty hours logged.

The remaining thirteen per cent mostly concern the genre's usual subjects: difficulty spikes, and whether the length justifies the price. Nothing resembling the technical wrecks we have seen this autumn.

![Phantom Blade Zero, Soul](/images/phantombladezero/screenshot-4.webp)

## What I take from it

There is a lesson in this launch, and it goes beyond the game.

For two years, the argument against Phantom Blade Zero was that it was too good to be true. That argument was reasonable: we have all watched vertical slices turn into unrecognisable products.

The studio answered in the only way that works, by shipping the game. And the first review buyers see today is somebody simply saying the trailer was real.

At sixty euros, for an action game with combat at this level, in a setting the genre almost never uses, and a narrative countdown that gives the story weight, it is an easy recommendation.
