There is an idea in Rivage I have not seen anywhere else, and it deserves knowing even though the rest struggles: **codes and passwords are randomised for every playthrough**.

In other words, you can read every solution in the world and you will still have to solve the puzzles. For a puzzle game in 2026 that is an elegant answer to a problem nobody dared attack.

A shame the rest of the launch is what it is.

![Rivage, the A.R.E.S. station](/images/rivage/screenshot-1.webp)

## The setting

In the 25th century humanity has expanded beyond its solar system. VESTA is discovered, a planet that might become a new home, and the A.R.E.S. station is launched into orbit to study it.

Then a space phenomenon catches the station and its crew.

You play Miranda, who wakes alone on board, trapped in that phenomenon. Clues from the past echo through the place: an object out of place, a door newly unlocked, a message left behind, almost as if the station itself were challenging you.

It is a very good basis, and the station is **fully interactable**, with artefacts to examine, research data to analyse and hidden notes scattered across abandoned workstations. Each crew member had their own goals, fears and philosophies, and only fragments remain.

## What works

The convinced players are very clear about what they like.

**The puzzles hold up.** Hard enough to make you search, not hard enough to make you quit. Several players praise sound puzzles and creative ideas you do not meet elsewhere.

**The aesthetic succeeds.** Even negative reviews acknowledge the game is beautiful and the atmosphere works.

**And the time loop is appreciated.** You restart, but you learn a little more each time, which gives a sense of progress even while going back.

Expect five to six hours, which is consistent with the price for this kind of game.

![Rivage, a puzzle on board](/images/rivage/screenshot-3.webp)

## What is wrong, and it is specific

The game shows **35 positive reviews out of 62**, placing it at a mixed reception. And the complaints are almost never about the game itself.

**The image.** That is grievance number one, and it recurs in nearly every negative review: a blurry or grainy render players cannot correct whatever the settings. One describes spending over an hour in menus without succeeding.

**A resolution bug.** Several players report the render percentage resetting on its own after being raised to 100.

**Missing options.** No dropdown in the settings menu, common options absent, and no AMD upscaling.

**And no colour-blind mode**, while the game leans heavily on colour-based minigames. That is the most serious complaint in my view, because it is not comfort but accessibility: some players simply cannot play certain sequences.

On substance, one recurring complaint too: some puzzles do not feel organic. A player sums it up by asking why somebody would circle batteries they want put away instead of simply putting the batteries away.

## What I make of it

At 22.49 euros, published by Raw Fury, this game had everything needed to be a fine discovery. It has ideas, it looks good, and its randomised codes are a genuine find.

But you do not review an intention, you review what runs on the machine, and right now a share of buyers cannot get a sharp image.

So my recommendation is clear: **wait for a patch**. The studio visibly has work to do on its graphics options, and those are exactly the things fixed within weeks.

And if you are colour-blind, wait until a dedicated mode is announced before buying.
