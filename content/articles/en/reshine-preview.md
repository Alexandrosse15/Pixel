There is a sentence in Quite OK Games' latest announcement that I would love to see more often: "There won't be any shop management, customers, skill trees, or meta-progression systems. We don't want to turn Reshine into a management game or a complex simulator."

At a time when every relaxing game ends up making you run a business, that is almost a manifesto.

![A gramophone on the attic workbench, and the catalogue of objects to restore](/images/reshine/screenshot-2.webp)

Reshine, out on 26 November, is an antique restoration game. You are in an attic, it is raining outside, and you take apart a hurricane lamp, a gramophone, an old telephone, piece by piece. Then you clean off the dust, sand down the rough spots, remove the rust, pick a colour and repaint. You put it all back together, and the game shows you the object before and after.

There is no way to make a mistake, no timer, and you can redo a restoration as many times as you like to try other colours. The studio estimates the full game at 8 to 10 hours.

![A freshly repainted turquoise hurricane lamp](/images/reshine/screenshot-3.webp)

The demo was among the 50 most played of the last Steam Next Fest, out of more than 7,000 games, with more than 400 reviews and 97% positive. Feedback has already been folded in, down to the details: accidentally clicking a fully painted part no longer resets its colour.

![A disassembled metal mechanism, its screws and red parts scattered](/images/reshine/screenshot-4.webp)

The price is 8.99 euros, with 20% off at launch. For ten hours or so of total calm, that is fair.
