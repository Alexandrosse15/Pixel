Tactical card games are a genre where you always end up comparing numbers. Panzer Deck has you comparing shapes.

![Panzer Deck, the battlefield](/images/panzerdeck/screenshot-1.webp)

## The idea

In Panzer Deck, cards are more than stats: they are **geometric patterns** that let you manage resources, recruit units and amplify their power.

And the recruitment system is directly tied to those patterns. To call up a new unit, say the legendary Sherman tank, you have to **find resources on the map arranged to match the unique pattern on the card**.

In other words you do not pay a cost, you spot a configuration. The studio phrases it well: only a strategist's keen eye will spot these geometric correlations amid the chaos of the front.

It is a puzzle mechanic grafted onto a war game, and the graft holds because both ask the same thing: see the board differently from your opponent.

## What surrounds it

The rest is an honest, well-built tactics game.

You manage **ammunition and fuel** to recruit and reposition your forces for maximum impact, every resource fuelling your war machine.

You adapt your tactics to a dynamic map, and use the patterns to instantly boost your army's firepower.

Every unit has strengths and weaknesses to exploit ruthlessly, and terrain counts, as do the Operation cards that amplify your troops at decisive moments.

Between encounters you expand your deck to select precisely the set of tools suited to the next challenge.

![Panzer Deck, the card patterns](/images/panzerdeck/screenshot-3.webp)

## What it is worth

The game launched yesterday at 9.59 euros, developed and published by Smash Wall, and shows **19 positive reviews out of 21**.

The sample is tiny, I will not pretend otherwise, but the proposition is clear and the price is consistent.

The accessibility options deserve flagging for a studio this size: adjustable text size, contrast settings, adjustable difficulty, fully playable with a mouse.

My reservation concerns legibility over time. A game built on pattern recognition is tiring on the eyes, and the risk is ending up playing mechanically, hunting the shape without reading the battle. That is exactly the trap the Operation cards and the terrain have to avoid.

But for under ten euros, an idea this sharp in a genre this crowded is worth a look.
