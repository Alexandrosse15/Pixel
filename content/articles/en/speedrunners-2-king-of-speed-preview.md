There are games that ruined friendships with a missile in the back at the final corner. SpeedRunners was one of them, a wildly sharp 2D racing party game that became a cult hit in the 2010s. Its sequel, SpeedRunners 2: King of Speed, arrives on 3 September, and let us say it right away: we cannot sit still, as much as we dread it betraying its predecessor's perfect formula.

![SpeedRunners 2 King of Speed, the sharp competitive multiplayer racing](/images/speedrunners2/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

SpeedRunners 2: King of Speed is the sequel to one of the most loved indie games of the previous decade, a competitive platformer where the goal is simple and cruel: outrun your rivals until you push them off the screen. The game is expected on 3 September 2026 on Nintendo Switch, PS5, Xbox Series and PC, with day-one availability in Xbox Game Pass on PC and consoles. A demo is already playable to tide you over. The promise is clear: take the formula that made the first a success and amplify it, without distorting it.

## What we know

The premise stays faithful to the original, and that is reassuring. You run, jump and outsmart your rivals in online or local races for up to eight players, the goal being to get enough of a lead to eject the others out of the frame. Mastering movement is at the heart of the experience: you have to learn meticulously designed maps, exploit the pick-ups and swing on the grappling hook to shave off every millisecond of lead. It is that demand for virtuosity, that high skill ceiling, that gave the first all its depth, and the return of the grappling hook as a central tool is an excellent sign.

The arsenal promises matches as chaotic as they are strategic. You find pick-ups like missiles, traps, freezing rays and a golden grappling hook capable of pulling an opponent off-screen, all tools to sow mayhem and turn a race around in an instant. The announced content goes beyond simple racing: tournaments, custom challenges, leaderboards to climb and private lobbies to host, enough to structure the competition and sustain the replayability that gave the first its longevity. On paper, everything that gave SpeedRunners its zest is there, bigger.

![SpeedRunners 2 King of Speed, the grappling hook and pick-ups to eject your rivals](/images/speedrunners2/screenshot-2.webp)

## What worries us

The main fear, with the sequel to such a precisely calibrated game, is precisely tampering with the original's perfect balance. SpeedRunners drew its magic from a millimetric dosing between speed, control and chaos, and the slightest misalignment, less inspired maps, poorly balanced pick-ups, less precise movement, could be enough to break the alchemy. A sequel has to add without weighing down, and it is a balancing act few pull off. We are waiting to see whether King of Speed enriches the formula or settles for repeating it with a more recent coat of paint.

The other point of caution, inherent to this type of game, is the health of its online community. A competitive party game lives and dies on the number of active players and the vitality of its matchmaking. However good the game, if it struggles to gather people or to offer smooth online play at launch, part of its appeal collapses. The day-one presence in Game Pass is a major asset in that respect, as it guarantees an influx of players, but the infrastructure will have to keep up to convert that chance.

## Are we waiting for it or not?

SpeedRunners 2: King of Speed ticks all the boxes of the sequel we hoped for: the sharp, demanding formula of the original, a jubilant arsenal of pick-ups, the return of the grappling hook, an eight-player race and beefed-up competitive content with tournaments and leaderboards. For anyone who screamed with rage and laughter at the first, the anticipation is legitimate and the enthusiasm hard to contain. The available demo already gives a first impression, and the easy access through Game Pass promises a large community at launch.

But we keep in mind the fragility of such a precise balance, and the genre's dependence on an active online community. Everything will hinge on the sequel's ability to amplify the original's magic without betraying it, and on the solidity of its online play, two unknowns no product sheet can lift before release. So we are waiting for 3 September, controller in hand and friendships on the line, to decide. One thing is certain: if King of Speed recaptures even half the sharpness of its predecessor, evenings with friends will once again end in score-settling. See you at launch.
