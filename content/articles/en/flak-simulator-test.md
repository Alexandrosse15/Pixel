The first shell bursts behind the formation. The second, too early. The third, at last, among the bombers, and an engine starts smoking. Between each shot, someone had to call the range, two others had to correct elevation and traverse, a fourth had to set the fuze and the last had to load the next shell. That is Flak Simulator: one gun, and five people learning to serve it together.

![The crew around the 88 mm gun, in a sandbagged pit](/images/flaksimulator/screenshot-1.webp)

Horseman Interactive's game puts you in charge of the German 88 mm anti-aircraft gun of the Second World War, in first person. Everything is manual: the rangefinder to track aircraft, the traverse and elevation wheels, the time fuze setting that decides the burst altitude, carrying ammunition from the supply point, the breech. You can give both aiming axes to one player or split them between two.

Its appeal lies entirely in communication. A shell bursting behind the formation is a clue. An early burst is too. You correct, try again, and after a few waves the crew finds a rhythm where everyone performs their part without waiting to be told. That moment is very satisfying, and fairly rare in co-op games.

![A shell bursting in the sky above the gun](/images/flaksimulator/screenshot-2.webp)

Launch content is thin, it is Early Access and you should know it: two locations and three scenarios. In Germany, by day and night (with searchlights to track aircraft), you defend the battery with the 88 and the lighter 2 cm gun against bomber formations and low-flying attackers. In North Africa, tanks join the air raids. A convoy escort mission, with a 2 cm gun mounted on an Opel Blitz truck, and the 40 mm Bofors are announced for later.

For those without four friends on hand, AI crew fill empty positions, and an Arcade mode gives each player their own gun with an automatic crew, for an individual score. It is more accessible, but it loses what gives the game its soul.

![A crewman carrying ammunition crates to the gun](/images/flaksimulator/screenshot-4.webp)

The game tells nothing of the war itself: no story, no context beyond uniforms and locations. It is a game of ballistics and teamwork, and it owns that very narrow frame.

An evening for five around the 88 is worth it. For solid longevity, you will have to wait for Early Access to fill out.
