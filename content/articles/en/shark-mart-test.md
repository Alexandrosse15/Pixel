You have to see a whale come into the shop. Huge and slow, it settles in among the shelves, and you grab your sponge to polish its belly and fins until they shine. It is absurd, it is cute, and it is the key to the game: whale loyalty gives you access to the small fry. The ones you are going to kidnap.

Shark Mart, as we explained in [our preview](/articles/shark-mart-preview), starts from an irresistible pitch. You and your friends are sharks disguised as dolphins, and your underwater convenience store is a front for a very particular little catering business in the basement.

![A whale being polished in the middle of the shop](/images/sharkmart/screenshot-8.webp)

The game alternates between two registers. By day, it is a fairly standard shop sim: stock shelves with sponges and water bottles, ring up sales, clean, rearrange the shop in build mode. Then you kidnap a customer, take them down to the kitchen, and have to wipe the traces before the fish police come asking questions. The interrogations, where you have to lie without showing your teeth, are the best moments.

In co-op, it is genuinely funny. There is always one friend who panics, another who left evidence lying around, and the visual humour (dolphins that are not dolphins, the orca doing its shopping) works very well.

![An orca customer in front of the shop's Open sign](/images/sharkmart/screenshot-5.webp)

The limit is the shop side. It is necessary for the joke, but it stays repetitive and shallow compared with the sims it borrows from. Solo, with nobody to laugh with, the loop runs out of steam fairly quickly.

At 11.49 euros (9.19 euros for the first two weeks), it is a great evening game with friends, less so something you keep playing alone for hours.

Your best alibi for the fish police belongs on the [InsertCoins Discord](https://discord.gg/473FE3dWvw). We are taking notes.
