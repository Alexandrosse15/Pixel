We approach every new cozy retail sim with the same polite weariness. Another shop, more shelves to fill, another till to jingle, and that soft aesthetic we've seen a thousand times. So we expected to shrug at Bookshop Simulator. Then we adopted a cat, stocked a shelf, rang up a customer, and looked up three hours later wondering what had just happened. Sometimes the magic hangs on little things, and often, it has whiskers.

![Bookshop Simulator, its warm bookshop to arrange and grow](/images/bookshopsimulator/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Bookshop Simulator is a cozy management game developed and published by Blep Games, available in early access since September 2025 on PC, with a full version planned for 2026. The principle is the genre's: you open your bookshop, order books, stock your shelves, ring up customers and reinvest your profits to decorate, expand and hire. It's a direct cousin of the supermarket sims and other shops swarming Steam, and you could file it away in that category without much interest. That would be a mistake, because it does a few little things that change everything. And its 95% positive reviews, across more than a thousand responses, are not stolen.

## The gameplay: the loop we know, but polished

Let's not kid ourselves, the heart of Bookshop Simulator is the classic management loop we've practiced elsewhere. You buy stock, set the layout, serve customers at the till, restock on the fly, and each sale earns experience for your character and your shop alike. On that front, the game reinvents nothing, and it doesn't pretend otherwise. But it executes that formula with a care and warmth that make all the difference. The progression is gentle, rewarding, and the simple act of organizing your bookshop, watching it grow and beautify, gives that hypnotic, comforting pleasure you come to a cozy game for.

Where the game truly stands out from the mass is in its little ideas that give it a soul. The rare books you collect grant powerful abilities, which adds an unexpected layer of progression and strategy to what could have been a mere shelf-sorter. And above all, the writing has fun: the book titles are riddled with puns on real works, and that constant mischief gives the game a personality and a permanent smile that its competitors sorely lack. It's the kind of detail that turns a simulated chore into a real pleasure, and that proves there are people who love books behind this project.

![Bookshop Simulator, the rare books with powerful abilities and the pun-filled titles](/images/bookshopsimulator/screenshot-2.webp)

## The cat, and the rest

We have to devote a paragraph to the cat, because it deserves it. You can adopt a feline that keeps the player and the customers company, and that simple presence says a lot about the game's philosophy: here, you don't seek cold optimization, you seek warmth, the cocoon, the pleasant little moment. All of Bookshop Simulator lies in that intention, that of creating a comforting space where you enjoy spending time with no pressure. Decoration, expansion, hiring staff, everything works toward that feeling of building your little literary corner of paradise, at your own pace.

Still, you have to be lucid about its status and its limits. It's an early access, with all that implies of content set to grow and mechanics still improvable. And despite its good ideas, it remains fundamentally a retail management game, with the repetitiveness inherent to the genre: past the enchantment of the first hours, the loop stays a loop, and not everyone will find enough there to last dozens of hours. Its depth is real but measured, and you should approach it for what it is, a gentle parenthesis rather than a management abyss.

## What we take away

Bookshop Simulator is reassuring proof that a saturated genre can still birth little successes, provided you put your heart into it. Where so many retail sims settle for spitting out a soulless formula, this one dresses it in warmth, mischief and well-observed ideas, from the rare books that grant powers to the puns on titles, not forgetting that cat that sums it all up. It's a sincere, polished and endearing cozy game that understands you come not for a challenge but for a refuge. Its 95% positive reviews are amply deserved.

You just have to approach it for what it is, with its genre limits and its early-access status. It's not a revolution, the management loop stays classic and its depth will quickly find its ceiling for the most demanding. But for anyone seeking a gentle game where shelving books becomes strangely satisfying, where a cat purrs beside the till and where every title cracks a smile, it's a comforting little gem. You go back to it without even realizing, and that's the finest thing you can say about a cozy game.

## Verdict

A cozy bookshop sim that transcends a saturated genre through its warmth, its puns and its power-granting rare books: not a revolution, but a sincere and endearing refuge.

**Strengths:**

- A classic management loop but executed with care and warmth
- The rare books that grant abilities, a clever layer of progression
- Writing full of puns on real titles, personality guaranteed
- A cat to adopt that sums up all the game's gentleness

**Weaknesses:**

- An early access with content still set to grow
- The repetitiveness inherent to retail management games
- A real depth but one that quickly finds its ceiling
