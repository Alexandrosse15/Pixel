The story is true, and most people know some version of it: in 2013, a Welsh IT worker, James Howells, accidentally threw away a hard drive holding 8,000 bitcoins, now buried in a Newport landfill. Lost Bitcoin runs with that fantasy: somewhere beneath a gigantic scrapyard lies your USB stick, and you have to find it.

![An industrial furnace swallowing scrap, flames and all](/images/lostbitcoin/screenshot-1.webp)

The loop is a first-person incremental. You pick up scrap, carry it to the furnace, throw it in, and every object pays out coins. The deeper you dig, the more it pays. More than 120 upgrades speed things up: vacuum up a whole tank of scrap, then fire black holes that swallow mountains of junk, all the way to building a colossus that devours the scrapyard.

On the bridge above the pit, you also build a factory: cranes, conveyors and sorting switches turn scrap into rubber ducks, piggy banks or arcade machines, earning money while you dig. USB sticks are everywhere and all look alike, and only the platform computer tells you what each one holds. Along the way you find safes, gold items and even an entire buried sailboat.

![A pit of scrap being sucked into a black hole](/images/lostbitcoin/screenshot-3.webp)

The game plays solo or with up to four online, and all its music is free for content creators to use. Its sole developer, Velto Games, states that generative tools were used for almost all content, which they say justifies a lower price. Out on 4 December, demo available.

Whoever finds the right USB stick first gets bragging rights on the [InsertCoins Discord](https://discord.gg/473FE3dWvw).
