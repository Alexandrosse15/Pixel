WARDOGS looks like an ordinary military shooter for ten minutes, and then you realise it is an economy game in disguise.

This guide covers what the game does not explain: where the money comes from, what to do with it, which roles actually pay, and how to avoid the two traps that ruin beginners. Our [full review is here](/en/articles/wardogs-test) if you are still deciding.

![WARDOGS, a vehicle racing for the zone](/images/wardogs/screenshot-3.webp)

## First, the win condition

Everything flows from it, and plenty of players aim at the wrong target for their first few hours.

**This is not a game you win by killing.** The side with **the most players inside the control zone** scores points. First to one hundred points takes the match.

Three immediate consequences.

A dead player inside the zone is worth more than a live one three hundred metres away. Your physical presence is the resource.

The sniper perched on a ridge, however effective, does not score for his side. He stops the enemy scoring, which is useful but secondary.

And since there are three sides, whoever leads becomes the target of the other two. Being second at the midpoint is often the best position.

## The economy, in one page

You start with **ten thousand dollars**. Each life, you buy a complete loadout: weapon, gear, utility, vehicles.

And the crucial point: **money persists from match to match**. What you do not spend today is available tomorrow.

So every death carrying expensive gear is a straight loss. That is not a symbolic punishment, it is a budget that will be missing later.

**What pays**, according to the studio itself: reviving a teammate, transporting allies to the zone, controlling the objective. In other words, everything a conventional shooter never rewards.

**And the hot zone pays double.** That is the game's risk lever: you go in to double your earnings knowing you might leave expensive gear behind.

## The golden rule of your first hours

I will be blunt, because it is the most common mistake.

**Do not buy expensive during your first five matches.**

The natural reflex is to spend your starting ten thousand on the best rifle available. That is precisely the best way to be broke after three matches, playing with default gear while everybody else drives around in vehicles.

Do the opposite. Light loadout, objective held, teammates revived, runs to the zone. You earn money by playing small, and **only once your balance is comfortable** does a heavy purchase become an investment rather than a gamble.

The game rewards financial patience as much as aim.

## The four roles that pay

You have no imposed class. You have profitable habits.

### The driver

Probably the game's most underrated role. Transporting allies to the zone is paid, and a vehicle running regular rotations earns continuously without ever firing a shot.

Two tips. Never park your vehicle conspicuously near the zone, it becomes a disguised spawn point for the enemy as much as a coffin for you. And keep it alive: a destroyed vehicle takes your income and often your team's spawn point with it.

### The medic

Reviving pays, and reviving a lot pays a lot. In a game where dying costs your teammates money, the player who picks them up quickly becomes somebody worth protecting.

Get into the habit of following the squad ten metres back rather than leading the charge. You will see the people you need to pick up go down.

### The builder

Fortifying a chokepoint or raising a forward base changes a match. It is also the most contested role in the community, with several players finding these structures slow the pace.

So my advice is measured: build useful rather than big. A position covering one approach beats a fortress that ties up three players for ten minutes.

### The sniper

The ghillie suit and long range are a legitimate choice, but remember the win condition. Hold a position that **stops the enemy entering the zone**, not a picturesque hill eight hundred metres from the subject.

## The two beginner traps

### The respawn trap

This is the community's number one complaint and you should know about it before playing.

When your side loses ground, you can end up able to respawn only at base or on a single vehicle, which gets camped the moment it is spotted. Players describe entire matches stuck at home.

Three habits limit the problem. Never let your team drop to a single forward spawn. If your spawn vehicle is spotted, move it rather than defend it. And if your side is locked in, break out through the quietest flank, not the main axis where the enemy is waiting.

Another useful point players flag: do not count on parachuting to reach contact. Direct drops onto the zone get noticed and get punished.

### The budget trap

This one is sneakier. You are earning well, you buy a heavy vehicle, you die in three minutes, and you are back where you started feeling like you played badly.

The remedy is accounting: decide before the match how much you are willing to lose. If your purchase is more than half your balance, it is not equipment, it is a bet.

## Reading a match properly

**Look at where the zone is before choosing your gear.** An urban zone, a zone in open ground and a zone on high ground need neither the same weapons nor the same vehicles.

**Watch whoever is leading.** With three sides, balance takes care of itself: when one side approaches a hundred points, the other two become objective allies. Know how to use that, and know how not to be the one who leads too early.

**And accept long matches.** A hundred points takes as long as it takes. Do not start a match with twenty minutes to spare.

## The technical side

A few points players have flagged since launch, worth knowing before you complain.

On release day, servers were unreachable for several hours. If you hit a loading screen that never ends, particularly in the tutorial, it is probably not your machine.

Optimisation remains imperfect and controller support is judged rough by players using it. If you have the choice, play mouse and keyboard for now.

And the reporting system is aggressive: one player picked up a chat restriction after spam reports. Avoid heating up chat with strangers.

## The summary, in order

Play small and hold the zone for five matches. Revive everything that falls. Buy a vehicle when your balance genuinely allows it, and run rotations. Build useful rather than large. And remember that victory is counted in presence inside a circle, not in kills.
