There is a category of games you launch to decompress and close with a smile, without having saved the world or beaten a single boss. Leafy Corner is a fine example: you run a small plant shop, advise customers, watch your shoots grow, and that is all, and it is already plenty. For ten euros, it is a concentrate of gentleness perfectly calibrated.

![Leafy Corner, the cozy plant shop to grow](/images/leafycorner/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Leafy Corner is developed by Fireline Games, a small studio founded by two former Fueled Up developers, a couple both in life and at work, and it is available on PC via Steam as well as on consoles at 9.99 euros. It is a cozy, casual management game where you run an adorable plant shop. The demo, released a few months before the game, had already won people over with 97 percent positive reviews, a strong signal for a production of this size. The full game amply confirms that good impression.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is the passionate shopkeeper's loop: grow, care, sell. You cultivate and care for a wide variety of real plants, help customers find the one that suits them, fulfil orders and customise your shop as it expands. This simple loop is solid and rewarding: every plant sold to the right customer, every order fulfilled, every new corner of the shop arranged delivers the small regular satisfaction that gives the cozy genre its zest. With more than a hundred plants to discover and a shop to expand, the progression holds up over time without ever forcing the pace.

What sets Leafy Corner apart from many lazy cozy games is the care given to its theme. The plants are real species, which adds a touch of authenticity and even tickles the player's curiosity, learning along the way to tell what likes light from what prefers shade. Advising a customer is as much a mini knowledge challenge as an act of commerce, and this slightly educational dimension enriches a formula that could have settled for the minimum. The game respects the player's time: no artificial pressure, no predatory systems, just a gentle invitation to grow your little patch of green.

![Leafy Corner, over a hundred real plants to cultivate and advise on](/images/leafycorner/screenshot-2.webp)

The limits are the known and owned ones of the cozy genre. The difficulty is almost nonexistent, which is coherent with the game's spirit but will leave challenge-seekers completely wanting. The soothing loop can also reveal a certain repetitiveness once the discovery effect fades, especially for those not already won over by the principle of contemplative management. It is not a game that will hold you for hundreds of hours, but a pleasure to savour in small sessions, which it fully owns.

The game's modest format is both its strength and its limit. At ten euros, Leafy Corner does not overpromise and delivers exactly what it announces, without artificially inflating its content. That is honest and refreshing, but it also bounds the experience to what it is: an excellent little relaxation game, not a sprawling production. Knowing what you came for is the key to fully enjoying it.

## The tech

Visually, Leafy Corner is a treat of gentleness. The cozy art direction fully owns its register, the plants are detailed and appealing, and the little shop's atmosphere radiates warmth and calm. It is precisely that kind of careful dressing that makes the difference between an endearing cozy game and an empty shell, and the studio executes it with genuine love of detail.

The technical reservations are minor and on the scale of the project. The management interface stays clear and pleasant, the whole runs without a hitch on PC as on Steam Deck, and nothing disturbs the sought-after serenity. A few extra side systems to extend the variety would have been welcome, but the reassured polish of the whole is missing from many lazier cozy productions, and that is to this small studio's credit.

![Leafy Corner, the warm atmosphere of the shop to customise](/images/leafycorner/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Leafy Corner is a fine success in its register, carried by genuine care and an evident love of the subject. Its gentle management loop, its more than a hundred real plants and its slightly educational dimension make it a cozy sim richer than it looks, all served by a warm art direction and a careful finish. For ten euros, it is a concentrate of relaxation that perfectly fulfils its contract.

Its limits, an almost absent difficulty and a certain repetitiveness, flow directly from its cozy identity and only weigh if you expected something else. For anyone looking for a soothing, well-made and sincere game to savour in small sessions, Leafy Corner deserves a spot on the shelf. We launched it to catch our breath, went back several evenings in a row, and ended up wanting to buy a real plant.

**A small but perfectly executed cozy sim, as soothing as it is sincerely crafted.**

Score: 7.5/10
