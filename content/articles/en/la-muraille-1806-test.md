"The generation that thought itself the most enlightened turned out to be the least prepared for the apocalypse."

1806. The Europe of the Enlightenment, of great empires and gunpowder. And then the countless hordes of Gog and Magog, straight out of the Bible, burst from the earth and overwhelm the continent. The Pope calls on every nation to forget its quarrels and unite. You command the Wall, the last bastion of free humanity.

That pitch alone makes La Muraille 1806 worth a stop.

![A black tide of enemies crashes against the bastions of the wall](/images/lamuraille1806/screenshot-3.webp)

This is not a classic tower defense. Your defenses are regiments of historical infantry that will not give an inch: French infantry, Scottish musketeers and the armies of all Europe under your command, backed by devastating artillery. Against them, hordes that carry no banners and pitch no camps: a continuous torrent pouring onto the wall.

The sight of those dark tides breaking against star forts under volleys of musket fire is the game's real achievement. Dozens of modifiers change the rules from one run to the next, and a light story follows the defenders between battles: a young officer, a nun, the Pope himself.

![The hordes pour through a breach in the snowy wall](/images/lamuraille1806/screenshot-5.webp)

The execution is more modest than the idea. Tactical depth stays limited, runs start to look alike once you find the right placements, and the story stays light, as the studio itself says. The developer also states that the art, music and translations were made with AI assistance, then reworked by hand, and you can feel it in a presentation that lacks personality.

The first ten reviews are 80% positive. At 8.72 euros, it is an original little defense game for anyone who finds the Napoleon meets Apocalypse mix as amusing as I do.
