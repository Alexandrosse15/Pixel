There is something reassuring in Car Park Capital's Steam reviews: almost everybody who gets stuck gets stuck in the same place, for the same reason.

They do not understand where the money comes from.

That is not an intelligence problem, it is a tutorial problem. It stops before the interesting part. Here is what it should have said.

![Car Park Capital, the town from above](/images/carparkcapital/screenshot-2.webp)

## The opening mistake: thinking the car park is your income

This is the misreading that costs the most, and it is perfectly logical when you arrive in a game called Car Park Capital.

You build a car park. You set a fee. You watch the coins drop. And twenty minutes later you notice it will never be enough to buy a city block.

That is normal. **Parking revenue is not your economic engine, it is your thermometer.**

Reread the pitch: you work for Big Auto, and your mission is to drive up car sales and oil consumption. The car park is not the product. The car park is the infrastructure that makes the car indispensable, and it is the car that pays.

As long as you think like a car park operator, you are playing the wrong game.

## The loop to understand

It runs in four beats, each feeding the next.

**1. You make the car necessary.** A district where parking is easy becomes a district people drive to. A district where the alternatives disappear becomes a district people have to drive to.

**2. People buy cars.** That is where your money is, not in the meter.

**3. Cars consume fuel.** Hence the oil derricks, which are not a decorative gag but a second revenue stream bolted onto the first.

**4. More cars means more spaces needed.** And you start again, bigger.

**The practical rule that follows: whenever you are torn between two expenses, ask which one puts more cars on the road.** Not which one adds more spaces.

## Fuel, and why it stays low

This is the most frequent complaint about the first scenario: fuel is constantly short whatever you do.

The reading to have is this: if your consumption permanently exceeds your production, that is not a problem to solve by consuming less, it is a signal that your chain is unbalanced upstream.

**Invest in extraction before expanding your estate.** One more car park with no fuel behind it is a car park creating demand you cannot meet, pushing your indicators down rather than up.

That is counter-intuitive, because every management player's instinct is to build the visible thing that pays. Here, the invisible thing gates the visible one.

## Read minds, genuinely

The game lets you click any resident and learn what they think: whether they have embraced the car, or whether they are annoyed by your fees and your derricks.

**Most players treat this as a gag. It is your dashboard.**

Concretely, use it to settle two questions the game displays nowhere else.

**How high can fees go?** Raise them, then read three or four people in that district. While irritation is not dominant, you have headroom. The moment it appears, you have hit the local ceiling, and the ceiling is not the same everywhere.

**Where to build next?** An already converted district has less growth potential than one still resisting. The thoughts tell you where the resistance is, and therefore where the margin is.

![Car Park Capital, a multi-storey car park](/images/carparkcapital/screenshot-4.webp)

## Propaganda is not optional

The game gives you glossy pro-car propaganda to spread. A lot of players treat it as a comfort expense, something for when you have spare cash.

It is the opposite. In a game whose loop rests on converting residents, **the tool that converts is the most profitable tool**, because it acts on step 1 of the loop, the one feeding all the others.

Spending on propaganda before you have car parks to fill is premature. Spending on propaganda the moment your car parks are not filling completely is exactly the right time.

## Protesters, and how to stop suffering them

Anti-petrol protesters set fire to cars, and several players describe the same frustration: tow trucks are slow, response vehicles are a pain to dispatch and recall, and you spend the scenario putting out fires.

Two habits to build.

**The first is to treat the cause.** Protesters react to what you do. A district saturated with derricks and high fees mechanically produces opposition. If you are spending your time managing fires, you pushed too hard too fast on one area.

**The second is not to deploy in a panic.** Response vehicles are slow to send and slow to recall, so dispatching at the last moment always costs you more than already having one in the sensitive zone. Position in advance, do not react afterwards.

## The three first-hour mistakes

**1. Expanding horizontally.** The game offers three-dimensional construction tools, so multi-storey car parks. A storey costs money, a plot costs a purchase and a demolition. Per square metre of town spent, vertical is almost always more profitable, and it annoys the neighbours less.

**2. Demolishing too early.** Buying land and flattening a house is the most expensive action in the game. Do it when you know exactly what goes there and what it earns. Not to expand "a bit".

**3. Doing everything in real time.** You cannot build while paused, which is a real and irritating limitation. The workaround is to make your purchases and expensive decisions in the quiet moments, just after solving a problem, not while a line of protesters is arriving.

## The summary in six lines

1. The car park is not your income, car sales and oil are.
2. Before every expense, ask whether it puts more cars on the road.
3. Raise extraction before expanding the estate, or fuel stays on the floor.
4. Residents' thoughts are your real dashboard, for fees and for choosing districts.
5. Spend on propaganda early, it acts on the first step of the loop.
6. Build upwards before buying land.
