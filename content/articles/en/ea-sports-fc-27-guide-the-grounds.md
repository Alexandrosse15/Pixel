The Grounds is available from launch and it replaces the menu with a place. This is where Clubs now lives, and it is where your Virtual Pro's progression gets built.

The problem is that the game drops you into it without telling you which of the three districts holds the thing you are after. Here is the map.

![EA SPORTS FC 27, a street pitch](/images/fc27/screenshot-3.webp)

## The three districts, and who hosts what

Each district is inspired by a football culture, and each hosts different activities. That is not only a matter of atmosphere: if you want 1v1, you need to know where to go.

### Montclair, French urban football

Cage pitches, concrete, street.

You will find **Rush**, **2v2**, **attacking scenarios** and **Bucket Ball** here.

This is the district densest in classic small-sided play. If you want to work on tight-space football, start here.

### Zeiza, Argentina

Architecture fusing European and Latin American, and the district of collective play.

You will find **3v3**, **Rush**, **Keep Away** and **Balloon Ball** here.

This is where team habits get settled. Keep Away in particular is not a gimmick, and we will come back to it.

### Parkside, the UK

Inspired by football's working-class origins, and the most varied district.

You will find **1v1**, **Bocce Ball**, **Goal Control**, the **Big Race**, and above all **access to the 11v11 stadium**.

Hold on to that last point: if you want real eleven-a-side football, Parkside is your entrance. It is the piece of information the game communicates worst.

## The activities explained, one by one

The game throws you in without posting the rules. Here they are.

**Bocce Ball (1v1).** You kick balls towards a target, with rings that award bonus scores. Pure weight of pass. Excellent for learning shot power, which pays off later in matches.

**Team Keepaway (3v3).** You protect the ball. You score points for possession and for passes. This is the single most directly useful exercise here, because it forces you to work on body orientation and passing choices under pressure, the two things that decide a Clubs match.

**The Big Race (four players).** A dribbling slalom through obstacles, first across wins. It is ridiculous and it is very good for learning tight close control.

**Attacking scenarios (2v2).** Challenges to complete, for instance volleys with no goalkeepers. This is the game's finishing drill.

**Bucket Ball (2v2).** You score by putting the ball through elevated baskets, worth 2 or 3 points depending on their position. Work on high trajectories and crossing.

**Balloon Ball (four players).** Everyone protects their balloon, last one standing wins. No tactical value whatsoever, and the best time you will have in the mode. Do not skip it.

**Goal Control (1v1).** There are eight goals on the pitch, positioned differently each round, and you have to control them. This is the game that punishes players who never look up.

**Drop Kick Targets.** Shooting challenges spread through the open world, with weekly objectives. This is your passive income: do them as you pass, they pay.

## Progression: Archetypes and Masteries

Here is the system to understand before wasting any time.

**Archetypes** are unlocked by default and freely resettable. You can switch whenever you like, at no cost.

**Archetype Masteries** grant **permanent** attribute upgrades, and those upgrades stay with you whatever archetype you play afterwards.

Read that sentence twice, because it contains the whole strategy of the mode.

**Consequence: do not specialise.** The natural instinct is to pick an archetype and push it as far as it goes. That is the wrong calculation. Since mastery gains are permanent and carry across, levelling several archetypes builds a more complete Pro than levelling one very high.

The method I recommend: level three archetypes in parallel, switching according to the activity. An attacking profile for the attacking scenarios, a technical profile for Keep Away, a defensive profile for 11v11. You bank masteries on all three, and your Pro gets better everywhere.

## Amps, and how not to waste them

**Amps** are **limited-time** items that boost attributes or add PlayStyles and perks. You equip **two Standard and one Signature** per match.

The important words are "limited time". An Amp you leave equipped through a Balloon Ball session is an Amp burned for nothing.

**Simple rule: save your Amps for competitive content.** Club Leagues, playoffs, live tournaments. For arcade activities, play bare. You do not care about the result and the item does not recharge.

Second rule: **one Signature is worth more than two Standard**, because PlayStyles change your behaviour, whereas an attribute bonus only shifts numbers. If you have to choose, save the Signature for the match that counts.

Worth noting, and this comes from the store itself: the Ultimate Edition supplies a tier 3 Amp inspired by a real player. In other words, there is a temporary advantage attached to the purchase price. I would rather you knew.

## Experience consumables

AXP consumables increase your levelling speed with multipliers, and they are **capped by your level** to help new players catch up.

Practical translation: they are far more effective early than late. **Use them at the lowest levels**, while the cap is not biting yet. Saving them for later is a mistake, they will be worth less.

The Ultimate Edition supplies two, each good for ten matches. If you have them, spend them in your first week.

## Club Objectives

They unlock cosmetic and customisation rewards. The Clubhouse updates according to your club's customisation, colours and badges included.

This is purely aesthetic, but it is where a group of friends builds an identity, and in a mode that lives on a group turning up regularly, aesthetics are not nothing. Choose your colours on the first evening, not a month in.

## A first-week plan

If you are starting today, here is the order I would follow.

**Day 1.** Do a full circuit of the three districts to place the pitches. Locate the stadium access in Parkside. Customise your club.

**Days 2 and 3.** Keep Away in Zeiza, on repeat. It is the activity that most improves your actual football, and it feeds your masteries while it does.

**Day 4.** Attacking scenarios in Montclair and Bocce Ball in Parkside. Finishing and weight of pass.

**Day 5.** Your first real 11v11 in Clubs, with your Amps equipped. You will see immediately what the previous four days changed.

**The rest of the week.** Drop Kick Targets as you pass, and rotate archetypes to bank masteries in all three directions.

And keep one evening for Balloon Ball. It serves absolutely no purpose, and it is the only thing in this edition that feels like free fun.
