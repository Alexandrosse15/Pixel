In Bio Inc. Redemption, the series that made DryGin Studios' name, you could choose to infect a patient to death. Bio Inc. Total Defense flips the roles. Your body is the map, and you are the one defending it.

![An organ up close, besieged by pathogens](/images/bioinctotaldefense/screenshot-2.webp)

It is an incremental tower defense with one twist that changes everything: there are no waves. From the start of a level, pathogens pour in through every entrance, non-stop. You begin with a few hundred enemies in a capillary and end up facing more than a hundred thousand across the whole body. You place defences in the vessels, protect the organs, and try to keep up.

There are eleven defences to unlock: white blood cells and macrophages that hunt invaders, tissue walls and mucus that slow the spread, fever spikes, acid glands and antibody cannons. Placement matters, and so does timing your septic shock, the last-resort ability that clears everything.

![A whole human body seen from above, criss-crossed by infections](/images/bioinctotaldefense/screenshot-1.webp)

The incremental side is generous: more than 150 upgrade nodes bought with Bio Points, Research Points earned with each win to unlock new defences, and 75 health factors to fine-tune your strategy (Chain Reaction, Last Stand, extra charges). Each run makes the next one easier, which is the whole joy of the genre.

The sight of tens of thousands of tiny glowing dots colliding in a network of vessels is very effective. The trade-off is readability: in the busiest levels, it is hard to see where things are breaking. And the early levels require some patience before the decisive upgrades arrive. The studio made the demo easier after feedback found it too hard, specifying that the full game keeps its own balance with every upgrade level available.

![The upgrade tree, a constellation of glowing nodes](/images/bioinctotaldefense/screenshot-4.webp)

DryGin says it uses automated tools for localisation and to rework or resize some visual assets, with the team reviewing the results. At 7.26 euros with the Black Friday discount, it is an excellent little game for fans of tower defense and climbing numbers.
