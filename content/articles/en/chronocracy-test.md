Niblets are small round creatures walking in single file across a planet seen side-on. Artem, Chronocracy's creator, says it all started with an old flash drive holding the prototype of a round world, where a few little guys wandered around and everything started lagging past ten creatures. The idea was to see whether they could build entire cities there. The result is a very cute real-time strategy game, and deeper than it looks.

![Chrono City, home of the Niblets, under an orange sky](/images/chronocracy/screenshot-2.webp)

You lead one of three tribes, starting with a town hall and a handful of followers. You build housing, secure food, recruit scholars to unlock technologies, and dig: the planet has a surface and depths, with mines to extract rare resources. More than twenty resources, more than fifteen buildings, and a progression running from bows and spears to firearms, attack helicopters and giant titans.

The world is not as peaceful as it looks. The adorable Fluffbirds have an explosive temper. Larvae, hostile neighbours and invaders from afar complete the picture. And the twist that gives the game its name is time travel: once mastered, it unlocks metamatter, used to build titans and defences, and lets you rewrite your tribe's fate. The creator sums up the intent: there is always a second chance.

![A giant titan facing a swarm of enemies against a red sky](/images/chronocracy/screenshot-3.webp)

The side-on view is original and readable, but it has its limits: as the city grows and fights intensify, it becomes hard to keep the big picture, and the interface is busy. The power curve, from spears to titans, also moves a bit fast to savour each era.

Regolite, published by HeroCraft, ran a playful launch campaign, presenting the game as a mystery from another timeline before "confessing" it was made by humans. The game shares that light tone. And it hides a few unlockable surprises, including a flying ship and a Soviet-style Khrushchyovka apartment block in the Niblets' city.

![The mines dug under the planet's surface](/images/chronocracy/screenshot-1.webp)

Fans of accessible strategy and endearing little civilisations will find plenty here.
