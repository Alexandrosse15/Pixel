The Backrooms have become a genre of their own, and let us be honest: most of these games consist of walking down a yellow corridor until something screams.

Code Yellow adds an idea that changes your relationship to the place: **you are being paid**.

![Backrooms: Code Yellow, the yellow hallways](/images/backroomscodeyellow/screenshot-1.webp)

## Work as a frame

The game plays one to six in online co-op, inside an infinitely massive, unpredictable realm of rooms, hallways and distorted architecture.

And the loop is presented as a working day: you explore together, you complete tasks, you collect data, and you return to **earn funding for the next day's work**.

That framing does more work than it looks. It turns fear into occupational risk, which is both funnier and more effective than a simple frightening stroll: you do not only run because you are scared, you run because you have data to bring back.

It is the same idea behind the recent success of co-op extraction games, applied to the most recognisable setting on the internet.

## And you are not alone

The studio writes it with appropriate restraint: do not get too comfortable, you may not be alone.

That is all we know, and it is probably deliberate.

![Backrooms: Code Yellow, exploring together](/images/backroomscodeyellow/screenshot-3.webp)

## What to know

The game launches today in early access, developed and published by GameMechanic, with no price displayed and no reviews as I write.

And I will be blunt about the score, because it is cautious.

**The genre is saturated and the bar is low.** A co-op Backrooms game comes out every three weeks, and the vast majority amount to a procedural corridor and a monster. To stand out, the tasks have to be genuinely interesting and the space legible despite its distortion.

**And the description is short.** Where this week's other releases detail their systems across three screens, this one fits in a few lines. That is not disqualifying, plenty of good games present themselves badly, but it leaves little to judge before playing at length.

What works in its favour is co-op up to six, which is more generous than the genre's norm, and the funding idea, which gives you a reason to come back.

Worth watching, then, rather than buying blind. And as always with early access, wait for early feedback if you are hesitating.
