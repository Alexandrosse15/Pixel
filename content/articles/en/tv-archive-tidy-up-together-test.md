I opened this game with a sigh. We had already done the wine cellar tidying simulator two days ago, I gave it a 6 while explaining that I do not even tidy my own miniatures, and I could not see myself following it with the same exercise using videotapes.

Then I saw the shelves. And I was eight years old.

![TV Archive Tidy Up Together, the aisles of tapes to file](/images/tvarchive/screenshot-1.webp)

## The video shop back home

I am a child of the video rental store. For those unlucky enough to be born too late, it was a shop full of cassettes, with a membership card you presented like a passport and a new releases wall you combed through every Saturday.

I am 36 this year, at least according to the calendar. Personally I am 24, and I maintain the calendar has a pacing problem it should get looked at.

What this game woke up is very precisely the sensation of hunting for your tape on the new releases wall. Jumanji. Bruce Almighty. That obsession with finding the case you wanted before another kid got to it, and the disappointment of the little red band telling you every copy was out.

And then there was the other aisle. The one at the back, with the pink neon, where I was not allowed to go. I never understood why the cover of the fourth instalment of a certain series looked so friendly, nor indeed why there was a fourth instalment. If anyone has the answer, I am still interested, and the Discord is open.

That is why this one spoke to me when the wine cellar left me cold. It is not the tidying, it is what you are tidying.

## The game, now

The task is simple and enormous: file 3,180 numbered tapes into the right categories. There are twenty of them, and the list is itself a trip: action, drama, TV series, cartoons, horror, comedy, athletics, football cups, romance, science fiction, baseball, tennis, news, game shows, basketball, motorsport, cooking shows, documentaries, boxing and talk shows.

Where studio Games Together genuinely separates itself from the competition is the skills. You unlock automatic numerical sorting of the tapes in your hand, highlighting of the correct shelf, highlighting of matching tapes, then outright automatic placement and automatic collection.

In other words, **the game progressively automates the work for you**. That is exactly what we criticised in Wild West Miner Simulator a few days ago: digging so you can dig faster, without ever reaching the moment where machines take over. Here that moment exists, and it is what turns a chore into optimisation.

Two modes round it out for the extremes: a sandbox where every skill is unlocked from the start, and a no-skill mode for those who want to suffer the old-fashioned way.

![TV Archive Tidy Up Together, the twenty categories and the skills](/images/tvarchive/screenshot-2.webp)

## The shelving duel, and the streamers

You are right about the audience: this is built for live broadcast. You can park your brain, talk to chat for two hours and remain perfectly competent. This is not Factorio, nobody is going to ask you to calculate a production ratio.

But the studio adds something the wine cellar did not have: online play, in co-op **and in competition**. Tidying an archive together is already a good idea. Fighting over the shelves to see who files fastest is genuinely funny, and it is the kind of format that exists to be streamed.

## What sets the score

This score comes from our own playthroughs, the game having just launched. It rewards the progressive automation, which gives a real curve to what could have been a mere task, and the online modes that pull the genre out of its usual solitude.

It accounts for the format's limit, the same one as the wine cellar: once the logic is understood, there is no second layer. The difference here is that the theme does half the work. A wall of VHS tapes is not a wall of bottles, it is loaded with things you actually lived.
