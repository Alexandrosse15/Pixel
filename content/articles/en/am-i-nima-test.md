The title is a palindrome. Am I Nima reads both ways, like the game, which never stops making you doubt what you think you know.

You are Nima. Or at least, you are someone who wakes up in the basement of the family home with no memories, locked in by a woman who says she is her mother. She makes you take tests. To get out, you have to convince her you really are her daughter. And to do that, you first have to relearn how to talk like her.

![A bedroom bathed in sickly green light](/images/aminima/screenshot-2.webp)

## A blank page, then words

The system at the heart of the game is brilliantly simple. You start with an empty mind. As you explore the rooms, you discover words tied to the objects around you: MOM, a worn plush toy, a scribbled note, BLOOD. These words pile up in a big dictionary, and you can combine them. Dad and Work give you a job. Two concepts give you a memory. Some combinations trigger visions, brief glimpses of the past that sometimes warp the room in front of you.

Then the words become answers. When the mother questions you, you pick from your vocabulary what Nima would have said. Get it wrong, and she notices.

It is a detective game dressed as a horror game, or the other way round. What impresses is that the system holds up: the dictionary grows enormously over the adventure without ever becoming unmanageable, and every new word genuinely opens something.

![The word notebook and a question from the mother: is there anything this reminds you of?](/images/aminima/screenshot-1.webp)

## Horror that sticks in your throat

Am I Nima does not rely on jump scares. Its art direction, flat blocks of saturated green and red, creates constant unease, and the game's broken, halting prose sets up an inner voice that cracks as it rebuilds itself. The questions pile up: why did this mother lock you up, what are these tests really for, and are you truly Nima?

Be aware that the game addresses, by implication, neglect of and violence towards a child, as well as eye mutilation. It is not for everyone.

![A drawing on a red sheet, and the hand that made it](/images/aminima/screenshot-3.webp)

HO! Games, a studio based in Brunei and funded by Outersloth (the fund run by the Among Us team), pushed the release from October to today. The game passed 275,000 wishlists before launch, and more than 1,700 people were playing a few hours in. Early reviews are unanimous.

One regret, shared by several players: there is no font size option, which hurts in a game with this much reading.

![A room striped with red walls, lit like an oven](/images/aminima/screenshot-4.webp)

At 15.74 euros, it is one of the strongest narrative experiences of the year.
