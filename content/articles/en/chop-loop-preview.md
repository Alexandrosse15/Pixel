Chop Loop was on this week's release list, but it has slipped to 16 December. No great loss: it is the kind of game you run during the holidays, in the background, with a cup of tea.

The concept fits in one sentence. You move your cursor over a small floating island, and every tree in the harvest zone takes damage. When they fall, they give you wood. Gold unlocks upgrades, and the little cabin gradually becomes an outsized logging operation.

![A floating island covered in mushrooms and falling trees](/images/choploop/screenshot-2.webp)

What sets it apart is the special trees. Some explode, others fire projectiles, others grant temporary buffs, and when well placed, they trigger chain reactions. Add a huge skill tree, eight rune slots to build around, and a colony of beavers working nonstop. An update added a quest tree, with an upgrade that turns the beavers into badgers, twice as fast and twice as strong. I have nothing to add.

A charged attack, by holding the mouse button, increases chopping radius and damage by up to 50%. It is the small active gesture many incrementals lack.

![The rune tree and a beaver at the foot of a magic tree](/images/choploop/screenshot-4.webp)

Lebleby Games states that generative tools were used for some sound effects. The demo is available in the meantime.
