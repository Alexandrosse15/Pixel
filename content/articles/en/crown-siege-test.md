The store page's first line sets expectations, and the studio deserves credit for it: **a short incremental siege game**.

Short. It is written down. Plenty of games in this genre prefer to talk about infinite content and let the player discover the truth six hours in.

![Crown Siege, defending the kingdom](/images/crownsiege/screenshot-1.webp)

## What you do

You spawn troops to protect your kingdom. You cut down goblins and other delights to earn gold. Every wave pushes your defences a little further.

You earn experience, level up your units, and claim crowns to unlock upgrades that strengthen your army and your tower.

Then you battle across a fantasy land, facing unique challenges, testing your build and conquering new realms.

It is the classic incremental loop applied to tower defence, and it is perfectly legible.

## What works

**It is immediate.** You understand everything in two minutes, you progress straight away, and the sense of growing power arrives quickly.

**It is one-handed.** The game is fully usable with a mouse, with a touch option, which makes it exactly the sort of thing you leave running in a corner of the screen.

**And the price matches the content**, at 8.49 euros.

![Crown Siege, army upgrades](/images/crownsiege/screenshot-3.webp)

## What limits it

The game shows **40 positive reviews out of 54**, roughly 74 per cent, which is decent without being exciting, and matches what the proposition can reasonably promise.

The genre's problem is well known and it applies: an incremental is judged on what it makes you want to do once the numbers turn absurd. A game announced as short has the merit of honesty, but it also inherits the consequence, which is that it ends.

If you want a curve to climb for weeks, this is not the candidate. If you want something to run for two or three evenings for the pleasure of watching a small army become enormous, that is precisely what is on offer.

A word finally on the presentation: it is cute, it is colourful, and it does not try to pass for something else. In an aisle where many games dress up as major productions to sell three mechanics, that modesty is welcome.
