The metroidvania is a genre we love as much as it wears us out, so much has it become an indie rite of passage. Every week brings its labyrinthine map, its abilities to unlock and its mandatory backtracking, to the point that you enter it already knowing the tune. Dewdrop Dynasty doesn't reinvent the formula, let's say it right away, but it plays it with enough snap, charm and personality that you quickly forget you've heard it a thousand times.

![Dewdrop Dynasty, the snappy metroidvania where you run, dash and crawl between dangers](/images/dewdropdynasty/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Dewdrop Dynasty is an action metroidvania developed by Firith Studio in collaboration with Goodgis, available since 21 July 2026 on PC. The pitch is simple and effective: you run, dash and crawl through an interwoven world, you face giant bosses, you dig up new weapons and abilities, you meet quirky characters, and above all you look for the way home. It's a fast, colorful metroidvania, carried by an endearing art direction, that fully owns its belonging to a well-trodden genre while seeking to stand out through rhythm and tone.

## The gameplay: speed and guns

What best sets Dewdrop Dynasty apart from the mass is its snappiness. Where many metroidvanias bet on calm exploration, this one runs on speed: you run, you dash, you crawl through tunnels, you chain moves with a fluidity that recalls an action platformer more than a contemplative stroll. That liveliness gives the game a delightful tempo, where crossing the map becomes a kinetic pleasure rather than a backtracking chore. Movement is the real hero, and it's well-felt enough to carry the whole.

Another good idea, the emphasis on weaponry. You dig up new firearms as you progress, which adds a layer of action and variety to the classic jump-and-sword formula. The giant bosses offer welcome intensity peaks, those spectacular clashes that pace a good metroidvania, and the gallery of quirky characters brings a touch of humor and freshness. All of it is wrapped in a colorful, character-filled art direction, the mark of Goodgis, that gives the game an immediately likeable identity. You feel a project made with love and a real sense of the pleasure of play.

![Dewdrop Dynasty, the giant bosses and firearms that spice up the metroidvania formula](/images/dewdropdynasty/screenshot-2.webp)

## What sticks: the genre's familiarity

You should stay honest, because the charm shouldn't mask the limits. Dewdrop Dynasty, despite its snap and personality, remains fundamentally a very classic metroidvania in its structure. You find the map to explore, the abilities that unlock new passages, the backtracking to comb once-inaccessible zones, in short all the DNA of a genre we know by heart. Whoever has done the rounds and seeks a real revolution won't find it here: the game excels at executing the formula with energy, not at reinventing it.

It's also a modest-sized independent production, and that means keeping measured expectations about its scale. The length, the richness of the map and the depth of the systems won't rival the genre's mastodons that defined the standard. It's not a flaw in itself, more an invitation to take it for what it is: a short, snappy and endearing metroidvania, rather than a sprawling epic. Approached with the right expectations, it amply keeps its promises; compared to the giants, it will inevitably show its small-budget limits.

![Dewdrop Dynasty, Goodgis' colorful, character-filled art direction](/images/dewdropdynasty/screenshot-3.webp)

## What we take away

Dewdrop Dynasty is an excellent representative of its genre for anyone who loves snappy, charming metroidvanias. Its movement speed, its emphasis on weaponry, its giant bosses and its endearing art direction make it a delightful experience, one that makes up for its structural familiarity with real personality and a constant pleasure of play. It's not a revolution, but it's an energetic, sincere execution of a formula we love, and sometimes that's exactly what we ask for. For the genre fan, it's a safe bet.

You just have to approach it with fair expectations. Dewdrop Dynasty doesn't reinvent the metroidvania and doesn't seek to rival the genre's monuments in scale: it's a charming, snappy independent production, to savor for its tempo and its tone rather than for outsized depth. Taken for what it is, a small, fast and endearing metroidvania made with heart, it fulfills its contract with a smile. We recommend it to anyone not yet tired of the genre, who appreciates a good dose of speed and personality.

## Verdict

A fast, colorful and endearing metroidvania that pulls out guns and faces giant bosses: not a revolution, but a snappy, charm-filled execution of a formula we love.

**Strengths:**

- Snappy movement, running, dashing and crawling, that carries the whole game
- The emphasis on weaponry that adds action and variety
- Giant bosses and a gallery of quirky characters
- A colorful, character-filled art direction

**Weaknesses:**

- A very classic metroidvania structure, with no real novelty
- A modest scale against the genre's monuments
- An independent production with small-budget limits
