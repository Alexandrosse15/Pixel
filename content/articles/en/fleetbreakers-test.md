The problem with real-time strategy is not the strategy. It is the quarter of an hour of setup before it starts.

Fleetbreakers begins from that observation and delivers exactly the promise we were waiting for: the decisions of an RTS, without the long build-up.

![Fleetbreakers, a space battle](/images/fleetbreakers/screenshot-1.webp)

## Who makes this game

Say it up front because it conditions the credibility of the promise: the studio claims the creators behind Halo Wars and Age of Empires, and it puts **Dave Pottinger** forward in its own communications.

For anybody the name means nothing to: he is an Ensemble Studios veteran, the studio that spent fifteen years trying to make RTS accessible without making it stupid. Halo Wars was already an answer to that question, on a controller.

Fleetbreakers is the 2026 version of the same problem, this time through the roguelite.

## How it works

You command from a mothership, but the battle is won through the squadrons you deploy.

You send agile fighters into contact, anchor the front line with heavier units, and use special manoeuvres to break enemy pressure. When it turns against you, mothership abilities protect your fleet, punish an enemy formation or open a path.

Between battles you spend resources, unlock new squadron types and combine upgrades that change your approach. A small strike force can become an armada if you make the right calls at the right time.

Every run generates its maps, encounters, discoveries, allies and branching routes. You push further for greater rewards, adjust your fleet against a new threat, and learn from every defeat.

![Fleetbreakers, squadron management](/images/fleetbreakers/screenshot-3.webp)

## What early access contains

The studio detailed its content three days before release, and it is fuller than average.

**A Story Mode**, a campaign against the Voltari Empire leading towards a conclusion planned for 1.0.

**Two playable factions**: the Cobalt Expedition, and the Slaraash Swarm to unlock, with different mechanics, ships, damage types and strategies.

**A Free Play mode**, with longer three-act runs where you have to beat three bosses in a row.

And over **60 missions** alongside over **40 non-combat encounters**, which is the detail that reassures me most: a roguelite offering nothing but battles always ends up wearing thin.

## What we do not know yet

The game launches today into early access, with no reviews, and the price was not displayed as I write.

The studio announced passing ten thousand wishlists a few days before launch. That is modest and healthy for a game this size, and it mostly means word of mouth will do the rest or it will not.

My reservation concerns the balance of the format. A shortened RTS has to stay legible without becoming simplistic, and that is a narrow ridge: too many options and you get back the complexity you were fleeing, too few and you end up with an autobattler with spaceships.

What works in its favour is that the people behind this project have spent their careers on exactly that question. If there is one team to give the benefit of the doubt on this precise point, it is this one.
