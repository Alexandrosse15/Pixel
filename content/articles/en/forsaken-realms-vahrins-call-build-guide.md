Forsaken Realms: Vahrin's Call's great strength, its fully classless progression, is also its biggest trap for beginners. That total freedom, inherited from The Elder Scrolls, can quickly turn to scatter: try to do everything, and you end up mediocre at everything. A good build here isn't about choosing a class, but about giving a coherent direction to a hero nothing constrains. This guide helps you exploit that freedom without getting lost, by understanding how to combine weapons, magic, alchemy and traps into a style that holds together. ## How does classless progression work?

Before building anything, you have to grasp the system's philosophy. Forsaken Realms locks you into no archetype: you're neither warrior, nor mage, nor thief by default, you become what you play. Your hero is shaped through five major intertwining levers: weapons, armor, abilities, alchemy ingredients and traps. It's by combining these tools that you define your style, and nothing stops you from being both a nimble archer and a fireball-slinger, or an axe warrior who lays traps.

That freedom is heady, but it demands a discipline: coherence. The classless trap is sprinkling everything without ever deepening anything, and ending up with an all-purpose hero who shines nowhere. Our fundamental advice: choose a main axis early, a way to deal damage and survive, then invest in it as a priority, only adding the other tools as complements. Freedom doesn't mean scatter, it means you choose your specialty instead of having it imposed. It's that frame you have to set yourself for the system to give its best.

## Which build styles should you favor?

The game lets you embody a multitude of profiles, but a few major styles stand out, each coherent and effective. The first is the archer-mage, that nimble-footed hero who keeps their distance, peppers with the bow and punctuates with fireballs: a mobile kiting style, ideal for whoever loves controlling space and avoiding blows. At the opposite, the melee warrior, axe or heavy weapon in hand, faces enemies head-on, bets on armor and raw power, and rewards whoever loves direct confrontation. Between the two, an infinity of hybrids is possible.

The real appeal of classless is precisely composing your own mix. A warrior can lean on alchemy to heal and strengthen, an archer can lay traps to control approaches, a mage can keep a backup weapon for melee. The key is to build around a clear identity, then add the tools that cover your weaknesses: mobility for the fragile, healing for the aggressive, control for the melee-vulnerable. Choose the style that fits your enjoyment, because in a long-haul narrative RPG, a build that entertains you beats a theoretically optimal but boring one.

## How do you combine weapons, magic, alchemy and traps?

It's in combining the five levers that the build's depth plays out. Weapons and armor form your base: they determine how you deal damage and take hits, and that's where you should start. Abilities enrich that base with offensive or utility options. But it's alchemy and traps that take a build from decent to formidable, because they're the tools players most often neglect.

Alchemy, through ingredients, lets you prepare heals, buffs and effects that can turn a hard fight: get into the habit of gathering ingredients while exploring and building a reserve before big encounters. Traps, for their part, add a precious tactical layer, especially for ranged styles: well placed, they control enemy approaches and turn an endured fight into a dictated one. Our advice: never treat alchemy and traps as afterthoughts. An archer who slows enemies with traps and strengthens through alchemy becomes far more powerful than the sum of their weapons. It's that synergy between the five levers that reveals the classless system's full potential.

## Forsaken Realms Vahrin's Call FAQ

**How does progression work in Forsaken Realms?**

It's fully classless: you shape your hero freely through your weapons, armor, abilities, alchemy ingredients and traps, without being locked into an archetype.

**Which build should you pick to start?**

Choose a clear axis, like the ranged archer-mage or the melee warrior, then invest in it as a priority before adding other tools as complements.

**Should you specialize or mix everything?**

Specialize first to avoid the all-purpose-hero trap, then add tools that cover your weaknesses. Coherence beats scatter.

**Are alchemy and traps important?**

Yes, they're the most neglected yet decisive levers: alchemy heals and strengthens, traps control enemies. Well used, they supercharge a build.

**Can you change style mid-game?**

Yes, classless freedom lets your hero evolve, but it's better to keep a coherent direction than to constantly reinvest everything.
