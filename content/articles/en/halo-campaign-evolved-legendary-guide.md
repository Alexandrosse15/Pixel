Halo's Legendary mode is a legend in itself, a rite of passage as cruel as it is rewarding. In Halo: Campaign Evolved, you have to face thirteen missions, the ten classics of Combat Evolved plus the three new Operation Meteorite levels, with shields twice as fragile and enemies twice as tough. This guide gathers the golden rules of survival and a level-by-level strategy to stop dying stupidly. It's not about brute force, but about playing smart. For our full opinion on the remake, head to the [Halo Campaign Evolved preview](/previews/halo-campaign-evolved-preview).

## How do you approach Legendary mode?

Before diving in mission by mission, a few golden rules that hold everywhere, and that you should carve in stone. On Legendary, your shields only withstand half the usual damage, while the enemies' are doubled: a few plasma shots drain you, and your standard weapons struggle to pierce enemy defenses. The direct consequence is that the plasma-pistol combo becomes your best friend. Drain an Elite's shield with an overcharged plasma pistol shot, then instantly switch to the Magnum to plant a bullet in their head. That gesture, to repeat hundreds of times, is the key to the whole campaign. The assault rifle, meanwhile, is nearly useless against shielded Elites, save it for the Flood and Grunts.

The other reflexes flow from the same principle: patience and smart positioning. Use cover constantly, shoot while exposing yourself as little as possible, and retreat the moment your shields flash to let them recharge. Always prioritize Elites, because killing the leader often makes the Grunts under their command flee. Grenades are vital: the plasma one to stick an Elite, the frag one in a group of Grunts, knowing the latter throw far more of them than on Normal. Rescue surviving marines for support, put them on the Warthog's gun, and never hesitate to hijack a Banshee or a Ghost to fly over or bypass a deadly passage. Finally, the melee attack from behind kills instantly: the stealth assassination is an underrated weapon.

## Operation Meteorite: the three prequel missions

New to the remake, Operation Meteorite takes place a year before Combat Evolved and follows Master Chief and Sergeant Johnson infiltrating a Covenant agricultural ship to find data leading to High Charity. These three boarding missions play out largely in tight corridors, an ideal environment for the plasma-pistol combo and stealth assassination. In these enclosed spaces, Elites come at you up close: always keep a charged plasma pistol, exploit doorway angles to avoid being surrounded, and let Johnson draw fire while you clean up with the Magnum.

Since this is new content, approach these levels with caution and method rather than blindly. Pick up Covenant weapons on the ground so you never run out of plasma, advance room by room securing each one before moving on, and use the nooks to recharge your shields between waves. A ship's corridors are a trap for the reckless but an advantage for the patient: they funnel enemies into chokepoints where your grenades and combo wreak havoc. Consider Operation Meteorite your Legendary warm-up, the chance to hone the plasma-pistol reflex before the main campaign's wide-open spaces.

## The early missions: from Pillar of Autumn to The Silent Cartographer

**The Pillar of Autumn.** The first classic mission serves as an introduction, but don't relax. Grab the Magnum as soon as possible, it's your queen weapon, and headshot the Grunts and Jackals to save ammo. The bridge Elites are handled with the plasma-pistol combo or a grenade. Toward the end, don't try to kill everything: sprint to the escape pod dodging pointless fights.

**Halo.** The first big exterior, with the Warthog. Free the marines to put them on the gun, they'll clear out the Grunts while you target the Elites with the combo. In the canyons and on the bridges, keep your distance and let the enemies come to you rather than charging in.

**Truth and Reconciliation.** A night mission where the sniper rifle is king: post up high and drop Elites with a headshot before they even spot you. The gravity-lift ascent is done through patient ranged elimination. Inside the ship, back to tight corridors and the plasma-pistol combo, watching out for active-camo Elites.

**The Silent Cartographer.** The island level, with a Warthog landing. This is where the Hunters appear: aim for their exposed orange back and torso, never their armor. Keep a rocket launcher or grenades for them and for tight groups. Secure the beach before pushing toward the Cartographer facility.

## The mid-campaign: from Assault on the Control Room to Two Betrayals

**Assault on the Control Room.** A long mission riddled with Elites and vehicles, on snowy bridges. Hijack a Banshee as soon as possible: it lets you fly over entire sections and avoid deadly fights. Ration your Magnum ammo, alternate with plasma picked up off the ground, and leave the Ghosts to the marines or hijack them.

**343 Guilty Spark.** The swamp, and above all the introduction of the Flood. A total change of strategy: the shotgun becomes essential against the Flood combat forms, at close range. Save its ammo carefully, and beware ambushes in enclosed spaces where the Flood surges from everywhere. The plasma-pistol combo loses its appeal here, make way for raw firepower.

**The Library.** The game's most dreaded mission, a marathon of Flood-infested corridors where you follow 343 Guilty Spark. The key is endurance and movement: never stand still, never let yourself be surrounded, and manage your shotgun and assault rifle ammo like treasure. Grenades clear the groups of infection forms. It's a test of nerves as much as skill: advance methodically, back to the wall when possible, and don't panic.

**Two Betrayals.** The reverse of Assault on the Control Room, with three-way battles between Covenant, Flood and Sentinels. Exploit these conflicts: let the factions kill each other and pick up the scraps. The Banshee remains your best ally for crossing the wide spaces and fleeing desperate situations.

## The home stretch: Keyes and The Maw

**Keyes.** Back aboard the Truth and Reconciliation, now Flood-infested, for intense three-way fights in tight corridors. The shotgun and plasma weapons are your best tools in these enclosed spaces where anything can overwhelm you. Advance slowly, secure each room, and exploit the clashes between Covenant and Flood to carve a path to Keyes.

**The Maw.** The grand finale, back on the infested Pillar of Autumn. The first part is a corridor fight, shotgun and plasma in hand, until the moment of truth: the famous Warthog run to escape before the explosion, timed and merciless. For this last stretch, forget combat and focus entirely on driving: anticipate the jumps, avoid explosions and obstacles, keep your speed and don't stop for anything. Clearing this run on Legendary means finishing the campaign and earning the right to breathe. You've earned it.

## Halo Campaign Evolved Legendary FAQ

**What is the best strategy on Legendary?**

The plasma-pistol combo: drain the enemy's shield with an overcharged plasma pistol shot, then finish with a Magnum bullet to the head. It's the key to the whole campaign.

**How many missions does Halo Campaign Evolved have?**

Thirteen in total: the three Operation Meteorite prequel missions plus the ten remastered Combat Evolved missions, from Pillar of Autumn to The Maw.

**Which is the hardest mission?**

The Library, a marathon of Flood-infested corridors that tests nerves as much as skill. Stay mobile, don't get surrounded and manage your ammo.

**Is the assault rifle useful on Legendary?**

Against shielded Elites, no, it's nearly useless. Save it for the Flood and Grunts, and rely on the plasma-pistol combo and the shotgun for the rest.

**Should you avoid some fights?**

Yes, notably in Pillar of Autumn and the final Warthog run of The Maw. Knowing when to flee or bypass a pointless fight, often by hijacking a vehicle, is as important as knowing how to fight.
