Decidedly, indie publishing has decided to send us running a book shop until we drop. After a first cozy game about a bookstore just a few days ago, here comes Bookbinding, second of its kind in a week, putting us back behind a counter binding volumes. You could roll your eyes at the repeat, except this one has an ace up its sleeve: under its cozy looks and soothing craft hides a distinctly darker plot, made of murder, secrets and tarot. The comfort blanket has claws, and that changes everything.

![Bookbinding, the binding workshop where you create custom covers](/images/bookbinding/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Bookbinding, subtitled Dark Cozy Sim, is a cozy narrative simulation developed by NDApolis and published by Caspianborn Publishing, available on PC since July 2026. You run a small book shop where you create custom covers for each customer, choosing designs, materials and details that reflect their personality and hidden desires. The game invites you to craft beautiful bindings, read between the lines of your customers' lives, and decide which secrets deserve to stay buried. Under its gentle aesthetic surfaces a murkier thread: a murder, and tarot cards able to twist fate itself. The demo, meanwhile, divided, with 67% positive reviews.

## The gameplay: the craft, and what it reveals

The heart of Bookbinding is its binding craft. You make custom covers, choose materials, patterns, details, in soothing manual work that will speak to lovers of the book-object and those fascinated by the symbolism of the volume itself. That cozy dimension, made of patient creation and care for detail, forms the game's reassuring surface, the one that recalls relaxing craft simulators. But each order is also a window onto a customer, their personality and buried desires, and that's where the game shifts toward something deeper than mere making.

Because under that craft plays out a genuine narrative plot, and that's what sets Bookbinding apart from the cozy wave it seems to emerge from. You read between the lines of your customers' lives, you decide which secrets to bury, and the shadow of a murder hangs over the whole, thickened by tarot that can bend fate. That tension between the gentleness of the binding and the darkness of the story gives the game its identity, that of a cozy game daring to unsettle, using its reassuring frame to better distill unease. It's an ambitious proposition, refusing to be just another soothing simulator, betting on its murky subtext to leave a mark.

![Bookbinding, reading customers' secrets and the tarot that twists fate](/images/bookbinding/screenshot-2.webp)

## What sticks

Let's be honest: the demo divided, with only 67% positive reviews, and that invites caution. That mixed score probably betrays thwarted expectations or improvable execution, and you should keep it in mind before diving in. A game marrying cozy and darkness walks a tightrope, and the slightest imbalance, a plot too diluted, a craft too repetitive, tension poorly dosed, can make the whole wobble. Bookbinding has real ambition, but ambition doesn't guarantee success, and the demo's mixed feedback reminds us the proposition is far from unanimous.

The other reservation is the fatigue of the setting. Second cozy book-shop game in a week, Bookbinding arrives in a context of saturation, and its binding craft, however careful, won't be enough to mask potential repetitiveness if the making loop runs empty. Everything rests on the strength of its plot and its ability to distill its darkness with precision: if the story keeps its promises, the game rises; if it stalls, it falls back into the mass of interchangeable cozy simulators. It's a narrative gamble, and its success depends entirely on the writing, that always-risky ground where the demo already sowed doubt.

![Bookbinding, the cozy workshop where the shadow of a murder surfaces](/images/bookbinding/screenshot-3.webp)

## What we take away

Bookbinding is a cozy game that has the merit of ambition, that of daring darkness where the genre wallows in gentleness. Its binding craft, with its custom covers and its care for detail, offers a soothing surface that will speak to lovers of the book-object, but it's its murky plot, made of murder, secrets to bury and tarot that twists fate, that gives it its real identity. That tension between the reassuring and the unsettling is a fine idea, that of a simulator refusing to be just a comfort blanket and betting on its subtext to leave a mark. On paper, it's the promise of a cozy game unlike the others.

You just have to approach it with caution. The demo wasn't unanimous, with only 67% positive reviews, a signal that invites tempering the enthusiasm and waiting to see whether the plot keeps its promises. Because everything rests on its writing and its delicate balance between gentleness and darkness, that tightrope it walks and on which its success depends. And its setting, second cozy book shop in a week, arrives in a context of saturation that doesn't do it favors. It's an intriguing narrative gamble, to watch closely, whose value will play out on the quality of its story more than its craft.

## Verdict

A cozy binding game hiding a dark plot of murder and tarot under its soothing craft: an intriguing ambition, but a demo that divided and an already saturated setting invite caution.

**Strengths:**

- A careful binding craft for lovers of the book-object
- A dark plot, murder and secrets, under the cozy looks
- The tarot that twists fate, an intriguing narrative mechanic
- The ambition of a cozy game that dares to unsettle

**Weaknesses:**

- A demo that divided, with only 67% positive reviews
- A narrative gamble whose success depends entirely on the writing
- A saturated setting, second cozy book shop in a week
