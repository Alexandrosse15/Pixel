Let us be clear: we launched Ugoku Sushi Bar expecting yet another sweet little cozy game, pretty to look at and empty after an hour. We ended up fishing our own catch, growing our own tea, playing dice at the casino and haggling over black market ingredients. Behind its adorable facade hides a management game far richer than its cute face lets on. The cuteness was just the bait.

![Ugoku Sushi Bar, preparing sushi behind the counter](/images/ugokusushibar/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Ugoku Sushi Bar is developed by Rogue Duck Interactive and launches on PC via Steam. The pitch is irresistibly gentle: you become a chef in a small Japanese conveyor-belt sushi bar, a cozy job simulator full of charm and heart. You prepare sushi on your crafting table, serve a colourful clientele, learn new recipes and build bonds with regulars. On paper, the promise is comfort in pixels. In practice, the game has a lot more under the hood.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is preparation and service. You assemble your sushi by hand on the crafting table, manage the influx of strongly flavoured customers, and juggle orders without letting the mood cool. This core loop is solid and satisfying: the crafting gesture has weight, serving the right plate to the right regular delivers a small joy, and the growth in skill feels natural. With a hundred recipes to learn and forty ingredients to unlock over time, the culinary progression holds up and rewards curiosity.

But what truly sets Ugoku Sushi Bar apart is the unexpected depth of its side activities. The game does not just lock you behind a counter: it sends you fishing, setting crab pots, harvesting tea, all activities that feed your kitchen and break the routine of service. And then there are the mischievous touches that make you smile, the casino dice games, the black market ingredients, the collectible chopstick skins, a whole ecosystem of side activities that gives a texture and personality rare in the cozy genre. It is that generosity, that refusal to limit itself to a single mechanic, that turns a pretty pastime into a real game.

![Ugoku Sushi Bar, fishing and the side activities that feed the kitchen](/images/ugokusushibar/screenshot-2.webp)

Upgrading the kitchen and the relationships with regulars close the loop cleverly. Reinvesting your earnings to improve your equipment, unlock new recipes and watch your loyal clientele return with their quirks, that is the gentle but constant progression that makes you want to start "just one more day". The game respects the player's time: there is no artificial pressure or predatory system, just a permanent invitation to explore a new facet.

Not everything is perfect, though. The difficulty stays very gentle, which is coherent with the cozy spirit but will leave challenge-seekers wanting more. Service can get a bit repetitive if you ignore the side activities, which are precisely what gives the whole thing its zest, and some systems would benefit from better introduction so you do not discover their richness by accident. These are minor reservations against the overall pleasure, but they exist.

## The tech

Visually, Ugoku Sushi Bar is a treat of gentleness. The cute art direction fully owns its register, the sushi is appetising, the characters have character and the little bar's atmosphere radiates warmth. It is precisely the kind of dressing that gives a cozy game its zest, and the studio executes it with care. The whole runs without a hitch and comes with welcome comfort, including cloud saves and remote co-op play to share the counter.

The few technical reservations are minor. You would sometimes like clearer feedback on what certain activities actually earn you, and the cute interface hides the game's depth a touch too well. But nothing that mars the pleasure, and the whole gives off a reassured polish that many lazier cozy productions lack.

![Ugoku Sushi Bar, upgrading the kitchen and the relationship with regulars](/images/ugokusushibar/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Ugoku Sushi Bar pulls off the feat of keeping the promise of its cuteness while hiding, behind it, a genuinely generous management game. The preparation loop is satisfying, the culinary progression holds up, and above all the depth of the side activities, from fishing to the black market, gives it a richness and personality rare in an often lazy genre. It is warm, clever and far more substantial than it looks.

Its limits, a very gentle difficulty and a few too-discreetly-introduced systems, flow directly from its cozy identity and do not weigh heavily against the charm of the whole. If you are looking for a comforting game that does not treat you like a child and truly has things to offer beneath its adorable surface, this one deserves a spot on your counter. We came for the cuteness, we stayed for everything else.

**A cozy game that understood being cute is no excuse to skip depth, and it is all the better for it.**

Score: 8/10
