Let's admit it: we loved the title first. An Eggstremely Hard Game, that silly pun on egg and extreme, made us smile before the first image, and that was almost enough to make us buyers. It belongs to that wave of punishing co-op games that make friends scream, the one Chained Together launched and that a flock of imitators now declines. The real question, the one that worried us, was whether it was just one more pale copy. Good news: its fragile egg gives it just enough of its own madness to exist.

![An Eggstremely Hard Game, the co-op physics game where you carry a fragile egg](/images/egghard/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

An Eggstremely Hard Game is a casual co-op platformer developed by BBear Studio and published by Devotion Interactive, available on PC since 24 July 2026. It offers solo, online co-op and shared-screen co-op, and describes itself as a physics game based on teamwork, clumsiness and the irrepressible power of the honk. The principle: cooperate to carry an egg through unpredictable challenges, juggling a capricious physics. It openly belongs to the lineage of punishing co-op games in the Chained Together vein, that family of titles turning friendship into a battlefield, alongside Stuck Together, PEAK, Climb and Cry and other A Difficult Game About Climbing.

## The gameplay: the egg, that silly and brilliant find

What saves An Eggstremely Hard Game from mere imitation is its central object: the egg. Where Chained Together chains you to your friends to climb an infernal tower, this game hands you a fragile egg to carry together, and that silly idea changes everything. The egg becomes more unstable with each impact, so the slightest fall, the slightest poorly negotiated shock brings you closer to disaster, in a growing tension that spices every crossing. You have to coordinate your movements, anticipate the capricious physics, and accept that collective clumsiness is both the source of laughter and drama. That cumulative-fragility mechanic gives the game its identity, distinct from mere chained climbing.

The rest lies in that very particular alchemy of the punishing co-op. You fail, you start again, you bicker gently, you laugh at your own uselessness, and you replay right away to try to do better. The honk, erected as a mechanic in its own right, adds a layer of jubilant absurdity, a sonic means of expression as useless as it is irresistible that seals the good-natured mood. It's a game designed for the evening with friends, where the pleasure springs as much from wrenched success as from shared chaos, and where the unpredictable physics guarantees each match will produce its batch of hilarious catastrophes. On that ground, the bet is met.

## What sticks

You have to stay lucid about the genre's limits. An Eggstremely Hard Game, like all its cousins, lives and dies by its social dimension: alone, the experience loses most of its flavor, and without partners ready to play the joyful-chaos game, frustration quickly overtakes laughter. It's a title to reserve for evenings with friends, whose interest collapses solo or with disengaged teammates. Its proposition is delightful in the right conditions, but totally dependent on them, and you should know that before diving in hoping to have fun alone in front of your screen.

The other reservation is lifespan and depth. These punishing co-op games rest on a strong but often thin concept, and everything depends on the variety of the challenges and the game's ability to renew its situations before the formula runs out of steam. The real risk is exhausting it once the egg's novelty is digested, and seeing the mechanic, however clever, show its limits over the long haul. It's an experience cut out for intense, memorable sessions rather than long-term investment, to take for what it is: an evening gem, not a marathon game.

## What we take away

An Eggstremely Hard Game confirms a good pun can hide a genuinely good game, and above all that it escapes the pale-copy trap. Its fragile egg, which becomes more unstable with each shock, gives it its own identity amid the Chained Together clones, and turns every crossing into a tense, hilarious coordination exercise. Carried by capricious physics, a honk erected as an absurd mechanic and that inimitable alchemy of the punishing co-op, it's an evening gem that does exactly what you ask of it: make you laugh, scream and replay with friends.

You just have to approach it for what it is. Like all games of its genre, it depends entirely on its social dimension: without partners caught up in the joyful chaos, the experience collapses, and solo holds little interest. And its lifespan rests on the variety of its challenges, with the risk of exhausting it once the egg's madness is tamed. But for an evening with friends ready to sacrifice their friendship on the altar of an over-fragile egg, it holds a delightful promise. The title was a joke, the game is a real success.

## Verdict

A punishing co-op game in the Chained Together lineage, saved from the pale copy by its fragile egg that breaks with each shock: a hilarious evening gem, entirely hinging on its social dimension.

**Strengths:**

- The fragile egg growing more unstable with each impact, a real identity
- The honk erected as an absurd, jubilant mechanic
- The inimitable alchemy of the punishing co-op among friends
- Solo, online co-op and shared screen available

**Weaknesses:**

- An experience that collapses solo or without engaged partners
- A lifespan hinging on the variety of the challenges
- A strong but thin concept, cut out for the evening more than the long term
