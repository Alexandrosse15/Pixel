A lever, a three by three grid, and lines to match. Every completed line summons a unit onto the battlefield on the right of the screen. When luck really kicks in, the screen fills with golden light and a legendary unit drops in.

Put like that, Slotbound sounds like a dopamine trap. It is one, by the way, and it owns it. But behind the slot machine there is an auto battler that asks for real decisions.

![A jackpot: the slot machine lights up and a legendary unit appears](/images/slotbound/screenshot-2.webp)

First, no unit is useless. The weakest are absorbed by the strongest to evolve them, and a plain one-star warrior ends up as something else, depending on the class branches you pick. Then, every summoned unit gets randomly rolled stats, which you have to match with its traits, its items and Cores that flat out change the rules. That is where it gets interesting: you do not just suffer randomness, you slowly build the tools to bend it.

The fun is very immediate. Enemy waves come, the machine spins, synergies chain, and the next run starts before you have decided to stop. The pixel art is neat and the battlefield reads clearly.

![A desert battle, with the slot machine on the left and the army on the right](/images/slotbound/screenshot-3.webp)

The reservations come from the genre and the budget. As in any game built on chance, some runs are lost on the third spin, and no item rescues a machine that refuses to love you. And the solo developer states on Steam that generative AI tools were used for some visual assets and promotional art, then reworked by hand.

A word on the launch: Slotbound was due out today, but Steam has not yet approved the launch build, and the developer is waiting on support. The demo is still available, and it is a good way to see whether the loop grabs you. It grabbed me.
