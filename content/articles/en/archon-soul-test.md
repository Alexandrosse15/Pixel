We launched Archon Soul with gritted teeth, ready to curse the randomness the way you curse any dice roguelike that loses you on a bad roll. Except here, when the die betrayed us, we had no one to blame but ourselves: we were the ones who built it. That is the game's brilliant idea, the one that turns the genre's usual rage into a mechanic's pleasure.

![Archon Soul, building dice from faces and runes](/images/archonsoul/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Archon Soul is developed by Polygryph, an independent studio, and launches on PC via Steam at around fourteen euros. It is a 2D roguelike dice-builder that owns its influences, Slay the Spire for deck construction and Dicey Dungeons for the dice, but adds its own twist: instead of suffering randomness, you forge your own dice. The goal: climb the Archon's palace, run after run, sharpening an ever deadlier combo machine. For an indie at this price, the mechanical ambition is enormous.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is a simple, brilliant find. A die is not a black box that decides your fate: it is an object you assemble face by face. You choose the die body, graft on runes with unique effects, modify what each face triggers. With forty runes, over thirty different bodies and tens of thousands of possible combinations, the promise is not marketing spin: it is a genuine workshop of probabilistic tinkering. Randomness still exists, but you bend its odds until it works for you. Raging at a roll, here, means admitting you built your die badly.

Add a pool of over two hundred spells and you get the second strategic layer: marrying your custom dice with spells that trigger in cascade to blow up combos. The feeling, when a chain you patiently assembled unfolds and pulverises a whole room, is exactly the satisfaction you come to the genre for. Four playable characters, each with their own strategies, radically change the approach and make you want to start over to explore another path. Replayability is not a vague promise, it is wired into the system.

![Archon Soul, a chain of spell combos mid-fight](/images/archonsoul/screenshot-2.webp)

What makes Archon Soul superior to many of its rivals is that permanent tension between consistency and power. The more you stabilise a die to guarantee an effect, the less room you leave for explosive rolls, and vice versa. Every run becomes a trade-off: secure a reliable machine, or bet on a wilder die with devastating potential? It is that question, posed at every upgrade, that hooks you and has you restarting "one more run" well past a reasonable hour.

The game does not entirely escape the genre's pitfalls. The power scaling can create runs where the balance tips brutally, one way or the other, and some rune combinations are visibly stronger than others, which slightly flattens the diversity of viable strategies over the very long term. The early going is also a bit intimidating: the depth of the assembly system takes a few runs to reveal its logic, and an impatient player might bail before the click. But once the click happens, it is hard to put the controller down.

## The tech

Archon Soul bets on a clean, legible 2D art direction, where every rune, face and effect stands out clearly, which is vital in a game where visual information drives decisions. The combos display with enough clarity to follow a complex cascade without getting lost, and the dice-building interface, a potential mess, stays surprisingly manageable once tamed.

On polish, the whole is solid for an indie of this size. Load times are short, the run-death-restart loop is smooth and free of needless friction, which is essential for a roguelike you chain. A few tooltips would benefit from better explaining the sharpest interactions between runes and spells, because the game hides a depth its interface does not always reveal upfront. Nothing serious, but a touch more teaching would keep some players from missing fascinating builds.

![Archon Soul, the dice and rune customisation workshop](/images/archonsoul/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Archon Soul takes the eternal complaint about dice roguelikes, the frustration of luck, and flips it into a selling point. By letting you build your own dice, it turns bad luck into a design error and combat into a workshop of probabilistic engineering. The depth of the system, the richness of the combos and the four characters ensure real replayability, not decorative, all for an unbeatable indie price.

A few flaws remain, imperfect balance over the very long term and a slightly abrupt start that demands patience before the click. But those are trifles against what the game pulls off: making randomness smart, and therefore terribly addictive. If you loved Slay the Spire or Dicey Dungeons, this one deserves a prime spot in your library. Yes, we grumbled at the dice. No, we could not stop.

**The roguelike that makes you responsible for your own bad luck, and that is exactly what makes it great.**

Score: 8/10
