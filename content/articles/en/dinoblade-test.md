Let's be honest for a second: however old we get, there's still a five-year-old deep inside us who, when shown a dinosaur, says yes without thinking. So when that dinosaur is a Spinosaurus wielding a sword twice its size and parrying blows like Sekiro, the question is no longer whether we're sold. We are. Dinoblade was born from a viral meme, and it could have stopped at the joke. Except it had the nerve to turn into a real soulslike, and that's where it gets serious.

![Dinoblade, the Spinosaurus armed with its giant sword in a desert canyon](/images/dinoblade/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Dinoblade is a soulslike action-RPG developed by a small studio and available on PC since 23 July 2026, after a demo that sent the counters through the roof. You play a young Spinosaurus armed with an oversized sword, thrown into a prehistoric world populated by equally warlike dinosaurs. The project started from an absurd image that went viral, and the studio was smart enough to take the joke seriously: more than 500,000 Steam wishlists before launch, proof we're not the only ones who kept our inner child. The promise is simple: dinosaurs, big swords, and screen-shaking boss fights.

## The gameplay: Sekiro among the dinosaurs

What surprises is that Dinoblade doesn't rest on its funny concept. Its combat system is built on parry and posture, in the direct lineage of Sekiro rather than the dodge-and-stamina model of Dark Souls. In practice, you don't flee blows, you read and return them: each successful parry builds toward the counter and rewards aggression. The game pushes you to stay in contact, learn the opponent's chains and break their guard, in a nervous rhythm that demands reading and composure. It's demanding, it's precise, and it's nothing like the parody you might have feared.

The real show is the bosses. Each territory is guarded by an Alpha, and the studio clearly had fun making these encounters as memorable as they are absurd. The T-Rex fight, for instance, opens with the boss using a smaller dinosaur as an improvised club before tossing it aside and producing a car-sized slab of sheet metal with a handle welded on. You laugh, then you die, then you start again, and that's exactly the genre's appeal. Between desert canyons, huge caverns, verdant meadows and fog-soaked jungles, each zone offers its atmosphere and its guardian, and the urge to see the next boss never fades.

![Dinoblade, a boss fight against an Alpha guarding its territory](/images/dinoblade/screenshot-2.webp)

## What sticks

You have to stay lucid, because the concept's charm doesn't erase everything. Dinoblade is the work of a modest studio, and you feel it in the technical polish. The consensus is consistent: the combat lands, the atmosphere works, but the execution has genuine rough edges. You come across stiff animations at times, capricious collisions and a camera that doesn't always love tight spaces, those little flaws that remind you this isn't a big-budget production. Nothing dealbreaking, but enough to snag, at moments, an otherwise very endearing game.

The other limit is scale. A soulslike lives on the richness of its bestiary, the variety of its builds and the depth of its progression, and Dinoblade stays tighter on that ground than the genre's heavyweights. You'd want more options, more systems to dig into once the discovery is over, and that's perhaps where repetition lurks over the long haul. It's a first draft full of ideas and personality, not yet the sprawling monster we'd dream of, and you should approach it for what it is: a slightly raw but sincerely inspired gem.

![Dinoblade, a verdant meadow crossed by the Spinosaurus and its sword](/images/dinoblade/screenshot-3.webp)

## What we take away

Dinoblade is the delightful proof that a joke can birth a genuinely good game when taken seriously. Under its meme concept, it hides a nervous, demanding soulslike, built on a Sekiro-style parry system that really works, and boss fights as spectacular as they are hilarious. You find intact that childish pleasure of the dinosaur that hits, doubled with the adult satisfaction of breaking a T-Rex's guard after ten tries. It's raw, it's improvable, but it's full of soul, and it's hard not to give in when the five-year-old inside is already jumping up and down.

You just have to accept its rough edges and measured scale. The technical polish betrays its budget, and the depth stays below the genre's references, but none of that undermines the essential: Dinoblade is a sincere, generous, endearing pleasure, carried by a mad idea owned all the way. For everyone who grew up without ever really letting go of their love of dinosaurs, it's an appointment you don't miss. You come for the joke, and you stay for the game.

## Verdict

A viral meme turned into a real nervous soulslike, where a giant-sworded Spinosaurus parries like Sekiro and fights delirious bosses: rough around the edges, but sincerely inspired and fiercely endearing.

**Strengths:**

- A Sekiro-style parry and posture system that really works
- Spectacular and hilarious boss fights
- Varied environments and a successful prehistoric atmosphere
- An absurd concept owned and taken seriously

**Weaknesses:**

- Technical polish that betrays the modest budget
- Depth and build variety below the genre's references
- A capricious camera in tight spaces
