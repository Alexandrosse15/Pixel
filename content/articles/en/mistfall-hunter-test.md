There are two types of players facing an extraction game: those who love the adrenaline rush when they hear footsteps in the fog with pockets full of loot, and those that same tension makes sick. Mistfall Hunter does not try to reconcile the two camps, it fully owns its demanding and cruel genre. And in that register, it comes out with honours.

![Mistfall Hunter, dark fantasy exploration in search of relics](/images/mistfallhunter/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Mistfall Hunter is developed by Bellring Games and available on PC, PS5 and Xbox Series, with a day-one presence in the Xbox Game Pass catalogue. It is a dark fantasy extraction ARPG where you set out, solo or in a three-player squad, to loot relics, fight monsters and rival hunters, then extract before it is too late. Sold at 24.99 euros, it made a strong impression during its open beta at Steam Next Fest, gathering close to 430,000 players, a figure that already testified to real craft.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is extraction tension, that cycle where every expedition is a bet between greed and caution. You enter a hostile zone, gather increasingly precious loot, and have to get out alive before the threat closes in, knowing a death can cost everything you gathered. This risk-reward loop is perfectly rendered: the satisfaction of a successful extraction with full pockets is as great as the frustration of a death a few metres from the exit, and it is precisely that emotional gap that hooks fans of the genre.

What sets Mistfall Hunter apart is the marriage of that formula with a polished dark fantasy ARPG identity. Combat against monsters has weight, character progression gives a real sense of growing power between expeditions, and the foggy, threatening atmosphere sets a gripping mood that serves the tension. The threat of rival hunters adds the layer of human danger proper to the genre: crossing another squad means never knowing whether you will avoid each other, rob each other or kill each other, and that uncertainty is the game's zest. In a three-player squad with coordinated friends, the experience reaches its peak.

![Mistfall Hunter, the tension of an extraction with the loot on the line](/images/mistfallhunter/screenshot-2.webp)

But you have to be clear-eyed about what the genre implies. Mistfall Hunter is intrinsically stressful and punishing: losing your hard-earned loot is part of the deal, and that cruelty, which is the zest for some, will be unbearable for others. It is not a flaw of the game, it is its DNA, but it mechanically narrows its audience to those who find pleasure in permanent risk rather than secured progression. Solo players in particular will endure a markedly rougher experience than those playing in a coordinated squad.

The other point of caution is longevity and balance, two nerve centres for a live-service game of this type. The loop is gripping, but its ability to hold up over hundreds of hours will depend on the studio's support, content renewal and the balance between risk and reward over time. At launch, the foundations are solid, but an extraction game lives or dies on its support, and that is a bet we can only fully judge over time. A few balance rough edges are also felt, as often in a genre where the slightest asymmetry is paid for dearly.

## The tech

Technically, Mistfall Hunter shows a solid production coherent with its ambition. The dark fantasy art direction, foggy and oppressive, perfectly serves the sought-after tension, and both combat and exploration benefit from enough legibility for decisions to be made knowingly, which is vital in a game where a mistake costs dearly. The presence on three platforms and in Game Pass from launch testifies to a project aiming wide and giving itself the means.

The technical reservations are those of a live-service launch. We will watch server stability under the load of the first weeks, crucial for a game resting on online cooperation and confrontation, as well as the balance that will need tuning. Nothing fatal at launch, and the technical backbone is there, but a game of this genre is judged as much on its first months of support as on its starting state.

![Mistfall Hunter, the foggy atmosphere and the threat of rival hunters](/images/mistfallhunter/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Mistfall Hunter is a solid, well-born extraction ARPG, carried by perfectly rendered tension, a polished dark fantasy identity and a ruthlessly effective risk-reward loop. For genre fans, especially in a three-player squad with coordinated friends, it offers exactly the adrenaline rushes they seek, with the bonus of easy access through Game Pass. The foundations laid at launch are convincing.

Its limits are inseparable from its genre: it is a stressful and punishing game that will immediately push away those put off by losing their loot, and its future will depend closely on the studio's support and its balance over time. But in its register, that of cruel and thrilling extraction, Mistfall Hunter keeps its promises. To reserve for those who love risking everything to win everything, and who know a death at the exit is part of the game.

**A tense, solid extraction ARPG, thrilling for risk lovers and merciless for the rest.**

Score: 7/10
