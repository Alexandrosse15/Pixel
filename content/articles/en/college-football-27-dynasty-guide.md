Dynasty mode is the beating heart of College Football 27, where you no longer just play games but build a program over entire seasons. It's also where many players drown, between recruiting boards, prestige gauges, roster needs and playbooks to choose. Yet once the systems click, Dynasty becomes a satisfaction machine, that of watching a nameless small school climb to the top thanks to your choices. This guide gathers the essentials to start right, recruit smartly and grow your dynasty. For our full opinion on the game, head to the [College Football 27 review](/tests/ea-sports-college-football-27-test).

## Which team should you pick to start Dynasty?

The team choice shapes your whole dynasty's flavor. Taking an already-established juggernaut, a big school with high prestige, guarantees an easy start: you attract the best prospects from the get-go, you win fast, and you learn the mechanics without pressure. It's comfortable, but the challenge is lesser, and room to grow reduced since you start at the top of the hierarchy.

The most rewarding experience, the one we recommend once you've absorbed the basics, is building your dynasty at a small school. Taking over a modest program and hoisting it season after season to the level of the big dogs is where Dynasty reveals its full depth and delivers the greatest satisfaction. You'll have to work with low prestige that limits your recruiting at first, dig up the talents big schools overlook, and earn your legitimacy game after game. It's slower, harder, but infinitely more memorable than dominating from the start with a giant. Our advice: maybe start with a solid team to learn, then launch your real project at a small school.

## Which coach should you create and why?

Creating your coach is a strategic choice you shouldn't rush. You pick among three profiles, or backstories: the Motivator, the Recruiter and the Tactician. Each orients your bonuses and your way of playing, and the right choice depends on your priority. If your goal is to build a talent pool, which is the crux of Dynasty, the Recruiter profile is the most beneficial: it makes you more efficient at scouting and at convincing the highest-rated prospects to join your program.

The other profiles have their appeal by style, the Motivator to get the best from your players, the Tactician for the pure gameplay side, but for a first dynasty, especially at a small school where every recruit counts, the Recruiter is the safe bet. It offsets your low starting prestige by giving you an edge where you need it most. So think about your project before clicking: this choice will stay with you the whole dynasty.

## How do you recruit effectively?

Recruiting is the heart of Dynasty, and the recruiting board is the tool where you scout, plan and sign tomorrow's players. The golden rule: recruit based on your roster's needs, not just the prospect ranking. Those needs are displayed in the recruiting section at the start of each new week, in the Prospect List tab. Signing the best available player is pointless if he plays a position already overstocked on your team, while a key position stays bare. Analyze your gaps before spending your scouting time.

Team prestige is the other major lever, and you have to understand it to progress. It's the composite value of all your program's grades: the better those grades, the better your prestige, and the more high-rated prospects you attract. It's a virtuous circle, winning and managing well improves your grades, which improves prestige, which eases recruiting, which makes you win more. At a small school, your priority is therefore to raise that prestige patiently, by overperforming with limited means, to gradually unlock access to the best talents. Smart recruiting, guided by needs and fueled by prestige, always beats isolated flashy moves.

## How do you use playbooks well?

Playbooks are an often-underestimated aspect, yet decisive for your play identity. Good news: you're not locked into your own school's playbook. You can use another school's, for example selecting a given team while adopting another's offensive playbook, which lets you align your system with your strengths rather than your logo. It's precious flexibility to best exploit your roster.

To go further, you can create your own playbook and arrange formations and plays to your liking, allowing original combinations you wouldn't see in real football. It's the ultimate tool for tacticians who want a custom play identity, tailored to their running backs and quarterbacks. Whether you borrow an existing playbook or build your own, the point is to choose a system that fits your roster's strengths: a run-heavy playbook with a dominant offensive line, an aerial game with a good quarterback and receivers. Adapting the system to the players, not the other way around, is the key to regular wins.

## College Football 27 FAQ

**Which team should you pick to start Dynasty?**

A big high-prestige school to learn easily, or a small school for a real rebuild project, far more rewarding once you've absorbed the basics.

**Which coach profile should you choose?**

The Recruiter is the most useful for most dynasties, because it makes scouting more efficient and helps convince the best prospects, especially at a small school.

**How do you improve your recruiting?**

Recruit based on your roster's needs displayed each week, and raise your program's prestige, because better prestige attracts better prospects.

**Can you change playbook?**

Yes, you can use another school's playbook or create your own, the ideal being to adapt the system to your roster's strengths.

**Is it better to play a small or a big school?**

A big school is easier, but building a dynasty at a small school offers the greatest depth and the finest satisfaction.
