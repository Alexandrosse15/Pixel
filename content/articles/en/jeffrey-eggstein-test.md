The title has to come first, because there is no way around it. Jeffrey Eggstein points straight at Jeffrey Epstein and the sex trafficking case that bears his name. The game does not hide it: you become an "international egg-trafficker", hounded by the press, the police and assassins.

It is the kind of premise that makes people run, and several players admit they launched it expecting the worst. Most come away with the same conclusion, which one Steam review puts plainly: the game handles its topic with the horror and disgust it deserves, glorifies nothing, and condemns everyone involved.

![A tray of eggs to sort and a flip phone showing a face](/images/jeffreyeggstein/screenshot-1.webp)

## Sort fast, sort right

The game itself is a mouse-driven skill game. Fixed camera, rotating inventory, baskets available for a limited time: you sort eggs into the right basket as fast as possible to satisfy a dangerous clientele. Every mistake costs reputation, and lose too much and you are "forever disposed of". Levels change setting and add constraints, up to a pool table where the balls are eggs.

It is simple, twitchy and surprisingly addictive. An Endless Mode with modifiers unlocks after your first run, with leaderboards. The multiplayer update, released in October, lets you play the entire game as a duo, with dedicated duo leaderboards.

![A pool table covered in eggs under a lamp](/images/jeffreyeggstein/screenshot-3.webp)

## Horror between levels

What surprises is the rest. Between sorting phases, you explore dark corridors through other people's eyes, and a disturbing story slowly takes shape, with links between scenes. Players say it gave them chills. The September update summed things up in its own way, announcing it had "added him to the gameplay now".

![A dark green corridor with a door waiting at the end](/images/jeffreyeggstein/screenshot-2.webp)

It remains a small Early Access game, with a handful of players online and a short running time. But it is a real critical hit on Steam (96% positive out of nearly 400), and it pulls off the risky feat of using a grotesque pun to talk about a very real horror without ever laughing at it.

You will not want to recommend it to everyone. At 4.15 euros, though, fans of experimental horror will find something rare.
