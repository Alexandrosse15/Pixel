I started this game braced for the usual caricature: the beret, the accordion, the baguette under the arm.

There is a bit of all that. But above all there is a mechanic I did not expect in a restaurant game: **hiding the cash**.

![Parisian Bistro Simulator, the bistro floor](/images/parisianbistro/screenshot-1.webp)

## Service first

The foundation is solid and familiar: a bistro to run, hungry customers, a kitchen and a bar.

The game divides the day into distinct moments. You prepare the kitchen for the morning rush and breakfast orders. You work service through interactive kitchen minigames. Then in the evening **you turn the rooftop into a nightlife destination**, with a DJ, cocktails and guests to entertain.

That division is what avoids the genre's fatigue. A restaurant game where every service resembles the last runs out in three hours; here, the morning floor and the midnight rooftop need neither the same actions nor the same staff.

## With eight, which is the good idea

Online co-op runs to **eight players**, which is a lot for this genre.

And it fits the proposition: everybody takes a role, kitchen, bar, floor, and the game becomes a genuine organisational problem rather than an individual race.

Anybody who has worked a service will recognise the sensation: chaos does not arrive when somebody is slow, it arrives when two people do the same job and nobody does the third.

![Parisian Bistro Simulator, the kitchen mid-service](/images/parisianbistro/screenshot-3.webp)

## The part I was not expecting

Behind the service sits full management: finances, inventory, suppliers, staff, expenses and customer satisfaction to balance.

Normal so far. Except the studio adds this, in plain words: you can **hide cash**, secure your earnings in a **personal vault**, and take on **shady side activities**. All while watching your **Financial Vigilance**, before attention you do not want finds you.

In other words, the game offers you fraud, with a gauge measuring your exposure.

That is a far more interesting idea than a simple gag. In most business simulators money only goes one way, upwards. Here there is a moral question and a risk question: what you take out of the till is yours, until the day it is not.

And for a game set in a Parisian bistro, let us say the choice of theme is not without humour.

## What it is worth

The game launched yesterday at 11.99 euros, developed by Quark Studios and co-published with Gamersky Games, and already shows **418 positive reviews out of 446**, roughly 94 per cent.

That is a remarkable score for a game at this price, and it matches what I see: the loop is brisk, the division of the day works, and the management layer is deeper than the packaging suggests.

My reservations belong to the genre. **Solo, the interest drops sharply**: a game built for eight roles plays badly with one. And **repetition looms**, as in every restaurant simulator, once the minigames are mastered.

But for twelve euros, with co-op up to eight and a concealment mechanic that gives you a real reason to play dirty, it is a good deal. We have written [a guide to getting started](/en/articles/parisian-bistro-simulator-guide), particularly on role allocation and financial vigilance.
