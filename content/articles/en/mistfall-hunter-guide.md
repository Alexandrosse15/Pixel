An extraction game does not forgive improvisation: you enter full of hope and often leave empty-handed with your morale in tatters. Mistfall Hunter rewards those who learn to tame their greed and read danger. This beginner guide gives you the reflexes to survive your first expeditions and, above all, to come back with the loot, which is already a victory.

![Mistfall Hunter, dark fantasy exploration and the hunt for loot](/images/mistfallhunter/screenshot-4.webp)

## How does the extraction loop work?

Above all, you need to understand the genre's contract. You enter a hostile zone, solo or in a three-player squad, you gather relics and increasingly precious loot, and you must reach an extraction point to leave with your gains. The subtlety is that a death before extraction can cost you everything you were carrying. Every expedition is therefore a permanent bet between continuing to enrich yourself and securing what you already have.

The beginner's first rule fits in one sentence: a modest extraction beats a glorious death. Your first outings should not aim for the jackpot but for learning: spotting extraction points, understanding the rhythm of threats, and above all getting into the habit of leaving alive. A player who extracts regularly with a small loot will progress far faster than one who keeps dying chasing the score of the century.

## How do you manage your greed?

Greed is enemy number one in an extraction game, and Mistfall Hunter is no exception. The temptation to push a little further for one last piece of loot is exactly what kills most players, a few metres from the exit. Set yourself a goal before entering, a loot value or a type of relic, and as soon as you have reached it, head for extraction without giving in to escalation.

Also learn to assess the risk-reward ratio in real time. A coveted piece of loot guarded by several monsters or located in a zone exposed to rivals is not always worth the risk of losing everything. The discipline of giving up an item when the situation gets too dangerous is a skill in its own right, and it is the one that separates the veterans from the eternally frustrated. Better to leave with eighty percent of what you aimed for than to die chasing the hundred percent.

## How do you survive the monsters and rival hunters?

Two threats await you: the monsters of the setting and other players. Against monsters, the key is not to get overwhelmed. Engage the fights you choose, not the ones forced on you, and avoid drawing several enemies at once into open space. Use the terrain, the chokepoints and the angles to never get surrounded, and always keep an escape route in mind.

Rival hunters are more unpredictable, because they think like you. Crossing another squad means never knowing whether you will avoid each other, rob each other or kill each other. For a beginner, caution comes first: the noise of a fight attracts predators, so do not engage in a loud confrontation if you can avoid it, and beware of high-loot zones that concentrate players. Listening is your best weapon: footsteps, shots and screams betray positions, and a player who hears before being seen always keeps the initiative.

## Should you play solo or in a squad?

Mistfall Hunter is played solo or in a three-player squad, and the choice radically changes the experience. Solo, you are stealthier and master of your decisions, but every encounter is more dangerous and a mistake does not forgive. It is a demanding mode, rewarding for those who love pure tension, but merciless for beginners.

In a coordinated squad, the experience is markedly more accessible and often more fun: you cover each other, share roles, and can revive a fallen ally depending on the situation. To start, playing with friends who communicate is by far the best way to learn the maps and mechanics without suffering the brutality of solo. Save solitary expeditions for later, when you master the extraction points and the rhythm of threats. Voice coordination is worth all the gear in the world.

## Mistfall Hunter FAQ

**What is the principle of extraction?**

You enter a hostile zone, gather loot, and must reach an extraction point to leave with your gains. Dying before extraction can cost you everything you were carrying.

**How do you avoid losing everything every run?**

Set yourself a loot goal before entering, and extract as soon as you reach it. A modest extraction always beats a glorious death a few metres from the exit.

**How do you handle rival hunters?**

Favour stealth, avoid loud fights that attract predators, and listen constantly: footsteps and shots betray positions. Hearing before being seen gives the initiative.

**Is it better to play solo or in a squad?**

To start, the three-player squad with friends who communicate is much more accessible and instructive. Save solo, more demanding and merciless, for when you master the maps.

**What is the first skill to work on?**

The discipline of extraction: knowing when to give up and leave alive. A player who extracts regularly progresses far faster than one who keeps dying chasing the jackpot.
