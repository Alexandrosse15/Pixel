The title says exactly what the game does, which is rare enough to note. You open the castle gate a little, just a little, you let your troops out, and you go and find the enemy instead of waiting for them.

That reversal is what separates Open The Gate from an ordinary tower defence.

![Open The Gate Just A Little, the tower defence and real-time strategy hybrid](/images/openthegate/screenshot-1.webp)

## A hybrid, and a coherent one

Developed by Abubu Dance and published by The Bueno Interactive, the game presents itself as a cross between tower defence and real-time strategy, on a medieval fantasy battlefield.

In practice you do not place turrets and wait for waves. You direct groups of units, capture towers, and push forward to bring down the enemy castle. Progression therefore runs both ways, which solves the main complaint levelled at the genre: immobility.

The economy is that of a survival game rather than a construction one. You collect gold from fallen enemies, and that gold trains new soldiers. In other words, every fight won funds the next, and a bad sequence dries you out. It is simple and it creates real tension.

Positioning is highlighted as decisive: each group is arranged on the bridge, and the layout matters as much as the composition. On this kind of confined terrain that is the right design choice, because it turns a battle into a spatial problem.

## What makes you come back

Four legions are announced, Crusader, Orc, Undead and Dynasty, each with its army, its special skill and a dragon to face at the end. The clever detail is that victory may reward you with the defeated legion's skill, and that you can start a new run choosing your own legion to turn that power on the others.

Around it, a classic but well-filled progression structure: experience earned at the end of a match, new units to recruit, an upgrade tree, and promotions turning units into stronger forms with titles and boosted stats.

The studio sums up its formula as a mix of planning, grouping, marching and a little luck. That last mention is honest, and it should be read as a warning: in a game where units advance on their own once launched, part of the outcome escapes you.

![Open The Gate Just A Little, the legions and tower capturing](/images/openthegate/screenshot-2.webp)

## What remains to be checked

And you, in a tower defence, do you stay behind the walls or go out and find the enemy?

Two points will decide its real worth. The first is balance between the four legions, because a hybrid of this type collapses if one composition dominates all others. The second is how much is left to chance: pleasant when it produces reversals, unbearable when it cancels good preparation. The studio states it plainly, which is correct, but the calibration will be judged controller in hand.

Good point worth noting, the game is available in French, which is not that common for a production of this size.
