In OPERATIVES: Revolve, when the enemy fires, the shot does not hit a health bar. It hits a card, sitting in front of you, and the card cracks, twists, and eventually flies apart.

"The unique part about this game is that it is a deckbuilder that presents your cards physically in the game world," its creator, Ee Chun Giap, told the Malaysian daily The Star. "When the enemy attacks you, they actually hit your cards."

The game was announced for this week. It will not make it: it now launches in January 2027. But the demo is playable right now on Steam, and it is one of the games I am most looking forward to this winter.

![A tank in a warehouse, facing the player's hand split into two columns](/images/operativesrevolve/screenshot-1.webp)

## Cards with hit points

In Slay the Spire and its whole lineage, a card is an action. You play it, it disappears, and your character has a health bar that soaks up the rest. OPERATIVES flips that. Your cards are your defence. Each has its own durability gauge, the Wheel, and can be destroyed in combat.

Fights take place on two fronts, left and right, each held by an operative. At the start of the turn, drawn cards are split between the two sides, and they block attacks coming from their direction. So far, nothing too unusual. The brilliant part comes next: to attack, you have to launch a card at the enemy. It then goes to the discard pile, and the side it was protecting is left more exposed.

Every attack is a sacrifice. Want to hit hard? You open a breach. Want to hold? You keep your cards in hand and deal no damage. The game's whole tension lies in that dilemma, repeated every turn, and I have never felt a trade-off this physical in a card game. You watch your left column shrink to a single cracked card, you see the enemy winding up its next blow on that side, and you genuinely hesitate.

![The deck and a card's sheet, with its chassis and wheel](/images/operativesrevolve/screenshot-2.webp)

## Cards you tinker with

The second system is toolkits. Each card can take two types of module, a Chassis and a Wheel, which change its look and its effects. You can remove and swap them at any time, which lets you reshape a deck mid-run against a specific enemy instead of being at the mercy of your draws. The game promises a wide selection, to combine with more than 200 cards.

The idea that excites me most is the operative pairing. Before each run, you pick two out of five and merge their decks. Each operative has their own cards, and their synergies change depending on the partner. Five operatives means ten possible pairs, and as many ways to play the same character.

The studio showed one of them in detail during October's Steam Next Fest: Mystic, "a captivating performer of Doctrine's divinity", who turns forbidden chemicals into miracles. Her style revolves around deck manipulation. Prophecy digs through the draw pile for a card with a different activation colour, Trick puts cards back on top of the draw pile to set up the next Prophecy, and Chemical Hex adds damage every time an afflicted enemy is hit. With plenty of zero-cost cards, she chains long sequences in a single turn. On paper, it is the classic combo archetype. But placed in a system where every card played weakens your defence, it becomes far riskier, and far more interesting.

## Metal Gear, Se7en and the Cold War

The visuals deserve attention, because they are what strikes you first. OPERATIVES does not look like an indie card game. The cards are real 3D objects, lit and grimy, and fights play out in first person in brutalist settings: warehouses, subway tunnels, industrial corridors. Every attack is staged with real work on camera, weight and anticipation.

Ee Chun Giap comes from animation and visual effects, and it shows. "Attack and defence will feel like it happens in front of you, and not just clicking icons like most card games," Giap told The Magic Rain. The visual references are the Metal Gear Solid series and the film Se7en, for its set design and visual storytelling. Both are there: the military aesthetic and robot spiders of one, the grime and gloom of the other.

The world is an alternate-history 1980s, in the middle of the Cold War. Humanity did not invent technology: it was handed over by the Mechanical Doctrine, an entity that descended among humans at the dawn of history. Operatives are its human avatars, tasked with crushing all resistance. And, without knowing it, they are starting to slip out of its control. The creator calls it "a strange horror".

## What the demo contains, and what is coming

The demo, released in February, offers two full operatives, more than 70 cards, more than 35 artifacts, more than 35 toolkits, and two of the game's three acts. It has evolved a lot through updates: draws are now split automatically between the two sides, you launch a card by dragging it onto the enemy, and the overall pace has been sped up. A combat log lets you play at maximum speed and review what happened afterwards.

The full version promises more than 200 cards, more than 200 items, all five operatives, highly adjustable difficulty and meta progression. Added to that are Service Cartridges, which work like potions but need a compatible card to activate, Mementos, which grant a special starting position if you beat a boss in your previous run, and an Attunement system to unlock features and toolkits between runs.

The game is also built for modest hardware: the studio claims around four hours of battery life on Steam Deck. The project's itch.io page states that no image generation was used.

## Why January

The delay was announced on 13 November, with an apology for the confusion. The developer explains that Steam's build review times had become unpredictable with the flood of end-of-year releases, and that announcing a firm date was too risky. Adding content and pushing back seemed better.

It is a story of persistence, really. Ee Chun Giap left a stable job to make this game, first solo under the Enigma Equation name, then with a small team after securing funding in 2025. The game earned several nominations at the SEA Game Awards and was featured in the Southeast Asian Games Showcase. It was announced for 2025, then for early 2026. January 2027 is a third date. Given the demo, I am happy to wait a little longer.

In the meantime, the demo is free. Launch it, lose an entire column of cards to the first boss, and you will understand why people are talking about it.
