In August, I left Mutti and his family halfway down the road. Early Access stopped at the end of Act 2, ten missions in, and the question the whole campaign asks was left hanging: where do you go when the world you know is burning behind you?

Version 1.0 answers. Act 4 closes the exodus, and I am obviously not saying where it leads. What I can say is that the ending keeps the promise of the opening: the story does not end on a conquest, it ends on a settlement. For an RTS, that is an almost cheeky ending.

![A fortified town on the riverbank, with irrigated fields and mudbrick houses](/images/tfc2-v1/screenshot-2.webp)

## What I wrote in August still holds

I am not going to redo [the Early Access review](/en/articles/tfc2-collapse-of-the-bronze-age-test): food as the resource everything hangs on, the four asymmetric peoples, the pixel art that makes you want to watch a town live, it is all there and it all works the same way.

The loop has not changed in nature. You build, you feed, and every soldier you recruit is one more mouth on a food chain that snaps at the first drought. I still think it is the best idea an RTS has offered me this year. You do not ask when to attack, you ask whether you can afford an army at all.

## The real gift is the editor

Wield Interactive ships the map editor it used to build its own campaign. Not a trimmed-down player version, the same tool: placing units and buildings, triggers, events, dialogue, all scripted in Lua.

To see what that means, remember where this game comes from. The first TFC generated its missions automatically, and that was its big flaw. The studio rebuilt all its tooling to write missions by hand. And now that tooling is yours.

A small Horde mode template is included to get started. That is smart: instead of opening an empty editor and closing it five minutes later, you get a scenario that works, and you break it to understand how it works. That is how people learned to mod Warcraft III, and that is how a community grows around a niche game. A map-making contest runs alongside the launch, too.

![A campaign dialogue: a character speaks from a burning house](/images/tfc2-v1/screenshot-4.webp)

Multiplayer now goes up to six players on dedicated servers, with computer opponents filling the empty seats.

## What has not improved

The interface is still dense. Building windows stack icons you have to learn to read, and the first hour asks for patience. It is not a hidden flaw, it is a genre that owns its complexity, but a chattier tutorial would not have hurt anyone.

And the audience is small. Seventeen players online on launch day as I write, 45 reviews, 84% of them positive. For a game whose long-term appeal now runs largely through an editor and community maps, that is a real question. An editor without creators is an empty workshop.

![An army deployed on a green map, between hills and an oasis](/images/tfc2-v1/screenshot-5.webp)

At 14.60 euros, on Windows, Mac and Linux, with a finished campaign and its creator's tools, it is one of the most generous RTS games of the year for the price. It just needs people.

If you build a map with the editor, show it on the [InsertCoins Discord](https://discord.gg/473FE3dWvw): I would love to see who tries to replay the fall of Ugarit.
