"You think this is 'just pinball'?" Appulse's Steam page asks, and the answer is no. It is pinball where the ball is a small armed ship, and every shot pushes you in the opposite direction.

That recoil is the whole idea. Once the flippers launch the ball, you keep controlling it by firing: a shot downwards to climb, a sideways shot to correct a trajectory, a burst to hover above a swarm of enemies. The studio insists: no luck, just skill.

![A futuristic pinball table, red neon and enemy ships](/images/appulse/screenshot-1.webp)

Around that gesture sits a roguelite structure. You defend the Vektor Dynamics fleet against the Iron Accord, under the codename T1LT-UNIT (the tilt pun is deliberate). You explore a space opera galaxy map, earn skills that power up the ball (more recoil, harder impacts, slowed time for perfect combos), and climb the leaderboard.

![Choosing a shuttle in the middle of a table mid-battle](/images/appulse/screenshot-2.webp)

Le Rado is published by Raw Fury (Kingdom, Sable, Cassette Beasts), which is usually a good sign. The studio has publicly taken a stand against generative AI in a post titled "Our Team's Creative Vision": everything in Appulse is handmade. The game is Steam Deck Verified, and a competition will give away a custom-built Appulse arcade cabinet.

The demo is available, and this is the kind of concept you need to get your hands on to judge. Out on 26 November.
