You put your finger exactly on the sore spot: thank goodness the mechanics hold up, because visually, however much we love vintage, it stings. There is a difference between owned retro aesthetics and raw austerity, and Runerock Arena sits firmly on the wrong side of that line for an eye used to 2026.

Now, if you can take the visual shock, there is a real management game underneath, and a generous one at that.

![Runerock Arena, the turn-based gladiator management game](/images/runerockarena/screenshot-1.webp)

## A stable, not a team

The distinction matters. You are not leading a party of heroes, you are running a gladiator business, with everything trivial and delicious that implies.

You recruit, you equip with weapons, armour and spells, you scout opposing teams before matches, and you fight in turn-based battles. Behind that you collect ticket revenue and win bonuses, and above all you pay salaries. The studio adds, with welcome humour, that you will also need to invent creative explanations for your defeats, the sun in your gladiator's eyes remaining an evergreen classic.

The part that gives the game its flavour is the career. Your fighters improve their skills and attributes, gain fame and victories, then retire. Age is a tracked attribute, and old gladiators are released from fighting. Watching a veteran rise, dominate and then decline is exactly the kind of arc that binds a player to a sports management game, and it is owned here.

On raw material, each gladiator has a name, an age, one of thirty-six classes, attributes for strength, dexterity, mana and health, melee and missile weapon skills, spell skills, miscellaneous abilities like dodge and magic resistance, and crucially morale, because nobody enjoys losing. Thirty-six classes, from the Minotaur to the Sorceress by way of the Stoneman, is a roster depth few games this size offer.

The ultimate goal is the League Championship of Runerock.

## What you should know

The game is by Areena Games, and a note on the store page addresses Finnish players specifically: this is a game by the original creator of the Areena games, Seppo Suorsa. Which is to say we are looking at the revival of a local cult series rather than an opportunistic project, and that also explains the owned old-school feel.

The game launches today with no user reviews, hence the absence of a score here. The real question for a title of this kind will be the depth of the turn-based combat engine: when you watch a lot and click a little, the fights must be legible and your equipment and roster decisions must show on screen.

A game for lovers of spreadsheets with a human face, then. Those players will stop seeing the graphics after ten minutes.
