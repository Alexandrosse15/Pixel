A week ago, [we reported here](/en/articles/fallen-tear-the-ascension-preview) that Fallen Tear was pushing its 1.0 back by seven days, and that the studio cited remaining optimisation problems.

The new date holds: the game launches today. And we now know what the studio did with that week.

![Fallen Tear, the world of Raoah](/images/fallentear/screenshot-1.webp)

## The detail that validates the delay

CMD Studios brought in **PH3 Games**, and specifically Peter Thoman, better known as Durante.

For anybody who does not follow this corner of the industry, that is the name behind a good share of the best-optimised PC ports of Japanese JRPGs of the past decade, and before that behind modding work that became famous for fixing what publishers had declined to fix.

In other words, a studio announcing a delay for optimisation reasons and then going to find exactly the person who specialises in it lends credit to the stated motive.

That is rare, and it deserved noting, because last-minute delays are more often the admission of a problem being papered over than the announcement of a solution that has been found.

## The game

You play **Hira**, a child born with the dark element, fated to stand against ancient gods.

The claimed blend is ambitious: the emotional weight and party building of classic JRPGs, the free exploration of metroidvanias, and a touch of souls-like challenge. Every death teaches, every bond matters, says the studio.

Everything is **hand-drawn**, with full professional voice acting, which remains rare at this level of production. You travel more than ten interconnected regions of Raoah, forests, lost cities and forgotten ruins, all full of hidden pathways.

Combat asks you to parry, dodge, swap weapons and items on the fly, and shift strategy mid-fight to keep bosses guessing.

And there is the **Fated Bonds** system, the game's most concrete homage to JRPGs: you recruit mages, swordsmen, tricksters and healers who fight and explore alongside you, lending abilities in combat and traversal.

![Fallen Tear, a boss fight](/images/fallentear/screenshot-3.webp)

## What it is worth

The game had been in early access since March 2026, at 24.99 euros, and showed **177 positive reviews out of 218** just before release, roughly 81 per cent.

That is a good score for an opening act, and it should be read as such: players were judging the start of a game, not the whole.

The studio also reminds us where the project came from: **3,002 backers** on its crowdfunding campaign, and years of development on a debut game.

My reservations remain the ones I flagged.

**Whether it holds up over time.** More than ten regions and nine bosses is a volume demanding well-tuned progression, and that is exactly where debut games stumble.

**And the balance between the three influences.** Mixing JRPG, metroidvania and souls is an appealing promise; succeeding at all three is another matter, and that is where opinions will split.

What I can say is that the studio did what it announced, on the schedule it set itself, and went looking for outside help on its weak point rather than hiding it. In an industry where the opposite is standard, that is already a good start.
