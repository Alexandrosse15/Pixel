Let us ask the awkward question upfront: do we really need one more hacking simulator? The genre is crowded with clones that have you tapping away on green terminals feeling like Mr. Robot. HackHub lands in this saturated landscape, and for once, it has a real answer to give. Its added value lies not in its rather classic digital-rebel fiction, but in what it builds around the player: a community, modding and real multiplayer.

![HackHub, the terminal and the hacking simulator interface](/images/hackhub/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

HackHub - Ultimate Hacker Simulator is developed by CrazyBear Interactive, backed by Bunny Dev and HotBunny, available on PC via Steam on Windows and Mac, and translated into around thirty languages including French. You play a seasoned hacker who unravels cyber secrets and foils mass surveillance to establish yourself as the ultimate digital rebel. The game has just left Early Access for a 1.0 version, with two major additions that change everything: modding and a real multiplayer system. The reception is solid, with 89 percent positive reviews.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game takes the fundamentals of the hacking sim, and it executes them correctly. You explore systems, defeat protections, progress through a counter-power fiction against mass surveillance, with that satisfaction proper to the genre: feeling a notch smarter than the machine you are infiltrating. The skill growth, the tools you unlock and the sense of increasing power against ever-trickier targets make up a familiar but effective loop, one that fulfils its contract for anyone who has always dreamed of playing hacker from their couch.

But the real answer to the question "what does this one add?" lies elsewhere, in what the 1.0 launch brings. Modding opens the door to a life extended by the community, which will be able to create its own scenarios, tools and challenges, turning a finished solo game into an evolving platform. That is exactly what most hacking sims lack, doomed to be done once the campaign is over. HackHub, on the other hand, bets on its players' creativity to renew itself, and it is an intelligent direction that clearly sets it apart from its rivals.

![HackHub, infiltrating systems and the hacker's progression](/images/hackhub/screenshot-2.webp)

Multiplayer is the other knockout argument, and it changes the very nature of the experience. Where the hacking sim is traditionally a solitary adventure, HackHub offers to live these infiltrations with several players, which opens up possibilities of cooperation and confrontation unheard of in the genre. It is that kind of ambition, sticking to the fantasy of the hacker collective rather than the lone wolf, that gives the game its own identity and a reason to exist beyond the umpteenth clone. The community focus, claimed from the design stage, runs through the whole proposition.

The game's limits lie in its foundations, more classic than its community layer. The digital-rebel fiction stays conventional, and the core hacking gameplay, while competent, does not reinvent the genre's mechanics. A player coming for a revolution in the hacking simulation itself will mostly find a good execution of known recipes. The game's richness will also depend largely on the vitality of its modding community and the health of its multiplayer, two bets on the future more than fixed assets.

## The tech

Technically, HackHub cares for its terminal-and-interface atmosphere, essential to immersion in the hacker fantasy. The legibility of the systems you infiltrate and the tools you handle is correct, an indispensable condition for a game resting entirely on manipulating on-screen information. Availability on Windows and Mac and translation into around thirty languages testify to care for accessibility, coherent with the title's community ambition.

The technical reservations are those of a game that has just integrated major features. Modding and multiplayer, freshly added in 1.0, will surely need tuning and support to reach their full potential, and their robustness at launch will largely determine the game's long-term value. But the whole runs correctly and effectively serves its ambition, without betraying its proposition through fatal technical flaws. The 89 percent positive reviews salute serious work.

![HackHub, the multiplayer and the game's community focus](/images/hackhub/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

HackHub answers genre fatigue not by reinventing hacking, but by building around it what its rivals lack: a community, modding and real multiplayer. Its core gameplay stays classic but competent, and it is its overlay, arrived with the 1.0 launch, that gives it an identity and a potential lifespan well above the average hacking sim. To the question "what does this one add?", the answer is clear: durability and the collective.

Its limits lie in its conventional fiction and a core game that does not shake up the codes, as well as in the dependence of its future richness on the vitality of its community. But in a genre saturated with dead-end clones, HackHub makes the intelligent bet of relying on its players, and that is what makes it recommendable. If you are looking for a hacking sim built to last and to be played with others, this one finally deserves a look.

**A hacking simulator classic at its core, but clever in its strategy: modding and multiplayer finally give it a real reason to exist.**

Score: 7.5/10
