The Sproutlings made a mistake. To make their world even greener, they built a smart terraforming machine. The machine went wrong, the planet is ravaged and overrun by malfunctioning robots, and now everything has to be replanted.

It is an ironic starting point for such a gentle game, and Green Again carries it lightly.

![A Sproutling colony on a hex map, between fields and robots](/images/greenagain/screenshot-1.webp)

You do not control each Sproutling. You direct the community: build wells and farms, collect scrap, capture ancient buildings, and decorate villages with meadows and trees. That last point is not a detail. The happiness of the inhabitants matters as much as the work, and an efficient but ugly colony ends up doing badly.

Crops unlock as you expand, from carrots and leeks to pumpkins and eggplants, and you have to plant the right seeds for the weather and the season. Generated maps are covered in fog, to explore for secret areas, resources and farmland. And bit by bit, barren regions turn green again, cleared of rubble and industrial waste.

![A colony settled by a river, in the snow](/images/greenagain/screenshot-3.webp)

The studio insists: unless you push difficulty to the max, the game stays relaxed. That is true, and it is both its strength and its limit. Robots attack the farms, but rarely hard enough to create real tension on normal difficulty. Anyone wanting a demanding colony game will turn the difficulty up. Everyone else will enjoy planting pumpkins without panicking. A creative mode even lets you build with no limits at all.

The art style is adorable, the map reads clearly, and the game launches today in version 1.0.

![Walker robots attack a farm amid the fields](/images/greenagain/screenshot-4.webp)
