In Spirit Crossing, other players first appear as shadows. Faceless silhouettes, fishing next to you or gliding past above the clouds. If you high-five them, or share an activity with them, they light up and reveal what they really look like.

It is a very simple piece of game design, and I find it wonderful. It solves in one stroke the problem every MMO has with shy people: here, nobody has to talk to anybody. You can stay a shadow all evening.

![A lantern-shaped spirit among players, on a windswept island](/images/spiritcrossing/screenshot-1.webp)

Spry Fox (Triple Town, Cozy Grove) has been working on it for six years, and the care shows. You build a house on a mountain top, help spirits troubled by magical storms, and slowly learn their language. The storms are the best idea: when they hit, everyone takes shelter around shared campfires. You end up ten people listening to someone play a song, without necessarily exchanging a word.

The game happily compares itself to Sky: Children of the Light crossed with Animal Crossing. That is fair, with one difference: Spirit Crossing leans into routine (daily quests, crafting, decorating) more than Sky does.

![A house interior being decorated, in build mode](/images/spiritcrossing/screenshot-4.webp)

## A business model that is finally reasonable

The game costs 29.99 dollars (22.49 euros on Steam with the launch discount), and there are no microtransactions. An optional subscription, the Cozy Club, pays for hosting and moderation: customising your flying companion, accessories, a resource exchange shop. No clothing or decor is locked behind it, and a month is included with purchase so you can try it. For a cozy MMO in 2026, that is almost an anomaly, in the good sense.

The launch is not flawless. Villager story arcs are temporarily capped at level 12 while the studio rewrites them, and Mr. Kit's decor challenges are paused. A live game launching with open work sites, then, but with a roadmap already published through summer 2027. An internet connection is required.

![Two players in front of a bubble creature, under a blue-green sky](/images/spiritcrossing/screenshot-5.webp)

933 people were playing on Steam at the time of writing, a few hours after launch, not counting Switch. Enough that the campfires are never empty.
