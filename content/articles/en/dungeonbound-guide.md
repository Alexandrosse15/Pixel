Dungeonbound throws a lot of information at you from the first run: an equipment grid, a shop, runes, a forge, three game modes. Here is what you need to avoid getting lost, plus a few habits that win you floors. For our take on the game itself, see [our review](/articles/dungeonbound-test).

![The combat screen: equipment on the left, enemy in the centre, shop on the right](/images/dungeonbound/screenshot-1.webp)

## Start with the Long Haul, not the Ascension

The Long Haul is the incremental mode. You play seven heroes (Warrior, Mage, Rogue, Monk, Archer, Alchemist, Paladin), each with their own gold, levels and permanent upgrades. Dying sends you home with your loot: you lose floors, never your progress.

It is the best place to learn how items interact, with no pressure. The Ascension, the roguelike mode, is unforgiving: five hearts, and when the last one goes, the build is destroyed. Only your rank survives.

A tip: do not spread your gold across all seven heroes early on. Invest properly in one or two; one hero's progress is never lost while you play another.

## Placement matters as much as the item

This is the most important rule, and the least obvious. Each attack travels around your board and triggers every item it crosses. Two consequences:

- **What touches what changes what it does.** Many items have adjacency effects. Before buying, check what a piece wants next to it.
- **Board runes empower everything placed on them.** Keep your best pieces on runes, even if it means moving everything else.

Click a type tag (an item category) to highlight every piece of that type on your board. It is the fastest way to check a synergy.

![The equipment board, its runes and the inventory list](/images/dungeonbound/screenshot-3.webp)

## Speed decides everything

Turn order depends on speed. Acting first also means acting more often, and a fast hero gains extra hits against a slow enemy. Early on, a speed item is often worth more than a slightly stronger damage item.

Overkill damage spills onto the next enemy in line: against groups, a single big hit pays off more than it seems.

## Merge, then forge

- **Merging**: two identical items become a stronger one, up to level 10. Do not sell duplicates on reflex.
- **The forge**: any two pieces become a hybrid that inherits both parents' abilities. The forge now offers two variants and shows stats before and after: take the time to compare.
- **The upgrade altar** keeps the piece on it, so you can upgrade it several times in a row.

Upgraded items can take gems, potions included. And remember locking: a locked item will not go out with the bag's "sell all" button.

## Statuses to know

The last update before launch added four statuses, and they change quite a few fights:

- **Blind**: 5% less dodge per stack, up to 50%.
- **Silence**: each stack blocks one timed or Mana item (it then shows a purple badge).
- **Root**: attacks 40% slower, and Charge does nothing.
- **Transmute**: one item becomes a random item of the same shape for 6 seconds.

Blind, Silence and Root each lose one stack per second. Against them, speed and items that do not rely on a timer are your best friends.

## In the Ascension, steal other people's ideas

The rivals in your way are real builds from other players. When one beats you, watch the replay: it is the best school in the game. You can browse the list of builds you have beaten, and build codes let you share or copy a setup.

On each of the first five floors, you pick one relic out of three. Relics bend the rules of the whole run: choose them for your board, not for their rarity. Ranked ascensions promote you at floor 8.

## In co-op

Everyone keeps their own purse, shop and relics, and the team shares five hearts. To give an item to a teammate, drop it onto their board. Some co-op exclusive items reward whoever gives them away: do not hoard them. When an enemy drops loot, everyone picks, and dice settle disputes.

Finally, the Daily gives everyone the same ascension each day, with three modifiers. No reward, just the leaderboard. It is the best place to measure your instincts against everyone else's.
