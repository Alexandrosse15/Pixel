There is a category of work you feel you own a little, because you found it alone and nobody around you talks about it. Steins;Gate is that for a lot of people: the anime marked its era, but the visual novel and the manga stayed niche outside Japan, and recommending one always carries a whiff of letting someone in on a secret.

As long as SERN does not find out, of course.

![STEINS;GATE RE:BOOT, Akihabara rebuilt](/images/steinsgatereboot/screenshot-1.webp)

## What this remake redoes, and it does plenty

Out today, 19 August 2026, developed by MAGES and published by Spike Chunsoft, this version retells the story of Rintaro Okabe and his microwave that can send messages into the past.

The rework is real, and it deserves credit before anything else is argued.

**huke returns to character design.** The original illustrator redraws the cast, with outfits and accessories brought up to date. That kind of detail matters for a work whose visual identity is inseparable from its artist.

**Akihabara has been rebuilt** with documentary care, from authentic period references. For a story set in one specific district at one specific moment, that is not decoration: it is part of the point.

Add a completely reworked interface, reanimated videos, remastered music, redone voice acting and an improved localisation. And above all **an original new scenario** expanding the world, which is the package's strongest argument.

The **Observer** tab also deserves a mention, helping you keep track of the worldlines. Anyone who tried to reach the original's true ending without a guide knows how opaque that was: this is a genuine comfort improvement.

## The awkward question, and it is arithmetic

RE:BOOT is priced at **53.99 euros**.

The original is still on sale on Steam. At **5.39 euros**. And it has not aged into mediocrity: it shows 24,220 positive reviews out of 24,963, an Overwhelmingly Positive reception, one of the best scores on the store.

Ten times the price. That is the point everything else orbits, and no argument about the remake's quality makes it disappear.

The debate that has been running among players since the announcement is more interesting than the game itself, because it raises a real critical question: do you judge a remake on its own terms, standing in the shoes of a newcomer, or against the original it claims to replace?

Both positions hold. Some outlets scored it at the maximum by treating it as a first encounter. Noisy Pixel gave it 6 out of 10 by treating it as a remake. Both are right inside their own frame.

My position is this: a remake exists only in relation to what it remakes. That is its definition. Scoring it without reference to the original means scoring an object stripped of half its nature.

## What the community objects to, and what is verifiable

Several criticisms circulate and are worth separating.

The central one concerns **the script used**. RE:BOOT builds on the STEINS;GATE ELITE text, the previous rework, rather than the original. ELITE was explicitly aimed at an audience coming from the anime, with tightened pacing. Long-time players see an abridged version, and argue that a remake presented as definitive should have started from the complete text.

Note the irony in passing: ELITE was already a remake. This is therefore the remake of a remake.

Other complaints are matters of finish, such as English typography compressed in height or Japanese text left in some illustrations. These are player reports from the opening hours, to be confirmed over time, but they recur often enough to flag.

The commercial context helps explain things. MAGES has been going through a difficult stretch, and Anonymous;Code, the series' previous entry, did not find its audience after a long development. Bringing out the safest asset in the catalogue is a publisher's decision before it is an author's.

## Who it is for

This is where the article becomes useful, because the answer depends entirely on your situation.

**If you have never touched Steins;Gate**, this is the prettiest and most comfortable version. The story remains one of the medium's finest, the reconstruction of Akihabara is superb, and the Observer tab will save you hunting blindly for the true ending. You will have a remarkable time.

**If you already know the work**, the question is what you are paying for. An original new scenario, new art and a technical rework, against fifty-four euros and a shortened script. Defensible if the work matters to you, not compulsory.

**If you were hesitating about starting the series**, the five-euro original remains a perfectly valid front door, longer and less pretty.

One practical point that will settle it for part of the audience: **there is no French translation**. It offers English, Japanese and Chinese in both scripts. For a visual novel, meaning a game that is nothing but text, that is a decisive barrier.

![STEINS;GATE RE:BOOT, the interface and the Observer tab](/images/steinsgatereboot/screenshot-3.webp)

## What sets the score

It rewards a serious rework, huke's return, an Akihabara rebuilt with rare care, an original new scenario that adds something, and navigation help across the worldlines that was badly missing.

It marks down a price ten times that of an original still available and still excellent, the choice of ELITE's shortened script over the complete text for a version presented as the reference, and the missing French version on an entirely text-based work.

The story is worth everything it has always been worth. What is under discussion is the packaging, and its price tag.

So a question, and it goes beyond this game: should a remake be judged alone, or against what it replaces?
