Jurassic Architect's starting idea has a simple elegance. No cloning, no DNA in amber: a Portal. On the other side, real dinosaurs. People from our world buy a ticket, step through, eat an ice cream in front of a triceratops, and you build the place.

![The glowing Portal at the centre of the park, surrounded by visitors](/images/jurassicarchitect/screenshot-4.webp)

The game from Polish studio MadOctopus, out on 24 November, is a park management game in the cozy vein. You draw enclosures of any shape, paint ponds and sand, plant ferns and palms. Each species wants its own ground and space, and its sheet tells you what is missing. Then come paths, food stands, a café, viewing towers, guided tours and a safari through the herds. And everything you build can be moved later, which should be standard in the genre and never is.

A T. rex that is hungry or bored will test its fence. Sometimes it gets out. Visitors flee, guards chase it with a tranquiliser dart. If you prefer a quiet park, escapes can be switched off in the options.

![A dinosaur's sheet, with its needs and mood](/images/jurassicarchitect/screenshot-1.webp)

The breeding side promises a lot: more than 50 species, happy pairs laying eggs, babies trotting behind their parents, and genes unique to each animal. You can breed stronger herds or rare colours, and a few legendary colours even glow at night. Pterosaurs in aviaries, plesiosaurs in lagoons, seasons, rain, storms that cut power to electric fences.

One clever idea to finish: a streamer mode lets Twitch or YouTube chat name the dinosaurs and vote for the next day's event. Several creators have already been playing the game for a few weeks.

The studio states that generative tools were used for some art, audio and text, specifying that the dinosaurs and the whole 3D world were not. The game has passed 30,000 wishlists and will be 15% off for its first two weeks.

![A wide view of the park, its enclosures and lagoons](/images/jurassicarchitect/screenshot-3.webp)
