Stellaris has been around for ten years, and [we told the story here](/articles/stellaris-10-ans-anniversaire-dossier) of how Paradox's game reinvented itself several times. Willpower, out on 23 November, is one of its most ambitious expansions on paper, because it tackles something the game had never really dealt with: ideas.

Until now, your ethics (militarist, xenophile, spiritualist and so on) defined your empire. With Willpower, a fanatic ethic can become an ideology, and an ideology travels. It spreads through your starbases, your hyper relays, dedicated broadcasters, and eventually wins over foreign populations. Those converts form fanatic factions that eat away at rival empires from within.

![A glowing starbase in the middle of a fleet](/images/stellariswillpower/screenshot-1.webp)

## Victory through faith

The new Ambition, Willpower, offers a brand new path to victory: get the galaxy to follow you, then defeat every fallen empire. Convert rather than annex. It is an idea that changes the rhythm of a Stellaris campaign, whose late game often boils down to wars of attrition.

Diplomacy follows: empires can adopt your doctrine, a federation can establish an official creed, and the Galactic Community becomes an ideological battlefield where you denounce rival doctrines. Espionage lets you sow doubt where your ideas have taken root. And if all else fails, a new casus belli lets you impose your ideology by war. As Paradox puts it, it is still persuasion, just with a few more battleships, and that is the best line on the page.

The downside is well thought out: if your momentum falters, your own colonies may radicalise and demand you honour your original values.

![The galaxy map and the ideology panel](/images/stellariswillpower/screenshot-3.webp)

## The rest of the content

Three origins: Golden Generation (an academy that trains your future leaders), Lost Creators (a machine intelligence searching for its creators, tied to an existing precursor) and Grand Mosaic (an extra civic and no forced story path). On top of that: five fanatic civics, over 40 events, eight ideological paragons, buildings, an inquisitor ship set and city set, a new advisor, and relocatable galactic galleries, with one model per ethic.

The expansion is co-developed with Abrakam, the Belgian studio behind Faeria, and costs 24.99 euros. Paradox states on the Steam page that generative tools were used for some visual reference work, and generated voices for an artificial antagonist and an advisor.

![An eclipse over a star system](/images/stellariswillpower/screenshot-4.webp)

Stellaris expansions are judged in use, after a few patches. But the core idea, turning your beliefs into a galactic force, is the most promising the game has had in a long time.
