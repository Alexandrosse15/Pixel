In early November, Arctic Drive passed 100,000 wishlists, and its demo 100,000 players, in the same week. The developers said they were "baffled". Fair enough: it is one of the most anticipated co-op survival games of the year's end, and it launches in Early Access on 25 November, on PC and as a Game Preview on Xbox.

![An old red pickup, bonnet open, on a snowy road](/images/arcticdrive/screenshot-1.webp)

The obvious reference is Pacific Drive: your car is your shelter, your storage and your only way out. But Arctic Drive plays with four, and that changes everything. One drives, another yells directions, a third rides in the back with the cargo. The fourth, according to the Steam page, "was forgotten in the last gas station".

Damage is fully simulated: the chassis bends, parts come loose, and one missed turn on the ice can end the trip. Every object in the world is physical. Jerry cans in the trunk, crates on the back seat, a trailer packed to bursting. But weight raises fuel consumption, and a turn taken too tight scatters the load across the snow. You recycle everything, cardboard, scrap, broken parts, to craft repairs and upgrades.

![A silver camper turned into a base, in a blizzard](/images/arcticdrive/screenshot-4.webp)

Each run generates a new road, with its cabins, gas stations and shelters. Hunger, thirst and body temperature to watch, whiteouts that swallow the road, night that falls all at once. You can hitch a trailer or camper and turn it into a base on wheels. The launch version promises more than 100 kilometres of road in the scenario, new vehicles and new locations. There are even tornadoes.

The Steam page also jokes by writing "Artic" in brackets, for everyone who forgets the c. A game with a sense of humour about its own spelling cannot be all bad.
