There is a line on Substrate's store page worth more than a sales pitch: some hunt, some cooperate, **many go extinct**.

Here is a simulation that owns the fact that most of what you create will not survive.

![Substrate: Emergence, the cell colony](/images/substrate/screenshot-1.webp)

## What the game simulates

You cultivate evolution, guiding your cells to outgrow your rivals, experimenting with mutations to find genetic advantages.

**Every cell has a unique genome** shaping its behaviour and appearance. Cells have functional organelles defined by their genes, and those genes pass down through generations, accumulating tiny mutations until they evolve into new strains.

And the studio stresses a point that matters: **nothing is scripted**. Every cell's behaviour is driven by a miniature neural network and by genetic algorithms.

Resources drive behaviour, interactions create emergent effects you did not predict, and simple organic systems combine into a living simulation.

## What makes it legible

That is the real problem with this kind of game, and the studio's answer is elegant.

**Every mutation is visible.** Cells fluoresce as they consume resources, replicate and compete, and **each colour reflects their genetic code**.

In other words you do not need to open a spreadsheet to know what is happening: the colony's colour tells you. That is exactly what separates a contemplative simulation from an illegible one.

Every gene mutates independently, and any cell can carry any number of any gene. The only limit is natural selection.

![Substrate: Emergence, mutations and colours](/images/substrate/screenshot-3.webp)

## The game around the simulation

Because this is not only a contemplative sandbox.

You intervene with direct cellular controls and engineer the chemistry of the environment. You use **chemistry as a weapon**. You claim zones to control the resources your rivals depend on, and you annihilate them.

And you catalogue your unique strains, documenting their lineages. They become more valuable as they dominate the substrate, creating an economic engine.

That last point seems the cleverest to me: turning a lineage's success into value gives you a reason to become attached to a strain rather than starting over after every failure.

## Who makes this game

The developer says it himself on the store page: he works solo, with **a background in cellular neuroscience** and experience as a musician.

That is rare, and it shows in the vocabulary used: organelles, lineages, natural selection, genetic algorithms. This is not a scientific coat of paint over a strategy game, it is somebody simulating what they know.

## What to know

The game launches today in early access, developed and published by Petri Dish Games, with no price displayed and no reviews as I write. It is single-player.

My reservation belongs to every emergence game: when nothing is scripted, the player needs enough grip for their decisions to count, or the simulation becomes a fascinating screensaver.

The direct cellular controls, the chemistry and the zone control are there for that, and it is the point I would watch in early feedback.

The accessibility options deserve flagging for a solo developer: adjustable text size, colour alternatives, contrast settings, which is particularly relevant in a game where information travels through colour.
