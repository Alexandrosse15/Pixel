There are games designed to bring people together. BOMBANANA! is not one of them. Its stroke of genius, or of sadism, is to have you defuse a bomb as a three, except one of you is blind, another deaf, and the third mute. In other words, the person who sees the bomb cannot say it, the one holding the manual hears nothing, and everyone screams into the void while the timer ticks down. It is a communication nightmare disguised as a monkey game, and the demo has already made 670,000 people cry with laughter.

![BOMBANANA!, cooperative defusal with three disabled monkeys](/images/bombanana/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

BOMBANANA! is developed by Lefto Studio and published by TARK, expected on PC via Steam in August 2026, with a demo already available and already a cult hit. It is an online cooperative bomb defusal game for three players, where you play a team of monkeys tasked with defusing banana bombs before they explode, from a minivan turned mobile demolition workshop. The concept openly draws on the Three Wise Monkeys, the one who sees no evil, the one who hears no evil and the one who speaks no evil, and turns it into the worst cooperative nightmare imaginable. The demo, released in mid-June, immediately exploded to become the most-played demo of Steam Next Fest with more than 670,000 players.

## What we know

The heart of the game is asymmetric communication pushed to the absurd. Each of the three monkeys suffers from a unique sensory handicap: one cannot speak, another cannot hear, and the third has only severely limited vision. Concretely, one player handles the bomb, another holds the manual with the defusal logic, and a third has to serve as the bridge between the two, except that bridge is wobbly by definition, since nobody has all their senses. The result is a deranged communication gymnastics where you have to invent codes, gestures, screams and homemade systems to convey the vital information before everything blows up.

If you know Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes, imagine the same tension, but with team members deliberately crippled in their means of communication. It is that constraint that gives the game all its zest: where a simple cooperative defusal would rest on good discussion, BOMBANANA! sabotages the discussion itself, and turns every match into a festival of misunderstandings, accusations and fits of laughter. The minivan-workshop, the colourful atmosphere and the mischievous tone of the monkeys wrap it all in a party-game dressing perfectly calibrated for evenings with friends, the kind you come out of on bad terms with everyone.

![BOMBANANA!, asymmetric communication and the minivan-workshop](/images/bombanana/screenshot-2.webp)

## What worries us

The first point of caution lies in the very nature of the concept: its depth over time. An idea this strong and hilarious works wonderfully over a few evenings, but a bomb-defusal party game has to renew its bombs, modules and situations to avoid running out of steam once the first shock of laughter fades. The whole question will be whether BOMBANANA! offers enough variety and difficulty progression to hold up beyond the novelty effect, or whether it stays an excellent demo gimmick stretched into a full game.

The second point of caution flows from the genre. Like any cooperative party game, BOMBANANA! lives exclusively on the presence of two complicit, chatty friends, or at least ready to scream together. Solo, it simply does not exist, and with strangers mute of their own accord, the magic fades. The viral success of the demo is reassuring, but it will take an active community and above all motivated groups of friends for the game to deploy its full potential for hilarious chaos. It is a three-player game, mandatorily, and that is both its strength and its constraint.

## Are we waiting for it or not?

BOMBANANA! is one of the most promising and funniest party games announced this year, and its demo hit is no accident. Its idea of having a bomb defused by three monkeys each deprived of a sense turns communication into jubilant torture, and its colourful dressing makes it a trigger for memorable evenings with friends. If you are looking for a game to test the strength of your friendships, here is a ruthlessly effective one.

But we keep in mind its two unknowns: the game's ability to renew its situations to hold up over time, and its total dependence on a trio of motivated friends. Everything will hinge on the richness of the full content, impossible to judge on the demo alone. So we are waiting for the August release, a banana bomb in hand and two friends to cordially insult, to decide. One thing is certain: rarely has a game understood so well that the real chaos comes not from the bomb, but from the people supposed to defuse it together. See you at launch.
