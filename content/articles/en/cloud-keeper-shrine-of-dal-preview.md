The bullet heaven has become such a crowded genre that it now takes a real idea to stand out. Cloud Keeper Shrine of Dal has at least one, and it is a pretty one: instead of dumbly stacking weapons, you recruit and fuse cloud companions to build a fairy-tale army, all in a setting of Korean folklore still rare in games. From a solo developer, this roguelite intrigues even before its release.

![Cloud Keeper Shrine of Dal, the bullet heaven inspired by Korean folklore](/images/cloudkeepershrineofdal/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Cloud Keeper Shrine of Dal is developed by Jetpackgone, a solo independent developer, and will enter Early Access on PC via Steam on 31 August 2026. A demo is already available to get a first impression. You play the Cloud Keeper, a guardian tasked with defending the land and serving the moon goddess, Dal. Facing waves of monsters that threaten the world, you summon magical cloud companions to fight by your side, all while collecting loot, relics and offerings to strengthen your growing army. The pitch cleverly blends the bullet heaven with a strong cultural identity.

## What we know

The game's big idea, the one meant to set it apart from the mass, is its cloud fusion system. You recruit different cloud companions, and you can combine three of the same type to create more powerful versions, unlocking increasingly strong strategies as the run progresses. That fusion mechanic adds a layer of thought and management to the usual bullet-heaven frenzy: you do not just survive, you compose and optimise your cloud army, which promises a welcome depth in a genre sometimes too automatic.

The content announced for Early Access is already substantial for a solo game. We are talking about seven characters with unique abilities, eleven cloud companion types, over fifty relics, eight shrine visitors, more than fifteen enemy types, three biomes and one boss. It is a solid base offering plenty to experiment with combinations and sustain the replayability dear to the genre. The dressing, nourished by Korean folklore and centred on the moon goddess Dal, gives the game an art direction and a soul that immediately set it apart from more generic bullet heavens.

![Cloud Keeper Shrine of Dal, the cloud companion fusion system](/images/cloudkeepershrineofdal/screenshot-2.webp)

## What worries us

The first point of caution is the game's status. Cloud Keeper Shrine of Dal arrives in Early Access, which means content set to grow, but also a game necessarily unfinished at launch. With only one boss announced for this first version, you can wonder whether the replayability promise will hold up over time before new content is added. The Early Access bet, especially for a solo developer, will depend entirely on the regularity and scale of the support.

The second point concerns balance, the genre's nerve centre. A fusion system as central as Cloud Keeper's has to be finely calibrated to prevent certain combinations from dominating everything and flattening the diversity of strategies. It is a delicate exercise, and we will have to see controller in hand whether the cloud fusion keeps its promise of depth or reduces to a race for the optimal combination. For a solo dev, it is a sizeable challenge, even if the displayed ambition is encouraging.

## Are we waiting for it or not?

Cloud Keeper Shrine of Dal has plenty to appeal to bullet-heaven fans looking for a bit of freshness. Its cloud fusion system brings a welcome strategic layer, its launch content is generous for a solo game, and its Korean folklore identity centred on the goddess Dal gives it a charm and personality rare in a saturated genre. The available demo already lets you test the formula and get an impression before committing.

But we keep in mind the unknowns of a solo-made Early Access: the robustness of the balance, the real depth of the fusion over time, and above all the studio's ability to regularly enrich a game that starts with only one boss. Everything will depend on the support and on keeping the promise. So we are watching closely, with the cautious enthusiasm inspired by a good idea in the hands of a solitary creator. If you love the genre, the demo is worth the detour, and so is the 31 August release. See you then for the real verdict.
