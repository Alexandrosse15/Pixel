Let us be honest: in a classic RTS, the part we love least is the chore of logistics. Micro-managing your workers, balancing your gathering, clicking a thousand times to keep your economy going while the real battle waits. Flow Of War makes a refreshing bet: what if we automated all that to keep only the best, the war? The result is a breath of fresh air in a genre often weighed down by its own complexity.

![Flow Of War, the automated-economy wave-defense RTS](/images/flowofwar/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Flow Of War is developed and published by Rongcon Games, available on PC via Steam. It is an original blend of classic RTS and wave defense, whose central idea is simple but clever: your workers automatically gather resources and manage your economy while you focus on strategy. You expand and fortify your kingdom, command elite armies, and survive procedurally generated enemy waves, on maps that are also fully procedural. A demo was already available before launch to test the formula.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is its automation idea, and it genuinely changes the experience. Thanks to an auto-micro mechanic applied to both workers and warriors, Flow Of War relieves you of the tedious management to let you focus on what really matters in an RTS: the overall strategy, positioning, battle decisions. No more frantic clicking to keep your resource gathering going or micro-managing every unit: the game handles the logistics, and you handle the war plan. For anyone who has always found the economic management of RTS games a drag, it is a liberation, and a real freshness.

That philosophy runs through the whole structure of the game, built around base building and defense. You expand your kingdom, fortify it, compose your elite armies, and face ever-denser enemy waves. The combination of RTS and wave defense works well: it gives a clear objective and a continuous rise in tension, while rewarding planning. And since the maps are procedurally generated, every match offers a fresh battlefield, which sustains replayability and forces you to adapt your strategy rather than reciting plans learned by heart.

![Flow Of War, the auto-micro of workers and warriors in the heat of battle](/images/flowofwar/screenshot-2.webp)

You must nonetheless temper the enthusiasm, because automation has a downside. By removing economic management and micro-management, Flow Of War also sacrifices part of the strategic depth that gives great RTS games their zest. Genre purists, the ones who precisely love optimising their economy and micro-managing their units to the pixel, will find the proposition too simplified, almost frustratingly easy on some aspects. It is an owned design choice, but one that narrows the tactical richness in favour of accessibility and pace.

The other reservation concerns variety and depth over time. The formula, refreshing and effective over its first hours, rests largely on the repetition of procedural waves, and we will have to see whether the game offers enough content and variety to hold up beyond the novelty effect. That is the lot of wave-defense-structured games, and Flow Of War will have to count on the richness of its armies, its fortifications and its situations to avoid going in circles. As it stands, it is an excellent, well-executed idea, more than a strategic behemoth.

## The tech

Technically, Flow Of War owns a functional presentation in service of its mechanic. The legibility of the action, crucial in a game where you must read the state of your defenses and the arrival of waves at a glance, is correct, which is essential since the auto-micro delegates the details to leave you the overview. The procedural map generation works well and effectively refreshes the setting every match.

The technical reservations are minor and on the scale of the project. A bit more visual variety and a slightly more careful dressing would have reinforced the immersion, but the whole runs cleanly and effectively serves its central idea. Flow Of War clearly prioritised the smoothness of its formula and the accessibility of its concept, and for this type of game, that is the right trade-off. You sense a studio that clearly identified what makes its idea appealing and put it forward.

![Flow Of War, base building and procedural waves](/images/flowofwar/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Flow Of War is a welcome breath of fresh air in the RTS, carried by a simple but clever idea: automating the economy and micro-management to keep only the pleasure of strategy and battle. Its blend of RTS and wave defense, its procedural maps and its auto-micro make it an accessible, sharp and surprisingly restful game for the genre, ideal for anyone who has always hated the chore of logistics. The execution is clean and the idea hits the mark.

Its limits are the direct downside of its philosophy: by simplifying, it sacrifices part of the strategic depth that will less delight purists, and its wave-defense formula will need variety to hold up over time. But in its register, that of the accessible, refreshing RTS, Flow Of War keeps its promise and stands out through its intelligence. If the idea of waging war without managing the economy has always been your dream, this game is made for you.

**The RTS that automates the chore to keep only the battle: refreshing and clever, if not deep.**

Score: 7.5/10
