Before everyone made auto-battlers, there was Gratuitous Space Battles. In 2009, Cliff Harris (Positech, also behind Democracy and Production Line) released a game where you designed your ships, placed them, gave a few orders, then watched the battle without being able to touch it. The studio now calls it the first auto-battler, and it is hard to argue.

Ridiculous Space Battles is its spiritual successor, rebuilt from scratch. And the title is a statement of intent.

![A battle in progress: laser beams, explosions and a planet in the background](/images/ridiculousspacebattles/screenshot-2.webp)

## What has changed

Placement now happens on a fixed grid, in fixed-size squadrons: one cruiser, or four frigates, or twenty-five fighters. No more drawing formations with the mouse, and that is real progress. Orders are still there (how close to approach, what to target, when to open fire), and combining them is how you win: take down shields, then armour, then the hull. Victory goes to the fleet with the most value in surviving or escaped ships, which pushes you to protect expensive units rather than sacrifice everything.

The ship designer is the real game. Each hull has its slots, each module its power and crew cost, and you spend far more time optimising a cruiser than watching battles. Which, to me, is exactly the right balance.

![The ship designer and the module list](/images/ridiculousspacebattles/screenshot-4.webp)

And then there is the spectacle, pushed to the point of absurdity. Wavy beams, explosions with shockwaves, swarms of laser-firing drones. The Steam page warns that your graphics card "will probably need therapy". It is only half exaggerating.

## The big gap

The game launches in Early Access, and that changes everything. The single-player campaign and online challenges, which were the heart of Gratuitous Space Battles' longevity, are announced for later. Right now, you play battles against preset fleets. It is very enjoyable for an evening, but you soon look for a thread to follow.

![The battle selection screen, four nebulae](/images/ridiculousspacebattles/screenshot-1.webp)

The studio also states that about 5% of the art (some backgrounds and module images) comes from an image generation tool. At 24.49 euros, it is a full-game price for a game that is not finished yet. Fans of the original can dive in, everyone else can wait for the campaign.
