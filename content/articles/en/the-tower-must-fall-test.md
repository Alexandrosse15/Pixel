The title warns you. Your tower is going to fall. Not maybe: definitely.

The Tower Must Fall is a tower defense in the most literal sense. There is one tower, yours, and you build it by stacking modular blocks: core blocks, special blocks that fire flaming arrows or lightning bolts, repair blocks. The enemy comes in waves and pounds on it. When it collapses, you collect materials, weapons and upgrades for the next one.

![A block tower by the water, with a new technology to choose](/images/towermustfall/screenshot-4.webp)

So it is a roguelite, but with a physical feel that is rare in the genre. You watch your tower lean. You know you placed that arrow block too high, that the left side lacks support. And sometimes an absurd construction, a sort of stone mushroom bristling with cannons, holds three more waves than expected. Those moments are the game.

![The block unlock tree, on parchment](/images/towermustfall/screenshot-2.webp)

Ben, the developer, works alone, and this game was Ben's first attempt at going full-time. The final version has five biomes with their bosses, 18 enemy types, 6 core blocks and 20 special blocks, and over 200 upgrades. It is not huge, but it is dense for 6.99 euros, and the game is Steam Deck Verified, where it feels right at home.

My reservation is readability: when the screen fills with enemies and projectiles, it becomes hard to tell which block is taking what, and therefore to understand why the tower fell. In a game whose whole point is learning from your collapses, that is a shame.

![A tower collapsing in a cave, at night](/images/towermustfall/screenshot-3.webp)

But the idea is excellent and well executed. The small pixel art is neat, runs are short, and the urge to restart comes on its own.

Your most improbable tower deserves a screenshot on the [InsertCoins Discord](https://discord.gg/473FE3dWvw), especially if it was standing by a miracle.
