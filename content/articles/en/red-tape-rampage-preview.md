You sensed the trap before even playing it, and you were right: Red Tape Rampage is built for the compulsive optimiser. It is a satirical incremental where you turn paperwork, questionable decisions and corporate nonsense into progress. In other words, a machine of rising numbers dressed as an open-plan office.

And yes, you will be managing teams of misfits. That is in fact the subject.

![Red Tape Rampage, the satirical office incremental](/images/redtaperampage/screenshot-1.webp)

## The mechanic that grates

The base operation belongs to the genre: you hire staff, organise them, raise your document output, and grow the bureaucracy. Standard.

The find lies elsewhere, and it is frankly nasty. When productivity drops, restructuring begins: you trigger mass layoffs, remove large parts of your workforce, and turn those ruthless decisions into new opportunities. The developer even confirmed to a player that firing everyone would become far more important in the later stages.

That is the satire working best in this format: instead of telling you modern management is cynical, the game makes you its enthusiastic accomplice, because it is mechanically profitable. The incremental player optimises without a second thought, and the game turns that impulse against them.

Around it, the structure looks solid on paper. Every promotion stage introduces new internal policies that change how you play, to be combined with unique items to create powerful, strange or completely absurd synergies. A research tree expands at each stage and opens new branches. The studio's motto sums it up: manage, restructure, synergise, get promoted, and the paperwork never ends, which is precisely the point.

## What to know

One detail matters, and the developer deserves credit for volunteering it: he publicly disclosed using artificial intelligence as a coding assistant during development, and plans AI-assisted localisation.

That is transparency we would like to see more often, and it prompts two remarks. On one hand, a coding assistant for an indie studio is now an ordinary working tool, no different from an engine or an editor. On the other, assisted localisation will need checking, because that is typically where this kind of automation produces clumsy text, especially in a game whose humour rests on corporate vocabulary and its stock phrases.

The game is out today, developed by Blunder Studios, with no user reviews yet, which explains the absence of a score. Worth flagging for you: it is not offered in French at launch.

The reservations remain those of any incremental. The curve must hold over time, and the satire must renew itself beyond the initial good joke. But if the idea of firing your entire department to unlock a synergy raises a guilty smile, you already know where your next evenings are going.
