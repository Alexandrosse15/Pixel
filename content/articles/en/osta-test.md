There is a particular, almost childlike joy in sweeping a field with a metal detector and hearing the beep that announces something under the earth. Osta built a whole game around that sensation, and the collector's mania that comes with it. It is a niche sim, slow, contemplative, that will not please everyone, but that hooks its prey with formidable gentleness.

![Osta, hunting for relics with the metal detector](/images/osta/screenshot-1.webp)

## The context

Osta is developed and published by Cup, available on PC via Steam as a single-player game. It is a militaria collecting simulator: you hunt for items through metal detecting, auctions and markets, build your collection and decorate your home to display it. The game invites you to travel to different locations to unearth relics of various conflicts throughout History. Tagged simulation, casual and immersive sim, it owns a calm, meticulous register, the opposite of thrills.

## The gameplay

The heart of the game is the treasure hunter's loop: search, find, acquire, display. Metal detecting is its centrepiece, with that tactile pleasure of raking a plot, interpreting the signals and digging at the promising spot to reveal what slept beneath the surface. Every find carries an almost historical charge, a fragment of a past conflict you clean, identify and catalogue. This heritage dimension gives the game a particular flavour: you do not collect abstract icons, you exhume pieces of memory, and that changes everything in the attachment you feel.

Around detecting, Osta deploys the other acquisition channels that flesh out the loop. Auctions and markets add a layer of management and flair: appraising an item, negotiating, knowing when to bid and when to walk away, that is the more economic side of the collector's craft. And decorating your home to arrange your collection closes the cycle with a creative satisfaction, that of seeing your display take shape and tell, piece by piece, the story you reconstructed yourself. Travelling to new locations refreshes the finds and keeps curiosity alive, an essential engine for a game that runs on discovery.

![Osta, decorating the home to display the collection](/images/osta/screenshot-2.webp)

You have to be clear-eyed about the nature of the game: Osta is a niche title, and its slow pace is a stance, not a flaw, but it determines its audience. Those looking for action, pressing objectives or tension will find nothing here, because the game bets entirely on the meditative pleasure of the dig and the gentle obsession of the collector. It is an experience to savour in calm sessions, almost like a hobby, and its appeal depends directly on your appetite for this kind of contemplative loop.

The game's limits stem from that same identity. Repetition is inherent to the formula, and without the spur of a collection to complete, some players will tire of the detecting mechanic once its novelty fades. The depth of the auction and appraisal systems could also have been pushed further to enrich the strategic side. Osta does well what it promises, but it does so in a deliberately narrow lane.

## The tech

Technically, Osta cares for atmosphere more than a show of force. The dig environments invite you to wander, the act of detecting is rendered with enough finesse to be satisfying, and the collection management interface stays clear, which is essential in a game built on cataloguing. The home decoration offers enough legibility and flexibility to make you want to fine-tune your display.

The technical reservations are minor and coherent with the project's scale. You would sometimes like more variety in the environments and a slightly richer staging of rare finds to amplify the thrill of discovery. But the whole runs without a hitch and serves its contemplative ambition well, never betraying its calm tone through a glaring flaw.

![Osta, appraising and cataloguing the found items](/images/osta/screenshot-3.webp)

## The verdict

Osta is a niche success, reserved for fans of calm simulations and owned collecting mania. Its treasure hunter's loop, carried by the tactile pleasure of detecting and the almost historical charge of its finds, delivers a gentle, particular satisfaction, extended by the auctions and the arrangement of your collection. It is meditative, meticulous and nicely themed around heritage.

Its slow pace and inherent repetition make it a game that sorts its audience without hedging: it will enchant the treasure hunters and bore those looking for action. As long as the idea of digging up History one relic at a time speaks to you, Osta offers a gripping and soothing pastime that fulfils its contract exactly. A specialist's game, unashamed, and that is just fine.

**A contemplative collecting simulator that turns the pleasure of the dig into a little niche treasure.**

Score: 7/10
