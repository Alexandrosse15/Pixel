Football management games have a problem nobody likes saying out loud: the genre's benchmark is so entrenched that every competitor has to explain why it exists.

We Are Football 2027 has an answer, and it comes down to one word: **configurable**.

![We Are Football 2027, the club overview](/images/wearefootball2027/screenshot-1.webp)

## What that means in practice

The game advertises **over two hundred playable countries**, expanded leagues and a wide range of international competitions.

But the argument is not volume, it is adjustment. You decide how complex, how fast and how wild your career becomes, **right down to the number of simulated league tiers in each nation**.

I like that idea, because it resolves the genre's permanent tension: players want a credible world, and a credible world is heavy to simulate. Here you arbitrate yourself, country by country.

**Creative Mode** takes the logic all the way: rewrite rules, rebuild systems, and unleash direct actions such as jumping to any club in the world with a single click.

In other words, this is a management game that accepts some players want to manage and others want to tinker with a universe.

## The announced additions

Three points deserve noting.

**Regional cups**, adding a layer of progression from the bottom: you start in your local district, fight your way to regional glory, and earn your ticket to the national stage. For anybody who loves the long climb from the base of the pyramid, that is exactly the missing content.

**Multi-stage negotiations**, where a rival club can swoop in at any moment. That is what gives a transfer weight, and it is often what competitors lack: a negotiation that can be taken from you is a negotiation that exists.

**And more tactical legibility**, with clearer effects and visible attribute requirements during match scenes.

![We Are Football 2027, a match](/images/wearefootball2027/screenshot-3.webp)

## What to know

The game launches today, developed and published by Winning Streak Games, with no price displayed and no reviews as I write.

It also offers a spectator mode, to watch your favourite club rise or fall without touching it, and the studio highlights support for community expansions, which is the real lifespan of a game like this.

My main reservation belongs to the whole series: the comparison is cruel. Against a competitor with twenty years of database and habit behind it, you need a strong reason to switch.

The reason exists, and it is configurability. If you have always wanted a management game that lets you decide the shape of your football universe rather than endure it, this is probably the only one on the market offering it this openly.
